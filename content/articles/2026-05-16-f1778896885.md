Who is the person “that’s running Taiwan?

The remarks were made aboard Air Force One while returning to Washington following his high-profile summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping in Beijing. When questioned by journalists about the future of a pending $14 billion U.S. arms package for Taiwan, Trump stated:

"I have to speak to the person that right now is, you know, you know who he is, that's running Taiwan."*

It's a textbook example of his pragmatic, disruptive style—keeping both sides of the Taiwan Strait guessing while positioning himself as the ultimate decision-maker. Trump’s choice of words offers several insights into his characteristically transactional and highly deliberate approach to foreign policy:

1. A "Business-First" View of Governance: Referring to a democratically elected head of state as "the person running" a place is classic Trump shorthand. Rather than focusing on formal diplomatic protocols or institutional titles, he views global politics through the lens of leadership dynamics essentially treating President Lai as the "CEO" or counterpart with whom a deal must be negotiated.

2. By omitting Lai Ching-te's actual name and title, Trump walks a careful diplomatic tightrope. Beijing fiercely opposes any language that grants sovereign legitimacy to Taiwan's leadership. Using a casual description allowed Trump to signal a willingness to communicate with Taipei without explicitly using official titles that would immediately trigger a massive diplomatic blowout with Beijing right after his summit with Xi.

3. Setting Up the Deal:** By explicitly tying the massive weapons sale to a future conversation with "the person running Taiwan," Trump is signaling that U.S. security assistance is not an automated guarantee. He is framing it as an open negotiation, reminding both Beijing and Taipei that he intends to deal directly with the key players on his own terms.
