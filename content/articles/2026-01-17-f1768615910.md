2026.1.17

"Destiny"

Having dinner with my doctoral classmates and senior peers, I was once again reminded of how extraordinary it is that a single, pivotal examination can turn strangers into lifelong connections. We had all been walking very different paths in life. Through indirect introductions, through friends of friends, through exam rankings, we suddenly found ourselves aligned on the same frequency. This, to me, is what destiny truly feels like.

Looking back, my learning journey was never one of calm focus or uninterrupted study. In junior high school, I was transferred between three different classes due to ability-based streaming. In high school, I repeated a year and spent four years there—if the reason had only been part-time work after school, that might have been understandable, but I also lost a significant amount of time to extracurricular activities. During university, academics were hardly my priority at all; my four years were shaped instead by work, travel abroad, and a relationship that has continued to this day. Many potential connections with classmates gradually faded, and aside from the bond that eventually became marriage, most of those relationships proved fleeting. As for work life, I will not dwell on it—thousands of business cards came and went, yet very few names or faces left any lasting impression.

Everything changed after I entered my master’s program. Classmates became close friends and true intellectual companions. Perhaps I had simply lived enough to meet the right people at the right time, or perhaps I had finally found my own community. Studying while working part-time felt joyful and meaningful. Learning was no longer about exchanging effort for a degree or competing with others; it became a process of self-recognition and of weaving stories between people. The department was merely a platform that brought together those with shared destiny, allowing us to move collectively toward a more fulfilling vision of life. It was, and still is, deeply beautiful.

Twenty-five years later, I feel profoundly grateful to have been given another chance to return to such a warm and inspiring learning environment. I will cherish this opportunity and work hard to keep pace with my seniors, while also growing together with my peers in a spirit of openness and shared progress.

In the end, I have come to realize that learning is never only about knowledge or credentials, but about timing, connection, and becoming ready to meet the right people. What once felt fragmented now feels quietly aligned. At this stage of life, I study not to prove myself, but to understand more deeply, to walk further with others, and to honor the rare gift of learning together—something I now know is neither accidental nor taken for granted.
