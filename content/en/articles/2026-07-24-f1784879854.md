2026.7.24

In a few more months I will enter this "golden decade" range. For someone about to turn 55, having a job at a school is something I could not be more grateful for. By taking in different students every year, although I teach roughly the same perspectives and information, the generational differences that change every year and the course design already make my career full of challenges.

CommonWealth Magazine ran a small feature saying that Taiwan's labour force participation rate is currently only 53.3%, vastly different from advanced countries. From Wen-Yue's analysis and the teacher shortage in the education sector, I feel that redesigning positions at schools of all levels may be a solution. But likewise, from the management mentality of the education-sector principals-in-waiting that I have come into contact with, I can probably predict that any reform or improvement will become impossible because of them.

So for me, this decade is a golden period of self-learning and growth: turning every activity and event I take part in into learning and joy, savouring the life lessons I face in the moment, practising my own distinctive professional thinking and precise insight, not criticising what is around me but adding encouragement, and living life together with those beside me. More importantly, taking physical health and a clear mind as the only precondition, so that I can lead this slowly ageing body to experience a happy day.

If there is no tomorrow, then every moment of today is most precious. Do what I love, enjoy time with family, treat colleagues and students kindly, and reasonably fulfil my responsibilities, and then before going to sleep today I will be completely content.
