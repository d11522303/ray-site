<figure class="ph">Photo</figure><p>Here are my tips for getting through. Among many competitors, our students have reached the third round, the interview. (Happy!) At this stage the most important thing is to give yourself a pat on the back: out of so many candidates, you have finally made it to the last round, the one about personal rapport.</p><p><br/>THSR internship third-round interview<br/><b>1. Announcement script</b><br/>There will be a sheet on the table with a few short sentences in Chinese and English. Read them out confidently in your own voice; this is the most basic part of the process. You may be asked to read in Taiwanese (Hokkien), which takes practice, so find a few sample sentences to rehearse.<br/><b>2. Prepare a self-introduction in Chinese and English</b> (I hear it may not be asked, but I recommend preparing it anyway)<br/>Prepare about 2 minutes of content in advance. In Chinese, start with your education and experience; opening with the most sincere and familiar self-introduction is the best way to get into a flow state, and your nervousness will vanish at once. The English introduction works the same way. If your English isn't strong, you can use ChatGPT as a little helper: ask it to turn your Chinese introduction into a 1-2 minute English version that matches your actual ability, then work hard to memorise it and read it aloud ten times. Ask a teacher or a classmate with good English to correct your pronunciation, or record yourself and listen to your pronunciation and intonation. These are all useful tools and methods for completing your English self-introduction.<br/><b>3. Questions and answers</b><br/>THSR is a service industry and interns are students, so the interviewers will mostly start from that angle. Treat it like a chat, but answer on topic.<br/>(1) Have you worked in the service industry? Was there a memorable customer service experience, and how did you handle it?<br/>(2) What do you think are your strengths? How have you performed at school?<br/>(3) What do you think a summer intern can learn?</p><p>-----------I am a divider--------------<br/><br/>Let me digress here and talk about my own interview experience at TransAsia Airways ground services. I remember it was close to the end of my military service, and I used the interview notice to ask my unit for leave. I get nervous easily, so of course I slept badly and had an upset stomach, and I don't have any relaxed, happy experience to share. I just want to tell every student who is about to have an interview: as long as you care, it will feel uncomfortable, you will worry about gain and loss, and you may even want to give up and not show up.</p><p>TransAsia Airways was in the Chung Shing Security headquarters building at Taipei Main Station. I set off by train directly from Longgang in Taoyuan for the interview. There weren't many people that day, probably because the earlier written exam had already filtered out quite a few, and more likely because back then TransAsia Airways was a small domestic airline and the service industry wasn't fashionable, so not many people applied.</p><p>The process that day was as follows:<br/>1. Self-introduction in Chinese and English:<br/>About 1-3 minutes. The interviewers would first ask standard questions to lead you into your Chinese and English self-introduction.<br/>2. Live Q&A:<br/>As I recall, the questions back then were:<br/>(1) You're a man, so why are you applying for a ground service counter at Chiang Kai-shek Airport?<br/>(2) If opening the counter on site means arriving at three in the morning, do you have transport?<br/>(3) Can you describe what you imagine the counter job involves?<br/>(4) If you aren't hired for this job, how will you plan your next step?<br/>(5) From the tests you did earlier, we know your aptitude. Now can you tell us what traits you think make you suited to this job?</p><p>A few days later I received an offer, but I didn't report for work, because their questions also made me think about my own career plan, and I gave up the chance to work ground services for TransAsia Airways at Chiang Kai-shek Airport. Later I changed jobs often, and gradually found that an interview is also a good chance to get to know the company: see what kind of people are interviewing you and what the company atmosphere is like. You will slowly discover your own choices and preferences.</p><p>I wish my students the best of results through their hard work. Opportunity is left for those who are prepared.<br/><br/></p>
