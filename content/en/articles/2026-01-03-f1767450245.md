Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP29

#70DaysToTheTourLeaderExam

4 To control trading between Han Chinese and indigenous peoples, the Dutch East India Company put the trading rights of indigenous villages under its rule out to public tender, and the winning merchant could monopolise trade with the village. Which of the following systems does this refer to? ① Fan da zu (indigenous land rent) ② Pacht system (Puo-sia system) ③ Jieshou system ④ Wang tian system (King's fields)

Explanation:

The correct answer to this question is ②, the Pacht system. This system was a trade system designed by the Dutch East India Company (VOC) during its colonial rule of Taiwan to manage the indigenous peoples effectively and obtain commercial profit. "Pacht" in the Dutch name means "lease" or "contract". In terms of business operation, the Dutch put the trade monopoly of particular indigenous villages up as the subject of regular public auctions. The winners were usually wealthy Han Chinese merchants, called "Pacht merchants".

After paying the bid price to the Dutch government, a Pacht merchant could monopolise trade in that village (for example, buying deerskins and selling daily necessities). Although this reduced the government's administrative costs, it often led to Pacht merchants exploiting the indigenous people and caused tension between ethnic groups.

#Fan da zu was the rent that, during the Qing period, Han Chinese paid every year to indigenous people (the landowners) for farming their land.

#The Jieshou system was mainly in the Lanyang Plain during the Qing period, in which "head jieshou" led "small jieshou" to clear land collectively and jointly resist risks.

#The Wang tian system was the Dutch claim that the land of Taiwan belonged to the Dutch king, and that farmers who cultivated it had to pay rent and taxes and provide oxen and farm tools.
