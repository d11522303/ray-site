One Blade of Grass, One Drop of Dew (Taiwanese proverb: every living thing has its own share of nourishment)

A few days ago I returned an item at a nearby home goods store. Because I had paid with my Apple Watch, the clerk had to write the refund method on the return slip. The young clerk stopped mid-writing and turned to others for help, because he, who looked about 18, could not spell the word APPLE.

Newborns in Taiwan this year, 2023, may be fewer than 130,000, lower than last year. Given how unfriendly the school system, the social atmosphere and the overall environment are, what reason do we have to ask young people to have children?

If a country in the future has only 130,000 new school entrants a year, I think what we should discuss is not the falling birth rate "tsunami" of private schools unable to recruit students, but, more importantly, how to educate and cultivate those 130,000 babies born each year. The old exam-based competitive, tiered system existed because the population was large and there were too few places for too many people. Now that only 130,000 precious children remain, every young person is part of the nation's strength. Finding each individual's potential, rethinking how they learn, and strengthening their abilities so they become professionals or capable talents is a task for everyone in a society that wants to progress.

#楊陽英文 (Yang Yang English) noted in a shared post: the ranking lists do not show that every year possibly tens of thousands of high school students give up on learning English. This suggests that school English teaching currently leaves too many students behind and makes them give up, and teachers should reflect on this. Exempting these tens of thousands of students who have lost interest from the required English courses at every school, and appropriately directing them into other training or learning with development and potential, might be the solution. In the longer term, we must plan how to integrate bilingual ability into teaching so that students are not afraid of English, and stop defining student outcomes by scores. That is the direction educators should think about.

On another note, private schools that cannot find students should simply close. Having the Ministry of Education take charge of placing the school property instead misallocates professional staff and wastes national education resources. When a school cannot recruit students, its campus is just a piece of empty land.
