2025.0512

Sunrise and sunset in Haian Village

I have always felt that I would be someone who lives in the countryside. My childhood memories and the subjects I loved at school all pointed to that way of life. Even when I passed the entrance exam for the Affiliated Senior High School of National Taiwan Normal University after junior high, I hoped to get into a forestry or animal science department one day. But for some reason I missed out when choosing my track, and I let the exam slip too. Among so many choices, one involuntarily ends up somewhere one could hardly have imagined. Looking back, it was really the result of not examining myself properly and weighing things carefully at the moment of choice.

Of course, in a rural community you will never run into a goddess-like figure such as Mao Inoue. It is because of her that the film looks so beautiful, so don't be swept away by the romance and think real life is like that. The film is lovely, but in reality I am gradually learning to tell the distance between fact and acting. Still, some of its scenes and lines give me something more to think about.

For example, the "taro stew gathering" scene goes a bit far, but it expresses the gaps between city and country, lead and supporting roles, and reality and expectation. By using food and sharing to draw people together, it leads into communication and confession between people. Expectations should not be imagined or inferred, but spoken from the heart, facing the relationship between yourself and others with honesty.

Finally, I still want to remind myself that we only come around once. The most important thing is to choose the environment and the people we like to be with. And of course, savouring the air, food and sunlight of that environment with all five senses is the most important mission of being human.
