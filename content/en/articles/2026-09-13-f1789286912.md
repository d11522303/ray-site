13/687 Earl Grey

When I went with the students to buy drinks in Vancouver, the two of them said there was one favourite tea we had to order: London Fog. After ordering, I found it was a blended tea of Earl Grey plus fresh milk, and the taste was really good. London Fog originated in Vancouver in the 1990s! It is said that Mary Loria, who was pregnant at the time, often visited the Buckwheat Cafe in Vancouver. Because she couldn't drink coffee, she asked the shop to make Earl Grey with fresh milk and maple syrup to her taste. Later she asked other cafes to customise the drink too and recommended it to others. It became unexpectedly popular, and London Fog can now be found in many drink shops in Vancouver. It also became a memory point between me and the students on this Canada trip.

The name Earl Grey is also interesting. Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey, was British Prime Minister in the 1830s. Because he especially liked this flavoured tea with the fragrance of bergamot, the tea was named Earl Grey after his surname. Everyone likes it now, and it is the most commonly seen tea.

Before looking it up, I had no idea that British titles of nobility can be divided into five ranks from highest to lowest: Duke ➔ Marquess ➔ Earl ➔ Viscount ➔ Baron. Of these, the word Duke is the best known, since it was used as the name of a popular "national car", so most people recognise it, and the others are less common. Keep them in mind for fun while you drink your tea!
