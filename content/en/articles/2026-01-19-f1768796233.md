Before the Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exams, EP45

#TourLeaderExamCountdown54Days

6. After World War II, people's livelihoods were ruined and officials were corrupt. Together with the Communist Party's agitation, this led to popular protests, which ultimately led to clashes between officials and civilians, more than seven years of internal strife, massacres of civilians by government troops, and martial law. On which island in present-day South Korea did this happen?

(A) Geoje Island (B) Jeju Island (C) Jado (Purple Island) (D) Yeonpyeong Island

The correct answer is (B) Jeju Island.

If your impression of Jeju is only #WhenTheCamelliaBlooms (When Life Gives You Tangerines) and #IU, then you are like me! It was because of working through this question that I learned of the Jeju 4.3 incident, one of the most painful episodes in Korean history, and I realised the deepest reason for the local residents' distrust of the government and society that I saw in the Korean film Hard Hit (Cop and Robbers) not long ago.

Below is a summary of the response I got after asking AI:

After the end of World War II (1945), the Korean Peninsula was divided at the 38th parallel and placed under US and Soviet trusteeship respectively. South Korean society faced severe economic chaos, food shortages and official corruption, and the public was extremely dissatisfied with the situation. The spark of conflict came at the 1947 March 1st commemoration, when a mounted police officer injured a child and did not apologise, which triggered a protest by the crowd. The police then opened fire, causing civilian casualties.

On April 3, 1948, the leftist forces on Jeju Island, the #SouthKoreanWorkersParty, launched an armed uprising in opposition to South Korea holding a separate general election (fearing it would cause a permanent division of the peninsula). To suppress it, the Syngman Rhee government sent troops and police and right-wing youth groups (such as the Northwest Youth League) to Jeju Island to carry out forceful sweeps. In the process, they adopted a "scorched earth policy", and large numbers of innocent civilians were killed as communists ("reds"). The 4.3 incident left tens of thousands dead (about one tenth of Jeju's population at the time). Afterwards the South Korean government long treated the event as taboo, imposing martial law and political repression, and only began investigating the truth and rehabilitating victims after the 1990s.

If you are interested in Korea's military dictatorship and democratisation movement (1960s to 1980s), which profoundly record Korea's democratic transition, compare it with Taiwan's own democratisation. What the Korean government did then is worth those of us in Taiwan thinking about. Here are a few films, but please digest the emotional turns after watching them yourself.

*The Man Standing Next* (2020): about the 1979 assassination of President Park Chung-hee, exploring the infighting at the core of power and the end of a dictatorship.

*12.12: The Day* (2023): the December 12 coup, describing how Chun Doo-hwan seized power, and the life-and-death struggle within the army between those defending the constitution and the coup forces.

*A Taxi Driver* (2017): the Gwangju Democratization Movement. It shows, from an ordinary citizen's perspective, the bloody suppression of civilians by government troops and the importance of press freedom.

*1987: When the Day Comes* (2017): the June Democratic Struggle of 1987, a nationwide uprising sparked by a university student tortured to death, which finally forced the military government to accept democratic reform.

#1 A tour group is scheduled to visit Gwangju Metropolitan City, Korea's "City of Culture and Arts". On the tour, the tour leader mentions that the city experienced a serious clash between officials and civilians in 1980, and that many films and TV works are set against it. Which of the following statements about Gwangju is INCORRECT?

(A) The core landmarks where citizens gathered at the time were the "May 18 Democracy Square" and the South Jeolla Provincial Office.

(B) In the film A Taxi Driver, adapted from the event, the lead was played by the famous actor Song Kang-ho.

(C) Gwangju now has the "May 18 National Cemetery", which has become an important educational site for foreign visitors to learn about modern Korean history.

(D) The Gwangju Uprising was triggered by the Chun Doo-hwan government announcing the lifting of nationwide martial law, setting off celebrations among students.

Answer: (D) It was because Chun Doo-hwan extended martial law, banned all political activity and closed universities that students began protesting.

#2 On a tour of central Seoul, guides often combine modern architecture with historical stories. Which of the following sites both witnessed the June Democratic Struggle of 1987 and is a campus often seen in well-known Korean dramas and films, such as the historic location depicted in 1987: When the Day Comes? (A) Gyeongbokgung Palace (B) Yonsei University (C) Lotte World Tower (D) Bukchon Hanok Village

Answer: (B)

#3 In recent years the Korean government has opened to the public a site that has long symbolised the highest organ of power, one that witnessed decades of regime change from military dictatorship to democratisation. What is the site? (A) Seoul Tower (B) National Assembly Building (C) Blue House (Cheong Wa Dae) (D) Seodaemun Prison

Answer: (C) The Blue House was opened to visitors in 2022, but it is due to close again after the current president takes office and, after renovation, return to being the presidential office.
