Bangkok Trip Day 4: Drifting down the Chao Phraya (Mae Nam)

The Chao Phraya River is 1,352 kilometres long, with a basin of 150,000 square kilometres, about one third of Thailand's land area. Many of Bangkok's representative tourist attractions stand along the river. When I came to Bangkok with students many years ago, the water of the Chao Phraya was blue, but this time we saw a muddy earth-yellow, and the water level was much higher because of the rainy season.

The 2024 academic year (ROC year 113) ends today. In the blink of an eye, in a few more years I will have been a teacher for 20 years. My first business trip to Thailand was in 2006, and the Bangkok trip with the graduating class of the Hualien hospitality department is a cherished memory.

On this family self-guided trip, both of my family members were in Thailand for the first time. Their reactions were that prices in Thailand are close to Taipei's, and food choices are fewer than imagined, especially with a family member who can't eat spicy food, when there are almost no choices.

After walking around all day, I came to several conclusions:

1. Reasonably priced clothing isn't worth buying or browsing. The fashion isn't in sync with Taipei, so it's hard to find clothes that suit you.

2. The connections between the metro and the major attractions are still insufficient, and the pedestrian and transport friendliness is similar to New Taipei and Taoyuan, with no sense of leisure.

3. The service industry makes up too large a share in Thailand, and I didn't feel any striving or drive. Put another way, there is not enough learning in digital nomad or competitiveness terms. I can only say I envy the Thais, but we are not them and it's hard to appreciate.

4. There isn't enough variety in restaurants. Most are local restaurants or street food where hygiene and safety can't be guaranteed. The water also doesn't taste good, so we could only drink bottled water.

5. Hotel prices are lower than in Taiwan and service is more flexible, with enough staff to support service quality, but travel from point to point is still not up to standard.

6. The tourist attractions near Bangkok have no huge highlights. The fees for historical sites are heavily commercial, and admission prices have reached the point of giving up. On top of that, the tropical climate all year round makes it feel like you can't go out in the daytime, and the humid heat makes you not want to go out.

Overall, it isn't easy to revisit Bangkok. After visiting Thailand three times, I still can't find a motive to travel there, and I especially don't recommend it for older people. But young people and first-time visitors can treat this deeply international city as a touchstone to add to their travel experience points.
