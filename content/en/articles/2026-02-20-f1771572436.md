Daily Morning Reading No. 1 / 2026.2.20 (Fourth Day of Lunar New Year)

Who would you like to travel with?

Today's reading is an academic paper published in 2025 in the International Journal of Tourism Research (IJTR) by four scholars from China and Australia, titled "Who Would You Travel With? Identifying the Key Attributes of a Desirable Travel Partner." Its findings lay out in an orderly way the considerations people have when looking for travel companions.

The study used data from 1,086 user posts on the Douban platform and analysed them by content analysis, exploring the selection criteria in the popular practice among young Chinese of joining groups to find travel companions, and also analysing the factors influencing especially memorable travel experiences.

Who would you like to travel with? Do you think the same way? The study summarises four traits of the best travel companion:

#1 Emotional intelligence comes first: Emotional intelligence wins trust fast

People who stay calm and emotionally stable under travel stress and are sensitive to others' emotions can create a positive atmosphere even when flights are delayed or the itinerary is blocked.

#2 Experience is worth trusting: Experience builds confidence

Travel companions with travel skills, familiarity with the destination and problem-solving ability make others more willing to rely on them and share resources comfortably.

#3 Similar travel conditions reduce friction: Compatibility reduces friction

For short-term travel companions, the match in budget, personal energy, daily schedule and sightseeing order matters most; personality and values, or whether you click, come second.

#4 A sense of responsibility keeps the trip smooth: Responsibility keeps things running

Companions who carry out their respective duties, stick to the plan and offer practical suggestions make the trip go more smoothly; but under the option of #CreatingMemorableTravelExperiences, the importance of a sense of responsibility is slightly lower than the three above.

Travel patterns keep changing. In 2026 I visited the penguin parade on Phillip Island in Melbourne for the second time. From a rough look at the visitor centre car park, large coach groups were mainly Chinese groups only, and the over 80% of other vehicles were mostly shared one-day-tour minibuses, 20-seaters filled with independent travellers from all over the world. They gather at the meeting point, join the tour together, and disperse afterwards, free and unburdened.

What this paper discusses is the online travel-partner community behaviour that has been popular among young Chinese in recent years. Although it cannot be extrapolated to the travel behaviour of young Taiwanese, in terms of tourism academic research Taiwan lacks in the volume of traveller behaviour and the diversity of destinations. Most travellers concentrate on a few countries, and their travel behaviour also leans towards consumption, food and pleasure, rather than travel experiences such as natural wonders, cultural visits and activities in unfamiliar places.

Source: Fang, X., Yu, J., Zhang, K., & Huang, S. (2025). Who Would You Travel With? Identifying the Key Attributes of a Desirable Travel Partner. International Journal of Tourism Research, 27(5), e70124. (SSCI Q2 IF 5.7)
