Bangkok Travel Day 2: The Dirty Boat Experience on Khlong Saen Saep

Not used to the rhythm of travel, I woke up and fell asleep all night. I decided to go out for a run first thing in the morning to sweat and refresh, but after brunch the headache returned. Still, with the itinerary planned, we went out for a walk. We took the BTS to Siam station and walked to estimate tomorrow's meeting time, and only then found that Bangkok's metro fares are very expensive: just 5 stops cost 32 baht, and walking ahead to Sala Daeng on the return trip cost 28 baht, and it was very crowded. The Thai metro is not good value.

At noon, following online travel recommendations, we ate at Go-Ang Kaiton Pratunam (Hainanese chicken rice, three-time Michelin). Perhaps the Bangkok Michelin judges don't know what good chicken is. Anyway, it was overrated. Having tried it is enough; there is nothing worth mentioning about the taste.

After the meal we plucked up courage to ride the dirty boat (Khlong Saen Saep Express Boat). If you intend to ride, be very careful when boarding and getting off, and I don't recommend including it in a family trip with children or the elderly. The canal water really is dirty and smelly. If you care about hygiene and want a high-class trip, I don't recommend trying it. But when the boat speeds along the canal all the way through to the old town, there is genuinely a sense of time travel. My family felt it was very Bangkok, and a single trip costs just 14 baht, cheap and good value. There's no need to buy a day pass. Just wait for the boat and watch for your stop's name, and after boarding someone will come to ask and collect the fare. We boarded at Pratunam Pier by the Water Gate Market on the westbound line to the old town and rode to the last stop, Panfa Leelard, from where we could walk up to street level and continue the itinerary.

In the afternoon came the heavy rain proper to the rainy season, falling right on the old Boriphat street in the old town, next to the Golden Mount temple that houses the Buddha's relics (buy a ticket if you wish to pay respects). In the heavy rain we strolled along the 19th-century shophouse arcades. If you like wood carvings, you can take time to find a small shop and bargain for a souvenir. With nowhere to go in the rain, we decided to have coffee at JEDI Café by the canal. The university student said he wanted a cappuccino because of the AI meme Cappuccino Assassino. We didn't understand it and thought he was making things up. Then a foreign little kid in a Superman cape sitting nearby saw us chatting about the cappuccino assassin and came over specially to tell us he also loved Cappuccino Assassino. Meeting an internet fellow fan in a foreign land, we later watched giant monitor lizards swimming and walking by the canal together. It was joyful and surprising, and the wildness and childlike heart in the humidity felt delightful.

In the evening on the way back, we paid another 14 baht for the dirty boat to return to the bridge by Pratunam Pier for a Thai massage. My family went to Big C to pass the time, and I rested to recharge, another of the pleasures of travel.
