To the Students Who Keep Dogs: Something Your Teacher Has Never Been Able to Say

I have a daughter who is 14. She has followed us through countless moves, from Taipei to Hualien. No matter how busy we were, she would wait at home for us to come back, however long it took, however many days! Then one day she got stuck behind a cabinet while home alone, and we were travelling in Korea. When we came home after seven days, she was barely alive!

She stayed with us through our hardest days. When we were forced out of our home in Sanchong by gangsters, and during the weekly "ant-moving" between Muzha and Hualien, when load after load of belongings was carried over bit by bit, she was there with us, just like the memories we refused to give up, which had to be pieced back together one fragment at a time. She was family to us, and she could not miss a single occasion.

So when I see you bring your adorable, store-bought puppies to school, there are two things I really want to tell you:

First, if you are young and still have big dreams, if you still hope to go on a working holiday or study abroad, or you will move away for work and leave your hometown, I suggest you not keep a dog. They will miss you and wait for you, but you will forget them.

Second, if you love dogs very much and have the means to care for them and make them part of your family, please adopt a stray. They are just as lovable and just as loyal as a dog from a pet shop, but you will not be encouraging unreasonable breeding practices and a thoroughly corrupted pet market, and you can give one of them a home.

And if you love dogs but do not have time to keep one, then from November 29 please support this Taiwanese film. Giddens (Jiubadao) says all of its profits will go to help every life it possibly can.
