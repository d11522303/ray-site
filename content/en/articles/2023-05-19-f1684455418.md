[Morris Chang's sincere reminder to university students]

Every word is on point, every sentence sincere.

I am quoting the parts related to university students for current students to read carefully, especially the final summary: if you do not properly prepare a skill to make a living during university, you will later find that society does not really welcome you.

I often think back to how, when I took the university entrance exam, I didn't choose my major carefully and didn't align my studies with my own talents and passion, and so truly wasted the most energetic and ambitious years of my youth.

------------Below are excerpts from Morris Chang's talk, compiled by Dr. Ho-Chien Chen---------------

Q: How does university life affect one's future career development, that is, one's personality traits and abilities?

A: I think this question needs to be answered in two parts.

The first part is how university life affects personality,

and the second part is how it affects ability.

On personality, I think university has relatively little effect, because by the time people enter university they are usually already quite mature. And there are many factors that shape personality, such as family upbringing and the friends made from childhood, and by the time a person enters university they are already 18.

On ability, I think university has a great effect. I have always believed that the first thing you must learn in university is the ability to make a living in the future. Some people don't like me saying this, but I feel their picture is actually outdated. Their picture is that they come to university to enjoy knowledge and enjoy gaining wisdom. I think that in ancient Greece that idea did exist, and perhaps it still did up to the 19th century. By the 20th century, things changed greatly: more people went to university, and competition in the workplace intensified.

If you don't properly learn a skill for making a living at university, as soon as you step out you will immediately face what you consider an ugly world. In fact, this world has always been there; it is just that you have to prepare for it. If you prepare for it, then it is no longer an ugly world. But if you don't prepare, if at university you don't prepare a skill for making a living, you will find that the world does not really welcome you.
