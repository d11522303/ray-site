---
title: "The Search for a Nanny"
excerpt: "Recent news reminded me of looking for a nanny when we were new parents, including one unforgettable apartment with a baby, a parrot and an arowana."
published: true
---

<p>Recent news reminded me of the early days of raising a child.</p><p>When the stainless steel apartment door opened, a middle-aged nanny came to greet us. We looked around the living room. There was a plastic floor mat on the floor, and the auntie was sitting on it looking after a small baby. Along the walls were bundles and bundles of red-and-white striped plastic bags. Right at the entrance stood a very large aquarium, in which a red arowana swam in rather murky water. On the other side was a medium-sized parrot, which kept tilting its head to look at the two of us as we walked in, saying nothing.</p><p>Honestly, the moment the door opened we wanted to leave, but the nanny invited us to sit, that is, to sit on the floor mat with her. And she had been introduced by a friend, whose own child had been left with this nanny and grew up safe and healthy. So we chatted a little and routinely asked the basics about food preparation, bath times, daily schedule and so on.</p><p>But we couldn't stop worrying about that parrot, because it stood not far from the baby, its eyes bright as it observed us.</p><p>We asked: Auntie, you raise the children and the animals together in the living room?</p><p>The auntie answered happily: Oh, that parrot is very smart. It can talk. The children can learn to chat with it.</p><p>We couldn't hold back our astonishment, and it must have shown on our faces, so after a few more words we left that rather dim apartment with a baby on the floor, a parrot and an arowana.</p><p>When our little one was very small, about six months old, the two of us, new parents, set about looking for a nanny near home. We wanted a daytime caregiver for working hours, so we asked around online and in the neighbourhood, and after getting leads we visited the nannies' homes directly, to see the environment and chat with the nanny in person. I think this is the first approach for all parents who have to balance work and life while raising a child, because newborn care is complex and delicate, and handing your treasure over for a few hours means worrying for a few hours.</p><p>Without family support or the financial means for other options, we eventually still sent our child to a daycare center during work hours. It looked fairly large-scale, and the caregivers seemed trustworthy when we talked to them. We left the child there for just over two months, because after I found a job in eastern Taiwan our whole family moved to Hualien. That is another story, to be written when I have time.</p>
