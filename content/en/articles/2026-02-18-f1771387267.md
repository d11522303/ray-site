This work is by the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso (1881-1973), who worked mainly in France from 1904 to 1973. The painting "Weeping Woman" (1937, oil on canvas) was acquired in 1986 by the Victorian Art Foundation, with the assistance of the Jack and Ginia Liberman family, for AUD 1.6 million, and is the most expensive acquisition in the history of the Art Gallery of Australia to date.

What is even more interesting is that the painting was once stolen, and possibly by cultured people. After stealing it, they wrapped it up carefully and left it in a locker at Southern Cross Station. Their main appeal was for the government to value important art and cultural objects, so to this day nobody knows who did it. But at least this painting, now worth over a hundred million Australian dollars, still hangs in the most prominent position in the museum for everyone to see.

Below is a translation of the wall label on site:

The "Weeping Woman" series was created in late 1937 and is regarded as an "epilogue" to Picasso's famous painting Guernica. These shocking images repeatedly present the intense theme of a woman displaying her grief nakedly in public. Their imagery comes from the figure at the far left of the Guernica mural, a mother who screams uncontrollably in the bombing, flees in vain, and clutches her dead child to her chest. In addition, critics also read in them Picasso's turbulent emotional life: the works interweave the complex web of relationships between him and his ex-wife Olga Khokhlova, and his contemporaneous lovers Marie-Thérèse Walter and Dora Maar.
