#TourLeaderExamCountdown80Days

36. A tourist on a Singapore street sees a procession of barefoot ascetics carrying arch-shaped frames, with milk pots on their heads and iron hooks piercing their skin, tongues or faces. Which of the following festivals is most likely what he encountered? ① Thaipusam ② Hari Raya Aidilfitri ③ Vesak ④ Nine Emperor Gods Festival

Explanation:

In Southeast Asia's multi-ethnic societies, religious festivals are an important window into understanding historical migration and cultural coexistence. Thaipusam originates from Hinduism and is mainly celebrated by the Tamil community to commemorate the legend of the war god Murugan's victory over evil. During the festival, devotees show their piety and spirit of self-discipline through long pilgrimages, carrying the kavadi, and body-piercing rituals.

Hari Raya Aidilfitri (Eid al-Fitr) is an important Islamic festival marking the end of the fasting month of Ramadan. Through communal prayers, family reunions and asking for each other's forgiveness, Muslims emphasise self-discipline, gratitude and social harmony.

Vesak (Buddha Day) belongs to the traditions of Theravada Buddhist countries and commemorates the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and nirvana. Believers practise compassion and wisdom through bathing the Buddha, chanting, almsgiving and releasing animals, showing the deep influence of Buddhist culture in Southeast Asian society. By comparison, the Nine Emperor Gods Festival originates from Chinese folk belief and the Taoist system. In nine days of vegetarianism, welcoming and sending off the gods and various rituals, devotees pray for misfortune to be dispelled and community peace. These four festivals represent Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism and Chinese folk belief respectively, and develop side by side in places such as Malaysia and Singapore, fully reflecting the religious diversity and cultural interweaving formed in Southeast Asian society through migration, colonisation and long-term exchange.
