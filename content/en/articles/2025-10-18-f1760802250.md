[Midlife Crisis? Middle-Aged People Must Hang On]

A study published in March 2025 by the US National Bureau of Economic Research: Want to say goodbye to the midlife crisis? Sadly, the happiness curve is no longer U-shaped, and middle-aged people have to hang on.

The main points of the research:

The researchers found that young people in six English-speaking countries are becoming less and less happy, and the statistical trends reveal worrying mental health patterns. In all six countries, rates of anxiety, depression and self-harm among young people are rising. Australian data show that between 2007-2010 and 2019-2022, rates of mental disorders among young people rose by 40% for men and 60% for women. In the UK, prescriptions for antidepressants rose by 25% between 2015 and 2019. The study also notes that the decline in young people's wellbeing began around 2012-2013, coinciding with the spread of smartphones and social media, suggesting that changes in how we communicate may affect mental health.

In contrast, the wellbeing of today's older people has stayed stable or improved, keeping the traditional U-shaped happiness curve. But in this era young people are unhappy and the middle-aged are uneasy, so the real curve may no longer rise with age. It becomes a curve of ups and downs, and there's no such thing as U-shaped happiness any more.

So if you want to live a happy life, cut down on interaction on social media and look less at other people's wonderful lives. Perhaps looking at other people's tragedies will help you find the drive to keep living, and comfort you at the same time. Or else watch other people running every day, because I can roughly understand that people with that habit are all hanging on for something.
