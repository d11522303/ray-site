Opposite Case Study

In April I had a meal at a chain restaurant in Hualien. I wanted to order the extra-large beef rice bowl, large size, NT$159 (it is right there on the printed menu!), but the server told me there was only the small set meal at NT$139. I asked why it wasn't available.

Server: Sorry, only our noodle dishes can be upsized.

Me: But it's on the colour menu from your head office.

Server: Really?

Me: Go and get the menu at the counter and show her. Look!

Server: Oh, well, we still don't sell it, because if we upsize rice dishes, it doesn't work with our costs.

Me: Speechless...

This was only my second visit since I had been looking forward to this chain reopening in Hualien. The first time, the staff ignored me and the service attitude disappointed me so much that I stayed away for a while. Recently I saw the staff had changed and wanted to give them another chance, and it turned out to be even more disappointing!!

From the viewpoint of service theatre theory, a service encounter contains many moments that offer a chance for service recovery. This is especially true when a loyal customer makes a suggestion politely: the front-line staff member should be the lead performer in a show, rather than explaining things to the customer in terms of internal costs. But then, perhaps this shop is a franchise and it really was the proprietress herself explaining to me that one upsized bowl of rice would put them out of business!
