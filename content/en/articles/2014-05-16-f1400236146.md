---
title: "Taiwan's Night Markets Are Booming, but Who Pays the Hidden Costs?"
excerpt: "With Tourism Bureau support, night markets are everywhere, yet no receipts, no credit cards, no business registration. I wonder what this costs the taxpaying public."
published: true
---

With the push from the Tourism Bureau, Taiwan's night market culture has blossomed everywhere and become a "pride of Taiwan"?

But after visiting quite a few, I found that from south to north, the stalls in every night market are much the same; the designs are equally tacky; the surroundings equally messy.

Yet there is a big question that keeps nagging at me:

Every time we go to a night market, my family of three spends NT$300 or more on average.

But nobody issues a receipt?

And not a single stall takes credit cards??

And not one stall has a business registration???

And of course, the part-time girls calling out to customers (who look like child labour!), do they have labour and health insurance????

If I eat fried crab and have an allergic reaction, will the health department help investigate?????

..............

Thinking about this, I really feel sorry for those of us salaried workers who dutifully pay our taxes in Taiwan.

Even the official figures show that in 2013 the annual business revenue of Taiwan's street vendors was NT$551 billion

(I don't know whether this includes the newer large night market stalls), but most likely, beyond making the official unemployment rate a little lower, the cost paid by society may be even higher.

Reference: Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics, 2013 statistics
