【The Courage to Refuse Needs to Be Cultivated】

After reading Xiaoxiong Mama's post, I remembered something that happened to me in the second year of junior high.

Years ago, junior highs were all boys' schools, and classes were divided into A+ classes, A-track classes and B-track classes (I have been in all three kinds, and may share more another time). In my second year I was in an A-track class, and my homeroom teacher at the time is now a retired, well-known high school principal.

My grades were not bad, so I served as the teaching assistant for one of the homeroom teacher's subjects, recording scores and helping mark papers. Once the papers were marked and the scores recorded, it was the teacher's time to hit people: palm strikes, spankings, cheek pulling, half-squats, every kind of punishment. So everyone who took an exam was afraid, and so was whoever marked the papers and recorded the scores. If I got a score wrong, a classmate would be hit the wrong number of times, and I would be in trouble with everyone.

Classmates often came to me asking me to fix small mistakes. Mostly it was a "misreading" that could always be corrected before the teacher struck. It was a hassle and a worry, but hard to refuse! Thinking about it now, why was this a student's job? It was really the teacher being lazy.

One day, the big trouble was bound to come.

A classmate who was a kind of "big brother" in the class had done terribly on the exam, and it was certain he would be beaten until his backside was black and blue. At noon break he came to me to "discuss" it, and asked for my answers so he could copy them! I did not want to, but I was also afraid he would come after me outside school. I had learned this in first year when I was messing around in the B-track class: you have to read the situation, and you have to bow your head.

A few days passed, and I felt miserable and worried. I could not study and could not sleep well! Then the homeroom teacher appeared and called the two of us to the year-level teachers' office (a very large one, the kind where many teachers sit together).

The teacher said he did not believe that classmate could have scored well, and asked me whether I had helped change his answers.

I was terrified. I glanced at my classmate, he nodded, so I nodded and admitted it too!

The teacher exploded. Right there, he had the classmate and me lean with our hands on the desk in front of all the teachers of the whole year, and with a cane half as long as a person he viciously gave the classmate 20 hard strokes on the backside first, then, just as furiously, gave me the same 20 strokes in front of all the teachers.

I did not cry at the time, and neither did my classmate. The "big brother" said to me sincerely: "Sorry, it won't happen again."

Even as I type these words, I can still feel the burning heat on my backside and the heartache of humiliation and resentment!

But from that day on, I no longer had to be afraid walking around outside in Yonghe, I felt no unease inside, I could study again, and I slept until I could hardly get up.

But to this day, I still hate my second-year homeroom teacher.
