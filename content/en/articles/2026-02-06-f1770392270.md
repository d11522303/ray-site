#Tour Leader Exam Countdown: 36 Days

33 Lake Baikal in Siberia, Russia, has tourism resources nearby such as volcanic activity and hot springs, and is the deepest lake in the world, reaching 1,700 metres at its deepest. What is the main reason the lake is so deep? (A) River incision (B) Meteorite impact (C) Plate rifting (D) Glacial erosion

Explanation: The answer to this question is (C).

But geological causes aside, do you know the story of Su Wu herding sheep by the North Sea in the Han dynasty? That North Sea is Lake Baikal, now within Russia's borders. Tour groups from Taiwan often go there, so it is a popular destination. Hsing Wu's Department of Travel Management has interns working with Expert Travel, which runs tours there. Those interested can see the comments.

Lake Baikal covers about 31,722 square kilometres and lies 111 kilometres from the Mongolian border. It is the freshwater lake with the largest water volume in the world and also the deepest lake in the world, at its deepest 1,742 metres. It was inscribed as a World Natural Heritage site in 1996. The lake is 680 kilometres long from north to south and 40 to 50 kilometres wide from east to west. Lake Baikal is a rift lake, also known as a fault lake, formed when fault movement within a rift zone causes a block of land to subside. The East African Rift lakes, the Dead Sea and Lake Superior on the border between Canada and the United States are all rift lakes geologically.

#1 Which of the following statements about Lake Baikal is correct for a tour leader giving a guided commentary?

(A) It is the freshwater lake with the largest surface area in the world, about the size of Taiwan.

(B) It is the deepest lake in the world, holding about one fifth of the world's unfrozen fresh water.

(C) It is a saltwater lake, so it does not freeze even in the harsh Siberian winter.

(D) It is a border lake between Russia and Mongolia, and the northern half of the lake belongs to Russia.

Answer: (B)

#2 After the group arrives in Ulaanbaatar, the capital of Mongolia, and prepares to head to higher-altitude areas such as the Khangai Mountains, an elderly member reports dizziness and slight shortness of breath. As the tour leader, which of the following is the most appropriate response?

(A) Judge it to be an ordinary cold and immediately give the member strong fever-reducing medicine.

(B) Remind the member to drink plenty of water and reduce strenuous activity, closely monitor their blood oxygen level, and arrange for descent to a lower altitude immediately if symptoms worsen.

(C) Suggest the member drink local fermented mare's milk, believing alcohol promotes blood circulation and relieves altitude discomfort.

(D) So as not to delay the itinerary, encourage the member to persist and complete the day's mountain hike.

Answer: (B)
