Bangkok Trip Day 4-2: Down the Chao Phraya River

The Chao Phraya River (Menam River) is 1,352 kilometres long, with a drainage basin of 150,000 square kilometres, about one third of Thailand's land area. Many of Bangkok's representative tourist attractions stand beside the river. Years ago, when I came to Bangkok with students on their graduation trip, the water of the Chao Phraya was blue, but this time we saw a muddy yellow-brown, and the water was also much higher because of the rainy season.

The next part of the itinerary was to take the cheap Chao Phraya river boat to the riverside night market, with a Ladyboy cabaret show in the evening. But we then found that the Marine Dept stop could only take us to the stop before Asiatique. I asked the lady at the Wat Rat Singkhon pier, and she said it was just a short walk next to it, and that the pier only runs until 18:00 and was about to close, with no more boats after that. So the three of us strolled towards the Ferris wheel. Primary school students next to the pier were just getting out of school, and we went with them to buy cheap ice cream and lemon tea, and sat down for a while to enjoy the air conditioning.

On my last graduation trip with students on a package tour, we saw the Tiffany's Show (Jin Dongni cabaret), which I don't recommend much. This time my family said that coming to Thailand without seeing one felt like missing something, so we booked a dinner with the CALYPSO cabaret show and traditional Thai dance through Klook. There were not many people at dinner and the food was ordinary, but having traditional Thai dance with the meal was fun, and afterwards we could take photos before moving to the stage area to watch the show. The CALYPSO show was spectacular, with great lighting, sound and stage effects. I recorded a short clip for reference, and I really recommend seeing it live.

The atmosphere at the riverside market at night is great, with quite a few restaurants where you can drink and listen to music. I would suggest just watching the show or Muay Thai and then moving to a riverside restaurant to eat and chat. The night view of the Chao Phraya is much better than by day, with a gentle cool breeze and drifting music. Many people take various kinds of cruise to tour the river, which also looks nice, and is worth trying if you get the chance. Later a student told me the night cruises come in different grades, and there are even adult versions. Please look them up online yourself. I don't know about that!
