---
title: "Teaching Vietnamese Students and a Response to the Commonwealth Magazine Article"
excerpt: "Ray finds meaning in teaching diligent Vietnamese students as Taiwan's tourism and vocational education decline, and questions a magazine article that criticises without offering ideas."
published: true
---

TSMC is building plants in the US, and the media each say their own thing. Since I have no green card and am not an engineer, the changes in the economy and capital markets of recent years have nothing to do with me.

What does concern me is that Taiwan's tourism and vocational learning environment has kept declining, and thirty years of career investment and choices now look like a joke.

In class yesterday, as I explained the service content of tourism in Chinese, I was also learning the pronunciation of Vietnamese attractions with the Vietnamese students. I hope these students from afar will bring some opportunities and hope for me and for the future. I look positively on these focused and earnest Vietnamese students in my work environment. Like Taiwanese university students, they have to attend classes from 8 a.m. until nearly 5 p.m., and come and go freely on campus and in the city, just as we Taiwanese do when studying in Europe and America. As a teacher, I keep filling in my own understanding of international students, because behind every clear young pair of eyes there is a soul that hopes to grow.

From the Vietnamese students' earnest study of Chinese and their professional attitude to studying tourism, I gain dignity in teaching. And Vietnam has a young population of 100 million, an economic growth rate of nearly 9% last year, and the government has declared that the 1,500-kilometre north-south high-speed railway will open in 2035 and the country will become high-income by 2045. This gives me something to look forward to.

So I find this article in Commonwealth Magazine frustrating. The problems certainly exist, but an article that only points out problems without offering ideas, and influences the public with a self-righteous, only-criticising viewpoint, does it help Taiwanese society? Is it really true that private schools recruiting international students are just a back door for migrant labour?
