2025.4.17 Third Life: 836 days to go

Some things, once done, you can brag about for a lifetime;

Some things, if you only think about them, you will be nagged about for a lifetime.

I am very grateful to my student for the introduction. Whatever happens in the future, I must remember the kindness shown today. Yesterday, while watching a drama, one line moved me, about "making a decision".

A colleague asked the female lead: It's been hard lately, hasn't it?

The female lead: Actually it's fine. The hardest thing is when you can't make a decision.

I am long used to the life of teaching, and the regular income and rhythm make it hard to go back to a workplace in the service industry. But I know that from the moment I had this thought, it meant my life would end right here, and all its splendour and possibility would be zero. These past few days I thought it over and over, torn both ways, and in the end it was the line in the drama that turned my thinking into action.

I have set aside the idea of a doctorate in cultural architecture and urban planning and deleted the time slots from my calendar. I also asked a student who works as a tour leader on European tours in the travel industry about the possibility of part-time work, and he quickly referred possible contacts to me. I was very moved, because this is not easy. I know quite a few people in the travel industry, and many are known for their connections and often mention their relationships, but even getting a chance to meet is not easy, and even when I told my own classmates I wanted to lead groups, they would say it was the arrangement of the tour operator. Hard indeed, hard indeed.

For connecting with my future career, I need to start working from now, because in three years I might be forced into retiring from a private school. I don't want to be at a loss when that happens. Instead I hope to use this period to build my social media presence well and accumulate professional experience and contacts in tour groups, to be ready for the future.
