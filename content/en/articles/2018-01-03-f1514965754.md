The Ministry of Education is drafting an "Act on Off-Campus Internship Education for Junior Colleges and Above" to firmly strengthen the off-campus internship system and protect the rights of interns, and will hold four public hearings starting on 4 January 2018.

Key points of the legislation:

1. The principles for schools running off-campus internship education are written into law. Schools must offer off-campus internship courses according to the nature of each department's specialty and its competency-training needs. If an internship is a graduation requirement, it must be written into the school's regulations, and the school must select suitable internship organisations that meet the requirements for students.

2. The mechanisms for schools running off-campus internship education are strengthened. Schools must set up internship committees at each level, and must assign staff to guide and visit students during their internships. If students feel their rights are being harmed during an internship, there must be a mechanism for dispute resolution or complaints.

3. The protection of interns' rights is made explicit. Depending on the content of the internship, off-campus internships are divided into a general type and a work type, with clearly defined rights protections for each type, and the provisions on compensation for injuries arising from learning, training or internship work are strengthened, so as to genuinely safeguard interns' rights and safety during their internships.

4. The principles for schools arranging internships overseas are strengthened and the protection of overseas interns' rights is increased. Schools must clarify the relevant local laws and visa requirements and properly assess the host organisations. If an intern's rights are harmed and the situation is urgent, the intern must be recalled immediately.

5. A penalty mechanism is established. Penalty clauses are set out for each type of violation by schools and internship organisations, and for some violations a deadline for improvement is set. If improvement is not completed or proves ineffective, the competent authority may bar the school or internship organisation from running or taking part in off-campus internship education.
