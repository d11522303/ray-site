Bangkok Trip Day 8

Bangkok's pavements are not easy to walk on, uneven and full of ups and downs. Tall buildings and low-rise ones are mixed together across the city, and buses without air conditioning and motorbikes are still what residents rely on most. BTS fares are quite a bit higher than the Taipei MRT: a few hundred baht loaded onto a Rabbit card runs out in no time, and taxis and tuk-tuks all require haggling. Since we did not want to spend effort on transport, the department stores and sights along the BTS line were still our first choice.

Hot weather is normal in Bangkok. The heat itself is not scary. What is scary is that inside department stores you need a light jacket for the air conditioning, but the moment you step onto the elevated Skywalk, the heat is steaming. Going back and forth between the two, you have to watch out for your body not adjusting. So I, who love winter, have never listed tropical Bangkok among my favourite cities, much like Singapore, which I rarely visit unless I have to.

In the alleys off Sukhumvit Road there are Michelin-listed little eateries with only electric fans. The smells of food and people mingled together, and after a look we still turned into the food courts of the various department stores. They are not as cheap and tasty as the street stalls and local food, but we would rather not have to dip into the stomach medicine in our suitcase. Do you have any recommendations? In principle, at stalls we only dare to eat things freshly grilled or fried, and always as takeaway, never sitting down to eat.

Overall, though, I really envy this world-famous tourist city. It has a rich cultural foundation, with religious sites, royal palaces and historic ruins everywhere, and it has built a reputation in the global travel market, making it the first-choice city for many foreign visitors. Statistics show that in 2024 Thailand received 35.32 million international visitors, generating 1.66 trillion baht in tourism foreign-exchange revenue.

In 2024, Taiwan received only 7.87 million visitors, and the Tourism Administration, MOTC estimates international tourism revenue at about NT$322 billion. In terms of tourism economic scale, Taiwan is only one fifth of Thailand. Having actually toured Bangkok, I do not feel it is more fun than Taipei, and its cultural and historical depth is far greater, depending on whether we care about that. Overall, Taipei's museums, theatres and food are more competitive in quality and price, and its climate, transport and urban convenience are infrastructure decades ahead of Bangkok. If tourism has no pull, there may be other reasons that I cannot see.

Our family stayed in the city centre of Bangkok at ASOK, in a four-star hotel on Soi 18 for seven nights with meals for under NT$20,000. The rooms were clean and tidy and spacious, with a simple kitchen that made it very convenient to prepare food and use the space, and the staff spoke fluent English and were attentive and warm. It is a five-minute walk from Asok (E4) station on the BTS light green line, which makes it very suitable for family travel. I will post the hotel price in the comments for reference. At breakfast each day we ate with travellers of all nationalities, and there was no jostling for food. The breakfast spread was not large, but it changed a little every day and was not the same each day. The many tourists in the neighbourhood and the good public safety were the most important reasons we could rest easy at the hotel after each day of sightseeing.
