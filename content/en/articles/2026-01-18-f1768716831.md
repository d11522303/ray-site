4 Ancient Greek tragedies are very famous. "Oedipus the King" describes a prince of Thebes whom an oracle said would "kill his father and marry his mother." In the end the oracle came true. To punish himself, he gouged out his eyes in atonement. Who was this tragedian?

(A) Aeschylus

(B) Sophocles

(C) Euripides

(D) Pindar

Solution: The answer is (B)

The Origin of the Story of the #OedipusComplex

King #Laius of Thebes learned from an oracle that his son would kill his father and marry his mother. To prevent the tragedy, he ordered his newborn son #Oedipus to be abandoned in the wilderness to die. However, Oedipus was fortunate to be adopted by the king of a neighbouring country. When Oedipus grew up he learned of the same oracle, and to protect his adoptive parents (whom he mistakenly thought were his birth parents), he resolutely left his homeland and went alone to Thebes. On the journey, in a dispute over right of way, he accidentally killed an old man (who was in fact his birth father #Laius). Afterwards, using his wits, he solved the riddle of the Sphinx and saved Thebes, which was suffering a plague, and was acclaimed as king. As custom dictated he married the former king's widow, who was his birth mother #Jocasta.

Years later Thebes suffered famine again, and an oracle revealed that the murderer of the former king must be found. #Oedipus vowed to investigate the truth, only to realise in the course of the inquiry that he himself was the murderer. After the truth came out, Queen #Jocasta killed herself in shame, and #Oedipus, in despair, put out his own eyes and went into exile abroad, atoning and ending this cursed tragedy.

Later, when reading Sophocles' play, Freud believed that the reason this drama can still shock audiences across thousands of years is that it touches fears and desires buried deep in the human unconscious. Freud proposed that in early childhood a boy develops a strong dependence on and possessiveness toward his mother, and regards his powerful father as a rival, developing a psychological tendency to reject his father and be close to his mother. This is the #OedipusComplex that we often hear about.

#1 In Greek mythology there is a famous guardian creature called the Sphinx. Which of the following descriptions of her and her role in the story of Oedipus the King is incorrect?

(A) She is usually depicted with a woman's face, a lion's body, and a pair of wings

(B) She stopped passersby outside the city of Thebes and devoured them if they could not solve her riddle

(C) Oedipus defeated the Sphinx by force, shaming her into killing herself, and was therefore acclaimed king

(D) The answer to the famous riddle is "man," describing the human life journey from infancy through adulthood to old age

Correct answer: (C)

#2 While leading a European architecture and arts tour visiting the Theatre of Dionysus at the Acropolis in Athens, the tour guide mentions that ancient Greek tragedy is not only art but also shows a deep analysis of human nature. One work describes a protagonist who solves the riddle of the Sphinx and becomes king, yet ultimately finds himself mired in the fate of "killing his father and marrying his mother," and then blinds himself and wanders. Which work, praised by Aristotle as the "model of tragedy," is it?

(A) Medea

(B) Prometheus Bound

(C) Oedipus Rex

(D) Antigone

Correct answer: (C)
