19 The Ten Major Construction Projects were a series of national construction projects undertaken by the government after rapid economic growth in 1965. Which of the following combinations are all among the Ten Major Construction Projects? ① Sun Yat-sen Freeway ② Kaohsiung Port ③ Hualien Port ④ Taichung Port ⑤ Railway electrification ⑥ Kaohsiung Airport

#PerfectTen

Explanation: Actually, using 1965 for this question is inaccurate. The Ten Major Construction Projects date from the 1970s. They were proposed and promoted in 1973 by Premier Chiang Ching-kuo and carried out mainly between 1974 and 1979, aiming to improve Taiwan's infrastructure and drive industrial upgrading, not in 1965. Led by government policy after the global oil crisis, this series of nationwide large-scale projects used huge domestic investment to drive economic momentum. Six were transport projects, three were heavy industry projects and one was an energy project, with a total cost then estimated at NT$200 billion to NT$300 billion. These projects led Taiwan out of the energy crisis, accelerated economic and social development at the time, and contributed in some measure to the Taiwan economic miracle.

#TheTenMajorConstructionProjectsInclude: Sun Yat-sen Freeway, railway electrification, North-Link Railway, Chiang Kai-shek International Airport, Taichung Port, Suao Port, China Steel Corporation, China Shipbuilding Corporation, the petrochemical industry, and nuclear power plants.

This map shows the year for 1 January 2026 in different calendar systems. Most of the world, including Europe, the Americas, Africa and Australia, uses the Gregorian calendar, so the year is 2026. But some countries use other calendar systems.

Japan is Reiwa 8 and Imperial Year 2686. China officially uses the Gregorian calendar, but for some reason the map shows the Yellow Emperor era, Taoist calendar, Xuanyuan 4723. North Korea and Taiwan use their own founding-era calendars, with the year 115. Many Muslim countries follow the Islamic calendar, with the year 1447. In South Asia the differences are striking: India uses the Indian national calendar (1947), Nepal uses the Nepali calendar (1146), Thailand uses the Buddhist calendar (2569), Bhutan uses the Drukpa calendar (2482), and far away in Africa the ancient civilisation of Ethiopia treats this as the year 2018!
