<p>Instead of asking online whether a school is good, go and see it in person! Most university departments are open to visitors now. Before applying through recommendation or personal application, walk into the department and campus yourself and pay attention to the following: </p><p>1. What does the department bulletin board announce?<br/> (1) If it's teacher promotions or awards, go to the website and see who those professors are, what they teach, and whether you like it.<br/> (2) Student certificate results, internships, employment, competitions <br/> (3) Course information and exchange activities<br/> (4) More important is how up to date all of this is. Perhaps posters from a few years ago are still up, and then you know.</p><p>2. How does the campus and department environment feel?<br/> (1) Whether the campus is tidy and clean reflects the quality of the students and the school.<br/> (2) Whether the toilets are clean reflects how much the school values students and teachers.<br/> (3) If there is a student cafeteria, it's best to walk around, smell it and try it, because that will be a daily highlight in your future.</p><p>3. If there is a student dormitory, be sure to go and see it!<br/> (1) Ask students as they come and go how many share a room and how much it costs.<br/> (2) See whether there are blind spots around it.<br/> (3) Go on Dcard or Threads and see whether anyone has posted about the school dorms.</p><p>4. Don't skip the department website<br/> (1) Read about every full-time professor carefully, including their rank, specialty and courses.<br/> (2) Count how many professors there are. Too many and you will never know them all; too few means the student-teacher ratio is too high and it will always be the same few teaching.<br/> (3) Print out the overall course table and look at the courses carefully.<br/> (4) How "new" is the latest news (this is important).<br/> (5) Does the department list its specialised classrooms and equipment?<br/> (6) How good are the corporate internship partners, and are they well-known companies?</p><p>5. The school's geographic location is also important<br/>(1) How much commuting time will you need to get to and from class?<br/>(2) Rent and quality of nearby housing.<br/>(3) Is the traffic at the school gate chaotic?<br/>(4) What proportion of students ride scooters, and which models?</p><hr/><figure class="ph">Photo</figure>
