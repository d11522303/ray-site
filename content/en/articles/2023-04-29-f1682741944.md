【Is Taiwan's Most Professional Vocational High School About to Be Phased Out⁉️ 】

The news: Taitung's Tung-Kung Vocational High School placed under special guidance

Loving to make things with our hands is human nature. Because of my work I travel all over Taiwan recruiting students. Back when I was in Hualien, I visited the Tung-Kung vocational school twice at admissions fairs, and I greatly admired the students' work and the expertise the teachers had passed on. Yet more than ten years later, because of the falling birth rate, Tung-Kung is still on the list of schools under special guidance, and has formally entered the "hospice ward" of private schools.

In Daxi, Taoyuan, in northern Taiwan, there is also a private school, Chih Shan High School, with a professional woodworking department. I worry people don't know about it. These schools can't recruit students not because the teaching is poor or the teachers aren't excellent. Ask yourself, as a parent, would you send your child to study woodworking?

For years our education system has assessed students only by having them write answers with a pen: memorise, recite, fill in the blanks. So everyone in society says hands-on vocational education is important, but the cram schools near the schools are doing assessment worksheets, and the scrolling signs read 【Midterm math 100: Chen OO, 100: Lin OO, 99: Wang OO】. Wealthy families have their children learn violin and piano in music classes, and the posters read 【Chang OO and Chiu OO passed the music certification】. So, besides the falling birth rate, the atmosphere of society and the outlook of the education authorities shape what people choose to study.

I am grateful that the tourism and hospitality industry has had role models such as Wu Pao-chun, Chef Ah-chi and Chairman Yen, who were praised and promoted by the media, so parents who send their children into the hospitality and tourism programmes don't feel disappointed or anxious. But the Ministry of Education is cutting vocational tourism and hospitality places by 5% every year, slowly starving the training of talent for the industry at the root. What a pity! Instead of using the enthusiasm and momentum for learning to improve the quality of society from its foundations, they swing the direction of learning for short-term gain. Over the past decade, haven't the metaverse, NFTs, regional revitalisation, and even AI all risen and fallen, washed away with the tide?
