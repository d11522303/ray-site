Electrical Engineering at NTU, NTHU, NYCU and NCKU

I compiled the total admissions for electrical engineering departments at NTU, NTHU, NYCU and NCKU for the 2024 academic year (ROC year 113): 659 places in all. After excluding some additional quota places, I made a small chart, with the following takeaways:

1. Ranking of top-university electrical engineering intake: NYCU (232) > NTU (159) = NCKU (159) > NTHU (109). AI and the semiconductor industry are hot, yet top-university electrical engineering has only 659 places, compared with 653 in 2023 (ROC year 112). There is no obvious increase, so it remains a scarce resource.

2. Ranking of places by admission route: individual application (365) > distribution admission (147) > Star Plan recommendation (120). But the admission timeline runs over eight months: the General Scholastic Ability Test in January, Star Plan applications in February, Star Plan results in March, a free-for-all of first and second stage screening in May, individual application results in June, the Advanced Subjects Test in July and its results in August.

3. NTU and NTHU hold their second-stage individual application exams on the same day. Applicants to top electrical engineering departments and to medical schools are mostly the same group of students, so apart from NTU electrical engineering, most departments end up filling their places from waitlists. Medical schools are the same.

The development of AI did not appear suddenly. I attach my own top-ten choices by group from when I took the college entrance exam in 1991 (ROC year 80). The ranking in Group 2 is not much different from today's. For over thirty years, Taiwanese people's expectations and choices have been the same: electrical engineering has always been what all candidates and parents hope for. The reason is simply economic. Choosing literature, history and philosophy should be interest-driven, and it is only right to choose them if your family is under no financial pressure.

As for myself, I didn't think much back then. Not yet worldly-wise and not careful enough, I put down sociology without considering my own family situation. I didn't think it through, and I chose the school rather than the major, which is the most failed example of it.
