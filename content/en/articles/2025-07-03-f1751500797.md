[Reporter Chia-Jung Wu reports] In response to the growth in tourist numbers in the Matsu area, and to increase the number of tour guides in the region and raise the capacity and quality of visitor reception, the county government's Tourism Bureau will hold a preparatory course for the 2017 national tour guide examination from the 11th to the 13th. Experts and scholars from Taiwan are invited to give lectures and analysis, and three mock exams and highlights of question types are arranged to help students become familiar with the exam format and test-taking skills, effectively raising the pass rate of Matsu tour guides in the national examination.

The tour guide exam preparatory course admits 30 students, and registration is already full. Priority went to holders of the Matsu Tourism Guide Certificate and those registered as residents of Lienchiang County who intend to take the tour guide exam. Classes are held in the conference room on the 4th floor of the county government's Tourism Bureau, for a total of 24 hours.

Instructors include Ting-Shuo Weng, Professor and Board Director in the Department of Hotel Management at Tainan University of Technology; Huai-Chen Wang, Lecturer in the Wellness and Leisure Management Degree Program at Tainan University of Technology; Ruey-Feng Chen, Assistant Professor in the Department of Travel Management at Hsing Wu University; and Pin-Kang Chu, Director at Lion Travel.

Day 1 (the 11th): Overview of Tourism Resources, Taiwan (history, geography, preservation of tourism resources) and World (history, geography, preservation of tourism resources), with a mock exam and highlights of question types. Day 2 (the 12th): Practice (II), covering tourism administration and regulations; the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area and understanding of current cross-strait relations; the Hong Kong and Macao relations regulations; entry and exit laws; foreign exchange knowledge; the travel chapter of the Civil Code's law of obligations; and standard contracts for overseas travel, with a mock exam and highlights of question types.

Day 3 (the 13th): Practice (I), covering guiding, interpretation and practice; air ticketing; first-aid knowledge; travel safety and emergency handling; tourism psychology and behaviour; and international etiquette, with a mock exam and highlights of question types. Students who miss one-third or more of the class hours will not be issued a certificate of completion; this course cannot be used to apply for reserved eligibility, and students may not apply for a leave of absence.
