[What Do You Look at When You Get a Report Card?]

Scores in each subject? Class rank? Rank within the school? Overall national rank?

A hardworking high school student has just finished the national mock exam, and the system emailed a username and password so parents can log in and check. Opening the report card the system provided, you can see from the screenshot table that the number of candidates nationwide taking Chinese, English, Maths A and Science at this moment is 64,573, with 804 classmates from the same school and 38 in the class. The personal rank for this exam is recorded at the front.

The report cards from my own high school days have long since gone missing, but I still clearly remember that the moment I got a report card, what I looked at was not my score in each subject, but I hurriedly looked for my rank, which place I was in the class. So when I entered the system, the first thing I noticed was also the ranking at the very bottom.

Q: If the one looking at the report card were my high-school self, what is the first number that would imprint itself on my mind?

Q: Would the young me encourage myself, saying the ranking of the score doesn't mean much, it isn't a comparison, we should look at our own strengths and listen to the voice deep in our hearts?

Q: Could I manage not to think about how other classmates ranked? Or would I scroll through Instagram to see whether any classmates posted?

On another note, the night before last, for the 108 Curriculum's diverse-learning micro-credit course, the mother and the high schooler discussed the project content until midnight, and only then settled on a topic that both were happy with and willing to invest in. Under the current exam system, the Chinese, English, Maths and Science exams are the foundation of learning, but the Ministry of Education's curriculum system also has a big pile of learning-portfolio and diverse-learning uploads waiting for high school students to fill in and submit.
