Bangkok Trip Day 6: A dream-come-true trip to Kanchanaburi's Death Railway, the zoo and the Bridge on the River Kwai

I remember that during the pandemic I listed several travel destinations I looked forward to in life, and today's Bridge on the River Kwai and Death Railway is one of them. When the car reached the broad River Kwai, I truly couldn't imagine how they could have built a bridge here back then! The river span crossed by the bridge in the film wasn't nearly as grand as what we saw today. I think the foreign friends there all felt the same about the gap between the film and the real location.

In fact, when we first arrived in Bangkok, the driver who took us to the hotel asked where we wanted to go. When we mentioned the River Kwai, he said the expressway from Bangkok to Kanchanaburi had been completed, so the drive can be kept within two hours, more comfortable than before. Otherwise a one-day trip to the River Kwai bridge and Death Railway would be a rather hard journey.

This itinerary was a Bangkok holiday tour we booked in Taiwan, where travel companions from different places ride together in a nine-seat minivan. Today's companions were all quiet fellow travellers, and the guide didn't chat nonstop and even took lots of lovely photos of our family. The two groups of young people in the same car were also punctual and quiet, and listened to the explanations very seriously and attentively. It really is nice to have good travel companions. By contrast, I often drifted off and then asked more questions, becoming the "nagging adult" in my children's words.

The Death Railway trip was full of local culture and flavour. At the country station the number of mosquitoes was astonishing, so we tried to walk around along the tracks to avoid being attacked by unknown bugs. The train was also, as usual, late, and things only improved once the long, slow train finally pulled in.

On the full carriage we met a group of Chinese-department students from Suan Sunandha Rajabhat University in Bangkok, who chatted with us about their motivation for learning Chinese, and we did a little citizen diplomacy. They also recommended the snacks sold by vendors on the train, and after getting off they shared the best viewing spot with our family, so we could record, from the best angle, the destination I had long held in my heart as a lifetime travel goal. I was very satisfied.
