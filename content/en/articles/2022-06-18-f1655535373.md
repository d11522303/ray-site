Good Listening Share No. 3: 28 Minutes

[The Trap That Keeps Life Stuck]

Some skills come naturally, for example: eating.

But using the tools for eating well is not so simple.

Can everyone hold chopsticks properly?

On the way home from working late the night before last, I listened to a thought-provoking podcast (link in the comments), and I really thought back: what are the things I picked up easily and made a habit of, but later had to spend a great deal of effort and time rebuilding?

There are far too many. Let me give a funny example of my own.

[Learning to Hold a Pen Correctly]

Because I learned to hold chopsticks correctly first, from childhood I treated the pen like a chopstick, resting it on my ring finger to write. I wrote that way all the way from kindergarten through my military service to my job at China Credit Information Service. My colleagues there were all highly educated, which stirred in me the idea of taking a graduate school entrance exam while working, and after work I went to master's prep classes on Nanyang Street. Because I had to write exam papers, and my grip was wrong, my fingers hurt after writing a lot and I couldn't write fast. So I decided then and there to retrain how I held the pen.

It took me nearly six months to relearn how to hold a pen and write, going from ugly handwriting back to neat handwriting, and I could quickly write a lot on open-ended graduate exam papers without strain. In exam season I took the entrance exams for five or six business graduate schools. My nice handwriting didn't get me into National Chengchi University or National Taiwan University, but to this day I'm glad I can write the correct way.

And later I used this same approach of tearing down and starting over to fix quite a few boring little things that bothered me. They didn't help my life much either, but it always feels more comfortable.
