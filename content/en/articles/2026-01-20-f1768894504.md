Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP46

#TourLeaderExamCountdown53Days

7. Which century's Buddhist cultural architecture and sculpture do Korea's Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple represent? (A) 5th century (B) 6th century (C) 7th century (D) 8th century

The correct answer is (D)

Solution: Korean chronology is rarely covered in Taiwan's compulsory education, and watching Korean dramas alone makes it hard to tell which period events take place in. So this kind of question is not easy to get right, and guessing C or B would be wrong. If I were writing the question, I would bring in the timing and course of Buddhism's transmission in China, so that candidates could reach a suitable and meaningful answer by inference. It is a pity that this question tests only memorised content, although these are Korean World Cultural Heritage sites and must be introduced when guiding tours.

#KoreanHistory in a nutshell, in a few key stages:

1. Prehistory and Gojoseon (legend to 108 BC)

Dangun founds the nation: according to legend, in 2333 BC Hwanung, son of the Heavenly Emperor, united with a woman who had been transformed from a bear and gave birth to Dangun, who founded "Gojoseon." In 108 BC, Emperor Wu of Han destroyed Gojoseon and set up four commanderies, including Lelang, in the north of the peninsula.

2. The Three Kingdoms period (57 BC - AD 668)

A period when heroes emerged and warlords divided the peninsula: #Goguryeo in the north, #Baekje in the southwest with frequent exchanges with Japan, and #Silla in the southeast, which allied with the Tang dynasty and came to rule the peninsula.

3. Unified Silla and the North-South States period (668 - 935)

Silla is the era of Bulguksa and Seokguram, when Buddhist art reached its peak.

4. The Goryeo dynasty (918 - 1392)

Goryeo is the source of the pronunciation of "Korea." Buddhism was the state religion and the culture was brilliant, with the world's first metal movable-type printing, celadon and more. In its history it suffered invasion by the Mongols (Yuan dynasty) and became the Yuan's Zhengdong Province and a "son-in-law state."

5. The Joseon dynasty (1392 - 1910)

This was the last feudal dynasty in Korean history, and the one that most deeply influences the modern day (Yi Joseon). Confucianism governed the state: Buddhism was abolished in favour of Confucianism, and a strict social hierarchy was established. King Sejong the Great created #Hunminjeongeum (the Hangul alphabet), and late in the dynasty a closed-country policy was pursued, after which Korea became a Japanese colony.

6. The Japanese occupation and modern division (1910 - present)

# Japanese rule (1910-1945): 35 years of colonial rule that stirred strong nationalism.

# North-South division (1945): After the end of World War II, the 38th parallel became the boundary, with US and Soviet troops stationed on either side.

# The Korean War (1950-1953): A tragic war between kinsmen that ended in a ceasefire, creating the division between north and south that continues to this day.

# The Korean Peninsula: divided into the Republic of Korea and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, two different systems.

# 1 In Korean history there was a period whose art was deeply influenced by the Chinese Tang dynasty, in which Buddhism was the state religion and culture reached its peak in the 8th century. Which period is this? (A) Gojoseon (B) Goguryeo (C) Unified Silla (D) Joseon dynasty

Answer: (C)

# 2 Regarding cultural exchange between the Tang dynasty and the Korean Peninsula, which of the following statements is wrong?

(A) Silla and the Tang formed the "Silla-Tang Allied Forces," which destroyed Baekje and then Goguryeo in turn

(B) Silla sent large numbers of "students in Tang" and "scholar monks" to Chang'an to absorb Tang laws and Buddhist culture

(C) The famous Tang esoteric Buddhism (Tangmi) was introduced and directly influenced the sculptural art of buildings such as Seokguram

(D) To maintain friendly relations, the Tang dynasty introduced the Korean alphabet (Hunminjeongeum) into China as a frontier trade script

Answer: (D)

# 3 Silla's unique "bone-rank system" had a far-reaching influence on society at the time. Which of the following statements is correct?

(A) The bone-rank system was a merit system in which status was promoted according to personal achievements

(B) Only people of "sacred bone" or "true bone" bloodlines were qualified to inherit the throne or hold high office

(C) The bone-rank system was abolished by King Sejong the Great in the Joseon dynasty and replaced by the civil service examination

(D) Bone rank only determined political status and did not affect the size of one's residence, the colour of one's clothing or the details of daily life

Answer: (B). The bone-rank system was a strict "bloodline class system," not a merit system. It determined not only the level of office but also strictly regulated food, clothing, housing and travel. The system disappeared with the fall of Silla, and was not abolished only in the Joseon dynasty.
