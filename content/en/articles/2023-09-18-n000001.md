Haruki Murakami: I always thought people grow old gradually. In fact, they don't. People grow old in an instant.

**2024/1/19**

A note for the record: on 1/11 I went back to Lemei and spent an hour having photos taken. Visually, the photos show a lot of improvement. If I treat my scalp the right way, maybe it will reward me.

**2023/12/10**

In class last Tuesday a student casually asked, "Teacher, why don't you get a hair transplant?" The room froze instantly. I stammered, and the student was even more at a loss. A kind student did their best to squeeze out a few comforting words, something like "But you're the most handsome teacher in the department!" In truth my head was buzzing, and I didn't hear anything after that. A few days have passed and I have calmed down a little. I would like to say sorry to that student: this is my own problem, and I put you in such an awkward spot.

Of course, I have also done a lot of reading these past few days. The truth is I have been working on this for a long time, but it is not like the TOEIC, where your score naturally rises if you study hard and practise. A person is constantly moving towards old age. Whether time can be stopped and hair made to grow again is, in my case at least, still a question mark. My family has been helping me look for products too, like this strange glowing cap endorsed by quite a few celebrities. The 2023 Christmas offer is NT$22,800 (basic) or NT$31,800 (professional). It looks fun, it just glows red, and there is plenty of discussion and recommendation on Dcard. I will probably buy one and try it.

**2023/9/18**

The alarming photos above were taken at the Lemei hair-care centre on Zhongxiao East Road. Before I walked in I had already prepared myself: this was a process I had to get through. There were two things I had to face. First, in 360-degree photos there is no hiding how much hair I have really lost. Second, I was certainly going to have to pay a fair amount. The whole procedure was much like what beauty influencers share online, except that the centre itself looks rather dated. The process is the same, though. The trial session cost NT$1,600, but a six-month course plus products came to NT$56,520. Before leaving, I steeled myself and swiped my card.

I decided to sell one lot of Foxconn shares to pay for the trial. The money I have lost by leaving it in the stock market for years is already far more than this, and I never felt any benefit from it. At least a hair-care course is also a kind of relaxation for body and mind. Compared with what I lost in the stock market, or in the recent scheme that took money from my family, this spending is actually a pleasure. Having someone wash my hair properly and using good products to care for it means far more than the time and wealth I have unknowingly wasted on money games. (Postscript: in the end I paid with dividends and my flexible savings instead.)

Writing this far, I feel much more at peace. I have often felt that I don't deserve things, that I don't deserve a good life, and only now, bald and paunchy, do I realise that what I wasted in my youth was the most precious time of all: the years when I was young.
