---
title: "Starting Over in the Teaching World, Approaching Fifty"
excerpt: "Reflections on my work principles before and after becoming a teacher, and why I feel I must build my next ability as I approach fifty."
published: true
---

Before I became a teacher, my work principle was that I must stay at any job for more than a year, and within the same job I should change units at least once a year to keep jumping out of my comfort zone. Besides, if I could take a side job alongside my full-time one (now called a "slash career"), I would try my best within my ability and time. Success or failure is judged by pay; heroes come from the young.

After becoming a teacher, my previous work experience and years of labour service were all reset to zero. In the education environment, seniority and age order matter, so I started again. At work, how long you have been there is the basis, and how close you are to retirement is what gets talked about. What matters most is whether you get promoted, and the work is similar and repetitive year after year. As for the students I teach, I hear every year that they are worse than the year before (or perhaps I am teaching worse every year).

So I have been trying to start again within the teaching environment. In 2021 I obtained the ministry-certified associate professor title, and my years of service will soon reach 15. I feel my teaching ability declines year by year, and both students and schools are nearly becoming history.

Approaching fifty, I must build another ability, and only then will I have a chance to rise again.
