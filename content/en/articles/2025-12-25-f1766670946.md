Tour Guide & Tour Leader Exam Prep EP20

#TourLeaderExam79DaysToGo

20 When was the north-south trunk railway on Taiwan's west coast completed?

① During Liu Ming-chuan's governance of Taiwan ② During the Japanese colonial period ③ During Shao Yu-lien's governance of Taiwan ④ After the government relocated to Taiwan

#2025MandarinTourGuideExamPastQuestion

Explanation

The completion of the west-coast north-south trunk railway took place mainly during the Japanese era. For military and economic governance, the rulers, led by Gotō Shinpei, civil administrator of the Taiwan Governor-General's Office, and chief engineer Hasegawa Kinsuke, greatly revised the old Qing-era line and extended it southward. Most unusually, construction started at the north and south ends simultaneously, and on 20 April 1908 the whole trunk line finally joined up in Taichung.

To show how much importance was attached to running Taiwan, a "Trunk Railway Completion Ceremony" was held in Taichung Park. Why was Taichung chosen for the ceremony? It was highly symbolic. First, it is the midpoint where north and south meet on the trunk line. Also, to welcome Prince Kan'in Kotohito, the Governor-General's Office built the "Lakeside Pavilion" (then called the "Twin Pavilions") in the pond of Taichung Park as a resting place. That building is still the emblem of Taichung City today, a concrete witness to this railway history. The imperial family travelled by rail to inspect and preside over the ceremony, symbolising that the Empire of Japan's actual control over the island's territory had reached every corner through the rails.

For Taiwan itself, the completion of the west-coast trunk railway also represented a social transformation brought by a "spatial revolution". Once the whole north-south line opened, travel time was cut to a dozen or so hours, and Taiwan for the first time had an "island-wide" sense of integrated time and space. At the same time, cities along the transport arteries (such as Taichung, Hsinchu and Chiayi) developed rapidly, while port cities that originally relied on water transport (such as Lukang and Yuejin) declined instead.

In this way, once the trunk line was completed, sugar from southern Taiwan could quickly be shipped to northern ports for export, bringing the "Sugar Gold era". The railway became the blood vessel through which the colonial government extracted resources, transported troops and governed.
