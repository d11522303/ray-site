title: "100 Straight Weeks of Running: Notes on My Apple Watch"
excerpt: "After 100 consecutive weeks of running with my Apple Watch, here are eight honest notes on what works, what doesn't, and why it's so expensive."
published: true
---

100 consecutive weeks of running means this Apple Watch is almost 2 years old. Here are some notes on it:

1. I mainly use the AW to tell the time and record runs. The Nike app can remind me of the time and record runs, but it can't measure cadence. Since I run for health and habit, I don't mind.

2. Syncing a podcast before a run and wearing bone-conduction headphones lets me listen to good radio programmes or interviews while running. I highly recommend this.

3. The AW works as an offline credit card for payments, which is very convenient. You can go out without carrying money.

4. The AW can't work as an EasyCard, so it can't be used to borrow a UBike (I tried many times without success; maybe I'm wrong).

5. The AW lets you check your heart rate and health indicators at any time. But I don't wear the watch to sleep, so I can't track my sleep.

6. I've used it for swimming a few times. Waterproofing is no problem, and in an indoor pool it can record short laps, because with 25-metre lengths I often lose count of how many I've swum.

7. The battery lasts only one day before it needs charging, which is a hassle for trips since you have to carry a charging cable. If I run a full marathon, will it run out of power? This year I could try it at a race.

8. The biggest drawback is that one watch costs over NT$10,000, which I find very expensive.
