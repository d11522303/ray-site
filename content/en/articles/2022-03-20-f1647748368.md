[P&O Ferries Dismissed Many Staff, and It Is Not P&O Cruises]

The Taiwanese international news site The News Lens ran an article, "P&O Cruises Fires 800 Employees by Video Without Warning, Sparking Backlash" (original in the comments). It reported that, because of the pandemic, 800 employees working for P&O Ferries were summarily dismissed, and since this happened in the UK, a country founded on workers' rights, it caused no small controversy.

The UK-born P&O is one of the oldest shipping companies in the world, with the full name The Peninsular & Oriental Steam Navigation Company. Since its founding in 1837, through complicated mergers and business developments, its three brands, P&O Ferries, P&O Maritime and P&O Heritage, are now operated by DP World of Dubai. They are the protagonists of this news event, so the P&O ferry in the picture is the large ferry that normally serves Britain, Ireland and continental Europe, a shipping company that belongs to ordinary transport and daily-life services.

P&O Cruises, the cruise line, is owned by the American Carnival Corporation and is a sister company of Princess Cruises, which most people know. As the global pandemic crisis eases, it has now resumed normal cruise services. On its official website you can see that sailings for April to June are already on sale. One example is the 8-day Norwegian Fjords cruise on Iona departing on 9 April 2022, at EUR 549 to 1,349 per person, which I honestly think is quite good (posted in the comments). The newest ship, Iona, also has a very elegant and comfortable interior, a completely Scandinavian style that suits me perfectly, and it is P&O Cruises' first mega-ship powered by natural gas.
