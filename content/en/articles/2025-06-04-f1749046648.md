2025.6.4

Only after the graduation ceremony could I properly sort through the photos, look at them closely, and slowly remember.

I used to think:

Students coming and going is the everyday routine of a teaching career,

and the teacher standing at the front of the lecture hall is only a passer-by in a student's life.

This was my original understanding when I took this job. Only after nearly twenty years of teaching did I slowly realise that being a teacher is not an occupation but a key role on the stage of both students' and teachers' lives.

How light or heavy it is depends on how much you identify with and commit to the role. When you play it wholeheartedly, it gets written into your life without your noticing, becoming one imprint after another, shaping the students and, even more, shaping yourself.

I am deeply grateful for the chance to become a teacher. I thank the senior colleagues and supervisors who guided me into the profession, and even more Taiwan Hospitality & Tourism College (THT) and Hsing Wu University (HWU), which built the stage for my teaching. An actor without a stage is rootless duckweed. Someone not very suited to being a teacher was given the chance to hone and deepen his understanding of life in the field of education, and the chance to know thousands of students. A school year, a semester, a course, a class, a report, a competition, a one-off rehearsal, each is a chance for teachers and students to connect.

In truth, such complicated feelings will slowly fade with time. I want to record in words the rich emotion of this very moment, because at this moment I was a teacher giving everything I had.
