23 Which three of the following countries became "French Indochina" in the 19th century?

① Myanmar, Vietnam, Kalimantan

② Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos

③ Vietnam, Laos, Brunei

④ Vietnam, Sarawak, Brunei

Explanation:

The urban structure, architectural style and historical role of Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City are both representative cases formed under the "French Indochina" colonial system. Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City represent the different historical paths of northern and southern Vietnam.

Hanoi was originally the political centre of Vietnam's traditional dynasties. After French colonisation it became the administrative capital of the Indochinese Union, and gradually developed a dual structure in which the Old Quarter and the French Quarter coexist. Tree-lined boulevards, the opera house and colonial government offices are interwoven with traditional streets, markets and temples, showing Vietnam's historical turn from a feudal dynasty to a modern nation.

By comparison, Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Saigon) was planned under French rule as the colonial economic and trade hub of the south, and its port construction and modernised districts earned it the name "Paris of the Orient". French-style post offices, churches and wide streets reflect the profound way colonial rule shaped urban space. The Vietnam War and the process of reunification in the 20th century added memories of war and national narrative to Ho Chi Minh City. To this day, Vietnam's two major cities have kept their colonial-era cityscapes within modern development, an important clue for travellers trying to understand Vietnam's colonial history, and the bread and coffee in Vietnamese cuisine also come from that colonial past.
