2025.4.13

A spring-day exam at National Taipei University of Technology was very pleasant, and sitting in the front row gave me peace of mind.

The only answer I changed on the whole paper was changing a 45%-correct answer to a wrong one, and it was exactly the question my child and I discussed last night while preparing: rice cultivation accounts for 12% of global CH4 (methane) greenhouse gas emissions, and livestock farming for another 33%. In my notes I had even written that I should understand why rice emits methane.

When I got home and looked up the material, I learned it is due to the rice plant's own characteristics: whether the carbon comes from soil or fertiliser, during the plant's respiration it is taken up from the soil through the opening and closing of pores and then released into the atmosphere, so the CH4 comes from emissions of the plant itself rather than from the soil (Ko Chia-yin, 2012).

https://www.delta-foundation.org.tw/blogdetail/2301

Of course I hope I pass the licensing exam so that the time and energy I invested yield a result, but I still have to wait for the outcome. The next step is to work hard on the TOEIC in mid-year, hoping to reach the 800-point mark in 2025.
