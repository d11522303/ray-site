[The Inclusive Classroom]

So much happened this past week that I almost couldn't get my head straight. Let me share an ordinary classroom moment. One day I had a packed four-period 8 am class of 150 students. Apart from the ones who arrived on time, the latecomers fell into two types: those who came in through the front door and those who came in through the back.

The front-door ones talked loudly...

Student: Teacher, I'm here for class. There was a traffic jam so I couldn't make it on time.

Teacher: OK, have a seat, and remember to take a worksheet to fill in.

Student: Teacher, I didn't bring a pen.

Teacher: I've got one. Remember to write your name first.

Student: Teacher, I have XX disorder, and then talks to himself for a minute #$%^&*

The back-door ones slipped in quietly...

Teacher: I notice several students in black clothes, baggy trousers and shiny chains walking in together and sitting at the very back.

Teacher: Come up and take a worksheet to fill in.

Student: Asks the classmate beside him to go up and get it, then leaves it to one side.

Teacher: Remember to write your name first and upload it to the course system at the end of class.

Student: No reaction. The phone in his hand stays sideways and his eyes never leave the screen.

Because it was an 8 am general-education class on the ground floor, the school had specially arranged for some students to sit at the very front, and they interacted with me in all kinds of random ways. So I played the fool along with them while still getting through the whole day's teaching plan, with a group at the back playing mobile games. I got through the morning in an atmosphere that was both infuriating and funny. Thank you to the hundred or so students who stayed awake and were willing to interact with me, fill in the worksheet and take notes. You caught your teacher in this class, and made my preparation worthwhile.
