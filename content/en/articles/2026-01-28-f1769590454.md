Before the Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exams, EP54

#45DaysLeftToTheTourLeaderExam

20 The grid-pattern streets of Kyoto, Japan, are generally believed to be the result of imitating the design of ancient Chinese capitals. Which of the following was the earliest Japanese city to imitate the design of a Chinese capital? (A) Heijō-kyō (B) Heian-kyō (C) Fujiwara-kyō (D) Nagaoka-kyō

The answer is (C) Fujiwara-kyō.

Fujiwara-kyō (690-710) was the first capital in Japanese history truly built in imitation of Tang-dynasty capital planning, using a checkerboard grid of streets (the jōbō system). Although Heijō-kyō (710) and Heian-kyō (794) are more famous and larger in scale, Fujiwara-kyō was the earliest city to begin this design style.

Japan's sixth-grade social studies textbook does not mention Fujiwara-kyō. The important Tang-style city planning is traced from Heijō-kyō (today's Nara) to Heian-kyō (today's Kyoto), and the textbook also compares them against the scale of Chang'an, showing the differences in size between the two capitals very concretely. If you are interested, take a close look.

Also, in the lower-right picture of the textbook there is a mention of "Jianzhen". In my cruise course I shared with students that there are two scheduled sailings between Shanghai and Kobe/Osaka, and one of the ships is named "Shin Ganjin" (New Jianzhen).

The monk Jianzhen was an eminent Tang-dynasty monk and physician, and the founder of the Risshū (Vinaya) school of Japanese Buddhism. At the invitation of Japanese monks, over 12 years and with 6 planned attempts to cross eastward to Japan, he never changed his original purpose even after going blind, and finally succeeded on the sixth attempt in reaching Japan, spreading the Dharma and the culture of the flourishing Tang. Jianzhen not only regulated Japanese Buddhist precepts but also introduced knowledge of architecture, sculpture and medicine, so he holds a representative place in Japanese history.

Here are two questions to practise!

#1 The Tang-dynasty monk Jianzhen was invited to go east to Japan to teach the Dharma. Through many failures, obstruction by officials and storms, he finally reached Japan. Which school did he found in Nara?

(A) Zen (B) Ritsu (Vinaya) (C) Pure Land (D) Tendai

Answer: (B) Ritsu (Vinaya) school

#2 When leading a tour group to Nara, Japan, if you want to take members to see a monument personally supervised in construction by Jianzhen and now one of the UNESCO World Heritage "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara", where should you go? (A) Tōdai-ji (B) Kinkaku-ji (C) Tōshōdai-ji (D) Kiyomizu-dera

Answer: (C) Tōshōdai-ji
