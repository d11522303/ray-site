title: "Designing a Real-World Travel Game Lesson Plan"
excerpt: "Designing real-world games for tour guiding classes is exhausting, but a fair, app-based, shared-experience lesson plan keeps students engaged."
published: true
---

2025.5.7

In class I always have to design activities that let students experience the real-world games a tour guide has to lead. The process is actually very tiring. When I explain things in the classroom with text or video, few students are really focused on understanding. When I ask everyone to go out of the classroom and experience an activity, I have to urge them again and again, and some reactions make me feel that I am interrupting their mobile game time.

So the lesson plan has to be carried out in order:

1. Take turns in groups, watching closely for fairness so that no one does nothing.

2. Use a phone app so that activity photos are uploaded in sync, with instant scoring and bonus points for likes. Students who don't take part and don't upload photos get no points.

3. Share and have fun afterwards to build a shared feeling.

So people who didn't come to class don't know what we did. Of course some students upload other people's photos. I don't catch them in the public domain, but I can send the student a private message to show I care.

In class like this the students all find it very interesting. It is just that the teacher who designs the activity pays out of pocket for teaching aids and arranges a plan mixing the virtual and the real for just 2 periods, and after getting through the 2 periods, by the time I speak with students again I have no voice left.
