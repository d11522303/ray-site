▋People who can see it but dare not move forward

This phrase comes from Professor Chung-Hsien Lee's observations on running marathons. I want to borrow it to explain a teacher's daily life on the last Friday of March.

At the end of March there are several deadlines and headcounts:

1. The deadline for the MOE "Dreams Come True" overseas internship programme (4 students).

2. The deadline for registration for the in-school OPC and cruise certification coaching classes (26 students, 50 students).

3. The deadline for ground-staff résumés for UNI Air's Nangan and Beigan airports (1 student).

4. The deadline for résumés to Cola, Lion, Orient, Wufu, Giant and Evergreen travel agencies (12 students, recruiting).

5. The deadline for a submission to a journal (1 person, procrastinating).

I'll also record the LINE messages this week related to students' internships:

"Teacher, I'm very lost. I see my classmates all preparing résumés, but I don't know which company to choose, and I even want to give up";

"Teacher, I only want to intern at an airline, and if I can't, then forget it";

"Teacher, what do travel agencies actually do? I don't like being a tour leader, because I only want to sit in an office"

"Teacher, if I don't submit my résumé by the deadline, will I have no chance at all?"

"Hello Teacher, I wrote a personal statement. Can you help me revise it?"

"Hello Teacher, I have sent my résumé. Please check that you've received it. Thank you, Teacher"

"Teacher, I'm not going to intern. Teacher Kora asked me to let you know"

"Teacher, I want to apply to Wufu and Orient. Is it fine to just use 104 for both, or do the two companies each have specific information to fill in?"

"Hello Teacher, I have sent my résumé. Please check that you've received it, thank you Teacher 👍"

"Hello Teacher, sorry to bother you. I'd like to ask you for the group chat for that cruise job opening"

"Teacher, I'm changing my preferences to Lion, Cola and Orient. I'm still at OO at the moment and can't get back to school this week"

————Nearly a hundred repeated messages like the above——————

▋Understand it from the students' future perspective

▋Think about it from the teacher's current vantage point

The phenomenon in the education setting is: "Candy is placed right in front of them, so why don't students make a move?" Many people reply, "Students today don't cherish what they have. The candy is right there and they won't even pick it up. They're never satisfied!" There is nothing wrong with this view, but it is the view of a bystander.

Whether students become people who can see it but dare not move forward depends on the teacher's catalysing and cognitive work with students before the deadline. It is like a course: if a teacher sets the midterm and final exams, puts the teaching materials online, will students actively read, understand, and even obediently come to take the exam? The answer is that most will not. During a course, the teacher needs instructional design and staged assessment, using many teaching techniques and material designs to encourage students to prepare, so that step by step they arrive at the exam day prepared.

A teacher's work is a combination of relationships among many people, matters and things. You can choose to keep it simple, just teaching and leaving class with fewer connections and doing your own thing; or you can choose to make it more complicated, in which case there are very many things you can do and you will connect many relationships, and you'll be chased by deadlines every month, especially someone like me with a serious procrastination habit, and I even get anxious! About eighty per cent of the matters above relate to students. In today's teaching environment, these are all encouragement-type programmes, so teachers must take the initiative to apply for them and students must take the initiative to participate. If teachers don't write proposals and don't offer much guidance, students have no way to take part and to experience the beauty of effort.

▋If teachers do nothing, then for students "the deadline is the giving-up day".

After a busy and draining Friday of student affairs, I'm up early on the weekend typing this rambling post, while in another window a journal article is half revised, and in 3 more days it will be my own giving-up day.
