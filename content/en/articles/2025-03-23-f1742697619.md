2025.3.23

I got home from NTHU in the early hours of the next day.

I took some food and supplies to Hsinchu to see my son. Yesterday was NTHU's spring career fair. He stayed on campus to study, and went with his hard-working classmates to look at the booths. They had wanted to hear from MediaTek and Novatek, but heard the booths had too many people, and as Realtek next door happened to have nobody, the three freshmen went over to ask questions.

The person they talked to was Realtek's HR manager, a National Taiwan University electrical engineering high achiever. My son said he was about my age. From a very detailed and realistic perspective, he talked with the three first-year electrical engineering students about careers and development in IC design. At dinner, my son talked to us about what the work at Realtek involves, and said he was a little unhappy, because the working atmosphere there was not what he had hoped for. Recently he has been wondering whether he chose the wrong major and direction. It seems that this is the ultimate goal of electrical engineering in Taiwan; should he consider studying abroad, and so on?

That night we chose a Korean barbecue restaurant on Nanda Road in Hsinchu. It was busy, and the food and setting were good, but it only accepted cash or bank transfers. At checkout I used an app to transfer nearly 2K from an account that only had 3K left, and I still wondered whether it was my fault that my child worries so much about the future and money, and cannot enjoy life properly at university.

So what did the Realtek manager talk about with the three of them?

First, he said they should work hard at their studies and compete for academic awards, because when they are hired the company looks not only at the department they graduated from but also at whether their school grades are at the top. The reason is simple: it is a very competitive environment. He gave the example of designing Apple's next-generation phone chip. Apple sends out a call to the world's major IC design companies for its product development, sets out the specifications, and then well-known IC design teams around the world compete to see which team can finish a design to the specifications in the shortest time and win the project. So in this global contest of brainpower and speed, only the strongest and most outstanding human resources have a chance to win.

Second, he mentioned the "responsibility system" of working. The manager said it is an attitude: treat the company's business as your own. You cannot think in terms of working hours (such as 8 hours a day) for assigned design work. The three students asked what a working day is, and the manager said at least 14 hours, stressing again the importance of the responsibility system, and that they should put the development of the company and of Taiwan's chip industry into their outlook on life; only then will they have the right attitude to stay on at the company. But the manager also mentioned that because salaries have risen steadily in recent years, many young employees who come to the company do not accept the responsibility system, so the company will move towards an hourly system in the future, with extra hours paid as overtime.

I don't quite dare to put the above on the internet, because a few days ago, when students from my department went for a sales interview at Dongnan Travel, the sales manager, a graduate of a master's programme in life sciences at National Taiwan University, described similar ideas to the students. I can feel the huge differences that young people from different learning backgrounds have about the responsibility system versus the hourly system in career development and outlook on life. This is something that cannot be put online for public trial. I once told students that if the company pays enough, there is nothing I would not do, and the look in the students' eyes seemed noncommittal.

Finally, the manager also mentioned that the development of IC design for electrical engineering is uncertain, and that being good right now does not mean good prospects in future. He suggested that the three students also double-major in related fields, such as software design in computer science or materials engineering. My son asked another question: would taking part in club and service activities at university, and adding cross-disciplinary experience and leadership skills, help with getting hired? The HR person's answer was ruthless: no, because the most important thing for them is to maintain the global leading-edge competitiveness of Taiwan's IC design, and those things don't sound as if they help the company win the competition, so they don't care much about them.

I blame myself a little for not having the connections to give high school students more opportunity to learn about this when my child chose a high school track. Once you choose information and electrical engineering, you are set on your path in life. However much learning-portfolio work and all-round development you do, in these high-paying, high-pressure working environments none of it compares with a super-powered IQ and a competitive mindset.

My income can only keep the family fed and clothed. On a weekend when NTHU is full of students who stayed on campus, finding a nice restaurant and chatting with my wife and son about all the big and small matters of study and life is the greatest happiness in my life. I am very grateful, and I encourage my son to keep his curiosity about society and the world, and not to let income or grades decide what kind of person he becomes. His mother and I will always support him.

When I transferred the money on my phone, I looked at my empty e-wallet, and I think I feel even sadder and more lost than my son does.
