2025.7.1 (added later)

My child took part in the summer activity of the National Tsing Hua University Indigenous Culture Club at Xinguang Tribe. Seeing a young face like my own experiencing a brand-new life made me feel deeply satisfied.

Teacher Baoni of Jianguo High School said that the first half of the year is only really over after midnight on 7/1. I think that applies to people who look forward to the sense of time. I believe that many elders, who have lived many years, experience time differently from when they were middle-aged, and always feel that a day with nothing done is suddenly dark. Day after day you feel life slipping away.

In fact it is not only silver-haired elders. After a day ends, many people perhaps cannot recall how the day passed at all, or even remember what they ate for the three meals.

So I have a method to get back the time that God has taken away.

The method is simple:

1. Give every day a sense of ritual: design a look for yourself for the day, and name the day for yourself with a "** Day".

For example, 7/2 is my lunch day with Teacher Kang, when we will look back on our eight or nine years of cooperation and effort, and eat lunch together in our secret base at school.

2. Schedule the important task of the day, fixing the time at 10 to 11, and start punctually at ten, focusing on this matter. While doing it you are doing the thing, and after finishing a sense of accomplishment wells up. When you rest, drink tea and eat a snack, recall the activity or event you just completed, deepening the meaning of this moment. Over the day you will complete several things, and the overall sense of time slows down, becoming a day full of memorable points.

3. Practise the daily arrangement repeatedly. There is no need to make a yearly or monthly plan first. Instead, arrange activities day by day, turning long-term tasks into small daily activities, and making this a rhythm of life.
