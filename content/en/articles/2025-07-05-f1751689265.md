Gold Scams

Gold scams in the news are frightening, and they remind me of a Grimms' fairy tale I read as a child, "Hans in Luck". Have you read it?

----------The Brothers Grimm-----------

Once upon a time there was a man named Hans.

Hans had served his master for seven years and decided to leave: "Master, my time is up. Now I want to go home; please give me my wages."

The master replied: "You have served me faithfully, and I have decided to treat you well."

With that, he gave him a lump of gold as big as Hans's head.

Hans had gold, and felt very happy at that moment. He kept thinking about what to exchange it for.

But in fact the gold did not make Hans happy. On the way home, he was constantly reminded that he would lack some things on the road, and so he was persuaded by many people to make several swaps:

He swapped the gold for a horse,

the horse for a cow,

the cow for a pig,

the pig for a goose,

and the goose for a grindstone and an ordinary field stone.

With each trade, he felt his wishes had come true. At that moment he felt like a child living every day as a Sunday, favoured by the goddess of luck.

Hans told himself: How lucky I am!

One day, when he was about to drink some water, the two heavy stones fell into the well.

Suddenly he was finally glad that he no longer had to carry the heavy stones.

Then he sighed: No one under the sun is luckier than I am.

Light-hearted, relieved of every burden, he set off on the way home.

----------The End of the Fairy Tale-----------

You must think Hans is foolish. If it were you, you would first have the gold appraised and find someone to exchange it for its equivalent in money, so that with money in hand you could buy anything! Any wish could have a chance of coming true!

That's right. When you have money in hand and people know it, there are scams all along the road. First they ask for your gold, then for your horse, your cow, your pig, your goose, leading you step by step into the pit with your endless desires! In the end you are left with two useless stones, and you still think you are happy!
