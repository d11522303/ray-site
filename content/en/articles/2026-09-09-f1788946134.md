---
title: "The Street Leading Out of This Station Goes All the Way to the Guandu Temple Square"
excerpt: "Ray remembers watching trains as a three-year-old at Guandu Station, and how coal soot from a steam locomotive left his left eye permanently weak."
published: true
---

The street leading out of this station goes all the way down to the Guandu Temple square.

When I was little I often came here alone to watch the trains and the people coming and going at the market stalls on both sides of the street. I remember many of them selling duck eggs. A three-year-old's world had trains, a station, the grown-ups selling tickets, and the crowds of pilgrims with bamboo baskets heading to and from Guandu Temple. Every day was so interesting and exciting.

But my left eye has very severe amblyopia. It is because I sat in front of Guandu Station every day waiting for trains. Once, a steam train just out of the tunnel puffed out coal smoke and a piece of coal cinder got into my left eye. I don't remember whether it hurt, or whether I went to see a doctor. Medical conditions were poor then, and I think my family's situation at the time was simply to let the eye heal on its own. As a result, my left eye has only ever had about 0.3 vision, and a black speck of coal cinder is still left in the white at the back of the eye, where I can't see it.

When I did my military physical, the difference in vision was so large that I might not have met the standard for enlistment. When they measured vision for the conscripts, I secretly cheated by reading the eye chart with my right eye so I could get in smoothly!
