[Mid-Career Change and Silver-Age Reskilling: A Plumbing and Electrical Degree Programme at Technological Universities]

I think a plumbing and electrical engineering department holds little appeal for young students today. But for those of us in our fifties and sixties who face the risk of unemployment yet still have the physical strength, or for older brothers and sisters who retired early and draw monthly pensions, if such a programme were offered on weekends or in continuing education, taught by teachers with professional technical skills, and combined with preparation for certificates from the Ministry of Labor or official trade associations, I would want to spend four years learning the trade.

The German-style education system and approach that @[100003046621143:2048:李忠憲] proposed is very suitable as a direction for professional transformation of technological universities. Many technological universities have received large Higher Education Sprout Project grants. For example, a certain technological university where many staff have just lost their jobs still received more than NT$60 million a year in 2023. Perhaps the project indicators are all ESG items that can't find a landing point. If this annual NT$5 billion-plus programme could drop the pretty regional revitalisation activities and research-output indicators, and instead be used to increase the number of professional certificates held by middle-aged people and the employment capacity and participation rate of those aged 50 to 70, it might be a chance for technological universities to return to vocational expertise and shed their reputation for flashy but empty thesis indicators.

Retirees in Taiwan keep eating, drinking and travelling around. Although that helps the weekday group business of our tourism industry, it contributes rather little to personal growth and social good. This is especially true for those whose monthly pensions are higher than the NT$27K that young people earn. If the system could require, through a credit-point scheme, that people drawing monthly pensions return to campus to learn skills and earn certificates, they could meet more good friends in class, and the skills they learn could be used to repair their own homes and maintain their neighbourhoods. How wonderful it would be to benefit on three fronts: practical life skills, mutual respect between self and community, and personal health.
