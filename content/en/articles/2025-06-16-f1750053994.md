2025.6.16

A few days ago the college office held a retirement recognition ceremony, giving the professors a little time to share their thoughts. Several used their staff ID numbers to say they had worked for 35 or 36 years and had finally reached the day of mandatory retirement. Most professors at the university start teaching when they graduate with a master's or doctorate, so many were nearly 30 when they first took up the post. Doing one job to the very end and then retiring is truly blessed and fulfilling. I sincerely congratulate them!

But I did odd jobs in factories from middle school, and only got my first labour insurance at 20. I have worked about 33 years, but after changing jobs N times, I know I will probably have no chance to stand up and share retirement thoughts. The reason is obvious: at private schools with a falling birth rate there are no students, and if one is forced out one must change careers. So the most important thing is to love every present moment, cherish each day with an income, and value every student who comes to study. Work is the practice of life, until the very last moment.

My biggest takeaway is that I did not look for a high-paying job when I started my career, only a job I liked, and I did my specialty well. But if in life you can rely only on yourself, then loving your specialty and interests may be the wrong approach.

Now, as people choose university majors, I sincerely feel that the department you study should have a future, and a good income (錢, "money", a pun on 前, "future").
