Bangkok Trip Day 7: An Unforgettable Travel Scare on the Final Day

The last day of the trip was all about strolling near the BTS stations. We had a leisurely hotel breakfast that turned into brunch, casually picked up the laundry we had sent nearby so there would be less to pack on the way home, and spent the whole day at a relaxed sightseeing pace, thinking back over the week's travels and getting ready to spend our remaining Thai baht before heading home.

Right at nine o'clock that night, just as I put down the 300-baht massage my family had arranged, the biggest scare of the trip appeared. When my child was about to go to the hotel gym to work out, he discovered his AirPods weren't with him. Looking at his phone, he saw a notification that the earbuds had left his side long ago, but he had taken it for just one of a huge pile of messages and hadn't noticed. Counting back, it was already six hours earlier, and the last signal came from between BTS stations E4 and E5. He couldn't remember where the AirPods had gone.

Fortunately, the calmest person in our family was right beside him. His mum suggested he might have dropped them at the burger place at lunch. At half past nine, two family members rushed, half walking and half running, to the counter just before the department store closed and sternly asked about the earbuds. The earbuds hadn't just been dropped; a Shake Shack employee had even changed the account and kept them. I felt that my family, speaking clearly in English to describe the lost item, made it sound as if most of the staff on site knew about it. The staff even went back into the kitchen to discuss it before deciding to return the earbuds, so just before the department store closed, we got the AirPods back. But when reconnecting them, we found the name had been changed to Thai, with the name Sandy added.

Everything above was relayed by my family, because I had returned to the hotel feeling relaxed after the massage and had no idea about all that happened in that hour. After this incident we all agreed that no matter how careful you are along the way, accidents will still happen. When travelling, always keep tolerance for mistakes and a sense of risk, keep some money and split belongings between people. All we can do is do good deeds, give more and prepare more, so that peace stays with our family.

***************************************

Let me also record another surprise I was part of. After dinner today, while having dessert, in the giant city of Bangkok with 20 million residents and millions of tourists, my colleague Kong and I somehow ran into each other at the same ice cream stall. It was at the Terminal 21 food court, the most "guava" (everyday, unpretentious) spot in Bangkok. Anyone who has been there knows that even family members travelling together can get lost in that plaza. And in fact we had already eaten our fill at the boat noodle restaurant and just wanted to order ice cream to cool off, when we bumped into our colleague Kong and his family, who were also travelling in Thailand to visit relatives.

The bonds between people are truly remarkable. Later, my family also mentioned on the way back that in Bangkok you can meet all kinds of travellers from around the world, which already feels unreal enough, and yet we could bump into a colleague from our own department office among millions of tourists. Perhaps in a previous life we had already met somewhere along a trade route or journey. We are all travellers through time and space who love travel and love freedom.
