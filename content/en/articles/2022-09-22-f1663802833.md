"Between Two Worlds": Social Immobility in Cruise Ship Work

Most vocational university students go straight to part-time jobs after class. The long hours and low pay of restaurant and convenience-store work are a vicious cycle that is hard to bear.

I really sympathise with how students juggle school and work. When I was in the "B-track" class in junior high, there were no remedial classes in the summer, so my family arranged for me to test circuit boards at a home workshop under Yongfu Bridge. In high school, while classmates went to cram schools near Taipei Main Station for physics, chemistry and English after class, I would rush off in my school uniform to a bowling alley on Zhulin Road in Yonghe to make coffee and carry plates.

I feel quite embarrassed thinking about it now. In fact, I think I only lasted a few months each time before I was too exhausted to continue, because I simply could not withstand the double pressure of long part-time hours and the intense schoolwork of high school.

These past few days the online debate over educational qualifications has been fierce, with everyone holding their own position. But I often wonder: if I had had the resources and time back then, would I have done better? Not necessarily, because success and failure cannot be guaranteed by one's educational or work background at any single moment.

One year, when I ran a cruise teaching programme and student study camp, I met a housekeeping cleaner at sea who was an industry-academia cooperation student from a tourism college. After having the chance to talk more and learn about their pay and working conditions, I came away with a very different understanding and impression of cruise travel.

I recommend everyone watch #BetweenTwoWorlds (Ouistreham), starring the famed French actress. She plays a writer who goes undercover on a ferry service and joins the cleaning crew doing housekeeping. Because of recruitment without borders, such jobs are often the only option for migrants and migrant workers. Beyond the complex shock of life experience and of cooperative-education employees, you feel the helplessness of how hard it is to move between the layers of an unpredictable social hierarchy.

If you have the chance to study with a focused mind and enjoy campus and club life, remember to go back and thank your family.
