title: "Moving to Hualien: Lotus Building"
excerpt: "Ray recalls renting a sea-and-mountain-view flat in Hualien, the realities of young family life there, and why moving to the countryside is harder than it looks."
published: true
---
<p>After living in Taipei for over thirty years, I suddenly decided, because of a job change, to move to Hualien, an island within the island. Naively, all I wanted was a home with a view of both mountains and sea. This was an apartment building on the border of Hualien City and Jian Township. From the windows you could see the Pacific Ocean, from the corridor you could see the Central Mountain Range, the nearby hill on the left was Liyu Mountain, and behind it was Liyu Lake, Hualien's largest inland lake. Two bedrooms, one living room and one bathroom for NT$10,000 a month felt reasonable to a Taipei person, and the sea and mountain views were truly priceless.</p><p>We lived in the Lotus Building beside the Qizichuan River for more than a year. Across the river in Jian, we found Baojuxiong Daycare Centre, which looked after our child during the day, giving both me and my family time of our own. Overall, the one who had the hardest time was our dog. She could only live on the balcony, because it was inconvenient with a baby in the house, and I would only take her downstairs for a walk when I had time.</p><p><b>I sincerely advise against keeping a dog while you are young and struggling. Without the time and space to love them properly, it is a pity for both person and dog.</b></p><p>This short-term rental in the Lotus Building was from 2006 to 2007, our temporary home in Hualien. Once we were settled, we thought about buying a place nearby. Wherever you live, if the rent and the mortgage payments work out to be about the same, you should make buying a home your top priority. Taking NT$10,000 a month for principal and interest as our budget, we tried searching in Hualien for homes with a total price of NT$2 million to NT$2.5 million. With the youth housing loan and our work income, if we lived frugally we could surely own a small place of our own. I will tell you about the one we eventually bought later. As for this rental, we actually offered NT$1.5 million to buy it, but the landlord was unwilling to sell, and the other units in the same building were too large and beyond our budget and ability. A young couple should do what they can within their means and treat family stability as the core of everything. Don't be too ambitious and make decisions you cannot afford.</p><p>The beauty of Hualien's sea and mountains honestly doesn't belong to young people struggling to get ahead. Busy every day with work, raising a child and the pressure of daily finances, the sea and mountains instead became a kind of separation from life. I also felt a haze of uncertainty about the future, because the mountains and sea are there every day, but so is the pressure of life that you have to face every day. It is a very contradictory feeling.</p><p>In recent years Taiwan has been promoting regional revitalisation as a policy. Honestly, it is far too romantic a plan. The two most important parts of revitalisation are the economy and the birth rate. In Hualien or other remote areas, unless your family has land, a house and a business, romantic ideas will only lead young people into a huge swamp where it is hard to grow and progress.</p><p>Only by moving to Hualien myself did I learn that reality is hard to beat imagination.</p><p>The great Hualien earthquake of 2024.4.3 caused losses that are hard to estimate. Thinking back to the Lotus Building, where we settled when we moved to Hualien 18 years ago, and comparing it with the building's terrible state right now, my regret and pain are still hard to calm.</p>
