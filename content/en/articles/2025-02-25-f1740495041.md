2025.2.25

My first award as a competition supervisor

When I had no students to coach, I would say that competitions are useless and merely pick out the top few, which means nothing. Today my state of mind is different. Teaching is progress in both directions. It seems that the students gain results from being taught, but the teacher actually gains more. How to guide students to face a challenge, face the rules and content of a competition with plans and steps, and carry it through to completion are all important things a teacher gains from coaching a competition. Without project-based competitions, everything becomes the routine of going to class and finishing class, and students let the simplest grades decide their learning achievement.

But can grades show that they have gained something or learned how to improve? In fact, it seems not. Teaching in class is simply the most effortless way: the teacher prepares lessons well, does not need to manage the differences between students, and uses exams or reports as the main measure of teaching results. The results and process this time have made me reflect.

Clearly, a competition depends on the students' own ability and ambition, so when forming a competition team, the teacher must also have the ambition to win, not just aim to take part. Plan everything fully, discuss and revise according to the plan, and carry on until the day the entry is submitted or the competition ends. It is a very complete learning experience. This is the second thing I have learned recently, and I feel it very strongly; it almost completely overturns my past ideas about teaching.

Still, thank you very much to the two lovely students from Dayuan. Without your earnest effort and execution, and your hard work editing the video until even you were satisfied with it, there would have been no chance to win an Honourable Mention in the national competition.

Heartfelt congratulations 🎉🎉🎉
