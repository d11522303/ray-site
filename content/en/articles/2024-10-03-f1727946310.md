2024.10.3

I just walked 2K along the road and saw quite a few dogs taking their owners out for a stroll while there was no wind or rain. It made me think of Snoopy, who lived on our balcony all those years. Snoopy was a stray we took in near Guting. Compared with today's dogs, who have their own beds at home, strollers to ride in when they go out, and even owners who cook dog food for them every day like private chefs, our Snoopy was so pitiful.

After she became part of our family, she lived on balconies: the balcony of an apartment near Sanhe Night Market in Sanchong, the balcony of the office building in Guting, the rooftop and balcony of the Muzha apartment, the balcony of the Lotus Building in Hualien, and the first-floor balcony in Wufenhu, Shoufeng. She drifted and wandered with us for fifteen years. Sometimes I think that if we hadn't adopted her back then, she might have lived out her days at the door of the little noodle shop in Guting.

In our first year in Hualien, wanting the Pacific view, we chose a 15th-floor 2LDK apartment with a sea-facing balcony. Our child had just been born, so Snoopy naturally lived on the balcony. It was around the end of summer and the start of autumn. As I recall, I went to Hualien for the interview in July, and in August I was told I would be taken on for a one-year trial as a lowest-grade project lecturer. Our child was only six months old, and the little Muzha apartment had only just been settled, yet we were rushing off to work in Hualien, weary from travel. Our household went from two working salaries to a family with only a one-year lecturer's pay. Looking at it today, my decision as a young man was utterly outrageous, completely ignoring the family's financial and living difficulties at the time, going to work at a private school in a remote area, and moving to Hualien exhausted, with even the dog worn out, for no good reason.

Right after arriving in Hualien we faced the test of typhoon season. I don't remember how many typhoons we met in the autumn of 2006, only that on typhoon nights we, a young couple, were in a high-rise facing the Pacific, so conspicuously exposed to the wind. In the strong gusts the building swayed along, and every spot in the rooms with a window air conditioner became a way for water to get in. Renting and living away from home, we had only bought mattresses and a plastic mat for the child and slept on the floor. We spent the whole night in terror in the wind and rain, and the poorest of all was Snoopy on the balcony. Looking after the child, blocking the water, and being rocked all night by the strong winds of the building, our family of four got through that typhoon season in a frightened, bedraggled state.

The next year we moved again, to Wufenhu in Shoufeng, where we lived for five years. The environment and life there were far better than in the city. In the Huadong Valley, sheltered by the Coastal Mountain Range and the Central Mountain Range, the typhoon seasons were not so frightening. We also chose to live on a lower floor for something steadier and more practical, and both Snoopy and the child had better space to move around.

I'm writing all this only because I'm afraid my impressions of life will slowly fade, like the Norfolk pines along the road after a Hualien typhoon, all stripped bare by the strong winds. The Lotus Building in Hualien where we lived is still a dangerous building because of earthquake damage. Every day we live through passes over a few months of waxing and waning moons, calm winds and gentle rain, the lush green of changing seasons, and we are so lost in it that we think the peace and beauty of the moment will last forever.
