---

7/23, Dashu (Major Heat) Day, and Hangzhou is scorching.

Yesterday's typhoon seemed to have no effect on the sun at all. The morning news kept reminding everyone in Hangzhou to protect themselves from the sun and the intense heat!

Today I was visiting Songcheng Group, a well-known local entertainment company in Hangzhou. Some of the students work outdoors, some wear ancient costumes to promote the park, some work at the hotel lobby, and of course some work in food and beverage service. In total, 14 students from 7 schools (Jinan, Penghu, Yilan, Providence, Hsing Wu, Chinese Culture and Jinwen) are doing their internships at the Songcheng Group's First World Hotel and the Songcheng theme park.

Songcheng Group is listed on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange. Formerly Hangzhou Songcheng Tourism Development Co., Ltd., it was founded in September 1994 and renamed Songcheng Performance Development Co., Ltd. in March 2014. Its headquarters are in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, and its main business is investing in, developing and operating theme parks and cultural tourism performances. In 2017 the group's annual revenue was about NT$15 billion. Most of its customers are visitors from mainland China, so the design of the whole theme park follows the tastes of mainland tourists.

Put simply, the students' internship here is a "down to earth" experience: getting to know the characteristics of 1.3 billion consumers, working alongside interns from every province, and facing a Hangzhou July heat that is nothing like Taiwan's maritime climate. Where they live and what they eat are different from home. Toward the end of the visit, two male students finished setting up the performance stage before they could join us for a photo and a chat. Look closely and you can see white salt stains on their black clothes! I really want to give these students a big thumbs-up for being willing to step out of their comfort zone.

The managers and colleagues at both Songcheng units that offered internships did their best to look after the students. The Taiwanese students felt cared for by the HR staff and their colleagues, and they found the food in the Songcheng park canteen to their liking. On days off they took buses around town, and the farthest they went by train was Shanghai. People often say Taiwanese students lack a "wolf spirit". I feel that Taiwanese students have humanity and warmth. Our hospitality vocational education is reflected in every move these students make: being polite, generous and wise is something wolves cannot do. What these students have is wisdom and refinement in their every gesture. I am truly proud to be their teacher.
