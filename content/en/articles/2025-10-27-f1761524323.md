[Timor-Leste Joins ASEAN]

The part about Timor-Leste in the World Tourism Geography course I just finished teaching needs updating.

Timor-Leste is a new country on the island of Timor, at the far east of Indonesia. Timor is a little smaller than Taiwan, about 30,000 square kilometres, and like Taiwan it is a mountainous island, with its highest peak reaching nearly 3,000 metres.

Unlike Indonesia, which was colonised by the Dutch, it was colonised by Portugal, so Timor-Leste's official languages are Tetum and Portuguese. Most residents are Catholic, and the island also has descendants of Hakka people who migrated there a hundred years ago.

At the end of the last century, Timor-Leste wanted independence from Indonesia, but was bloodily suppressed by the Indonesian authorities, and 300,000 people were forcibly driven to West Timor to become refugees. Through international mediation, Timor-Leste formally restored its independence at midnight on 20 May 2002, with the name Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste.

On 26 October 2025 the 47th ASEAN Summit was held in Kuala Lumpur. At the opening ceremony, the ASEAN countries and the Prime Minister of Timor-Leste stamped and signed the declaration document, and it formally joined ASEAN. Timor-Leste's Prime Minister Xanana Gusmao signed last, smiling as he held the pen, unable to hide his emotion, his eyes glistening with tears.

Taiwan belongs to few world organisations. Apart from the WTO and APEC, we have almost no place at international cooperation occasions. In the next few days the 2025 APEC Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting will be held in Busan, with big names such as Trump and Xi attending, but does anyone know whom Taiwan's president is sending to attend?

In the technology industry we hold many world number-ones, and our exposure and influence in economic strength are astonishing. But businesspeople have no borders and people climb to higher places, and those we negotiate with politically are world powers. Taiwan is just paying money as a comprador, and there will always be a day when it is used up. Wanting a real voice in the international arena is truly not easy.
