I think the China Post wrote this one for us!

On the sidewalks of Taiwan's cities, aren't motorcycles parked everywhere too? Pedestrians have lost their right of way entirely. With no sidewalk worth walking on, everyone is pushed onto the road, or simply falls into the habit of riding a motorcycle. This is really an indicator of a country lagging behind, yet everyone turns a blind eye. On this small island of Taiwan, in 2017, there are 13,711,075 motorcycles on the roads, which means nearly every adult in Taiwan has one (data source: http://stat.motc.gov.tw/mocdb/stmain.jsp?sys=100&funid=a3301.). For the sake of convenience, we let swarms of scooters multiply everywhere like cancer cells in our cities. They kill the look of the city, and they are a problem for national development.

Jakarta, Indonesia, has already begun to deal with this. In Taiwan, by contrast, motorcycle ads are still running nonstop just before the new school term, tempting young students to swap or upgrade their bikes. Yet when our students go on internships in Singapore or Japan, don't they get around perfectly well on foot plus public transport? If Taiwan wants to develop tourism properly, it should start with each of us.

If everyone switched to walking plus public transport, scrapped old motorcycles, and bought fewer new vehicles, Taiwan would slowly become more beautiful!
