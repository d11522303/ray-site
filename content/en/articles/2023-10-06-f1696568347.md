title: "Memories of Travelling Alone in 1980"
excerpt: "Ray recalls solo journeys as a child in 1980: secret pre-dawn bike rides, riding the train alone to Taichung, and finding his way to a new home by bus."

<p>Do you like travelling alone? I love it. I deeply love the observations I discover alone along the way, and look forward to the moments of emotion that unknown scenery brings me. This kind of solo travel began when I was very young; preparing for the trip, setting off, and returning like a weary bird to its nest were all so free and easy.</p><p>Did you go on long trips by yourself before the age of ten?</p><h2 id="heading-6147425">Bicycle Trips</h2><p>In 1980, when I was around second or third grade of primary school, my mother gave me about NT$1 pocket money a day, mostly in return for helping with housework, such as washing dishes, mopping the floor, or going to the alley corner or the wet market to buy groceries or fresh produce. Once children have a little money, they start to make plans, and my plan was mostly to go on bicycle trips. But the special thing was that my family often did not know I was out on these trips, because I went out at 3 am and got home just before dawn.</p><p>I would first use the small change I had saved, while running errands to the grocery store for eggs, salt and soy sauce, to buy myself some snacks: mostly red dried cod slices, Heiren cool candy, red dried mango slices that looked like betel nut, and on days I was especially looking forward to, a tube of toothpaste chocolate as a reward. Then I put them in a little bundle by my bed, and went to sleep early, waiting for the morning to come.</p><p>I had a bicycle with a basket on the front, kept in the stairwell of our apartment building. On a fine morning at three o'clock, I would take my snacks and ride off into the dark streets for a trip. I rode over the bridge across the big drainage ditch beside the wall of the Far Eastern Textile factory, turned right at the theatre entrance onto Sichuan Road, and then turned right again towards Far Eastern Memorial Hospital and Far East College. The roads at night over forty years ago were very quiet, and I could face the cool wind while eating nutritionless snacks, observing at the same time the completely different scenery beside the streets that were normally bustling with traffic. There used to be a swimming pool and a sports area near the hospital, and I would use that area as my turning point and rest stop. By around 4 am a few morning exercisers began to appear; as a small child riding a bike I was never asked about it, and felt completely at ease. Then I'd wind my way home in the same general direction, quietly open the door, go upstairs and go back to sleep in my bed.</p><p>These little trips went on for quite a while, so many times that I lost count, and none of my family ever noticed.</p><h2 id="heading-6147435">Rail Trips</h2><p>As a child, I went back to Taichung every summer vacation, but my young, busy parents could hardly find the time to take me back. In those days the train ride took a very, very long time, and neither the fare nor the round-trip time was something they could bear.</p><p>So I remember that in third grade, I went with my mother to Banqiao railway station. She bought a platform ticket and I bought a half-fare ticket, and I took the Chu-Kuang Express back to Taichung by myself. Along the way I found the conductor lady and told her I was travelling alone, and she kindly let me sit in her small seat, as she had to go push the refreshment cart to sell goods. I watched the station signs at every stop and silently recited every station name from Banqiao to Taichung, because that little seat had no window and I was afraid of missing my stop.</p><p>On arriving at Taichung Station, I would get off by myself and leave through the rear station exit. My grandfather would always be waiting before the rear gate's ticket barrier according to the train timetable, and would then ride me on his motorcycle back to our dormitory in the Xingda Wucun community. A solo rail journey was how I began my favourite, leisurely and long summer vacations.</p><h2 id="heading-6147443">Bus Trips</h2><p>I also remember clearly that in fifth grade, after finishing the gifted natural-science class at Houpu Primary School, I walked back to our fourth-floor apartment near the Yinghua Theatre on Daming Street off Sichuan Road in Banqiao. The door was locked and no one answered. Only then did I remember that it was moving day: our new home was near Jingping and Jing'an Roads in Zhonghe. Using my familiarity with the area and my knowledge of transport, I boarded my first ever Route 10 Taipei Bus. It left Sichuan Road, passed Banqiao station and Zhongshan Road, and turned at Jisui onto Liancheng Road. All the way I kept looking for familiar scenery and key landmarks until I saw the Zhonghe Temple Square stop, and I knew the new home was nearby. It was already dark. I got off at roughly where the Nanshan Road to Jing'an stop is now, and, using my memory of the route from my one visit to see the new home, I found our new house in Zhonghe. When I came in, my mother was preparing to make offerings to the local land god. She glanced at me and said only: "Oh, you're back."</p><p>Did you know that animals all have the instinct to return home without any help from learning?</p><p>Salmon do, elephants do, migratory birds do, and so do I.</p>
