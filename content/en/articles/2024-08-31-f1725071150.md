Reflections on Development Projects

I asked the university student who had just come home last night whether he knew about the big events such as the Ko Wen-je case.

He said: not really following, because he was busy with freshman camp activities, and it's a lot of fun.

True enough. Looking back at my own university years, I was worried about being bullied after enlisting, and some unknown people signed me up as a member of the Kuomintang. Back then I heard that if you didn't join the party, the two years would be hard, so who had time to care about so many other things.

After my discharge, working at China Credit Information Service slowly exposed me to all kinds of development projects, and many impressive things happened.

1. In 1997, when I had just joined the company, it was working on a big project, and as a rookie I was assigned to look at Taiwan's seaside bathing beaches. I first looked up Tourism Bureau data and had to write out the visitor volume at each place. Around then my supervisors even flew to Okinawa to look at a pineapple theme park and a cactus park, and I envied them so much. Later this big project's company, Taiwan Pineapple (台鳳), became a focus of the stock market, with a successful capital increase and a soaring share price. I won't say what happened afterwards. By the time I left Hualien ten years ago, the people involved in this case had only just been jailed, and now they have served their time and come out again.

2. In 1997, at a meeting at the World Trade Center, the attendees all talked about "one o one", using it again and again as an example. I thought it was just some kind of code name. There was no internet then, and only several months later did I learn that the meeting that day was an important planning meeting for Taipei 101.

3. In 1998, I took on a project "X" for the Chang Sheng Group, to plan the advertising billboard revenue from Taipei to the airport. My young self rode a motorbike to several desolate locations to take photos with a film camera. Only now do I know that the junction back then is today's Linkou A9 airport MRT station.

I'll write just this much for now. There were also Tung Ti's Tungtex (東帝士), which burned to the ground, Yulon's domestic cars sold through agents, Licheng Construction in Nankan, and the joint development at Taipei MRT Daan Station, all of which were the best viewing windows for me when I was only 25 and fresh into society. Through collecting corporate data and interviewing key executives, beyond an excessive number of written reports and over a dozen project presentations, they were also a kaleidoscope of workplace life for me, a rookie, on my first exploration of land development projects.

Luckily I was only a junior staff member and never ran into anything lucrative, but having taken part in many projects, I came away with some understanding. I wonder what the people being arrested now are thinking.
