When the pub closes, I leave

Churchill, the legendary British prime minister who led Britain through the Second World War, probably imagined and hoped to serve his country until his dying day. Yet when he lost an election, he told the press an intriguing line: "Mr. Editor, I leave when the pub closes#." Some say this is the graceful figure of a politician retiring; others say it is the drunken talk of an old man leaving the table.

In recent days I saw a former Premier and Vice President publish a book#, and Premier Su resign along with his cabinet, and the comparison struck me deeply. As a former foot soldier who worked for the KMT for many years and once took pride in working for the party and the country, I watched an elderly man, who sold off the party's assets in a failed bid for victory, publish a book years later, apparently believing the pub was still open for him. The sight of him lingering at the door, muttering resentfully, made me laugh.

Churchill, The Economist, 1 February 1965, quoted in Lang- worth, ed., Churchill By Himself, 528.

Lien Chan, Lien Chan Memoirs, 17 January 2023, Commonwealth Publishing.
