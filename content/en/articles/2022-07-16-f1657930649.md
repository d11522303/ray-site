Years ago on Zhixue Street in Hualien, I saw a student in slippers walk out of his dormitory on the street, get on his motorcycle and twist the throttle. His feet had not even reached the front footrests before he stopped at a breakfast shop 50 metres away.

I had cycled there to buy breakfast. Soon I watched the student, with his food bought, climb onto his ride again, and after another twist of the throttle he was back at the dormitory 50 metres away and went upstairs.

Zhixue Street has proper pedestrian pavements, but the two-lane road is lined on both sides with the motorcycles of students and residents. Too many people in Taiwan are so used to two-wheeled motor vehicles that they forget they still have two legs to walk on.

The same thing happened a few days ago when I went to a breakfast shop and saw a man, fully able-bodied, ride his motorcycle a few dozen metres out of his neighbourhood to pick up breakfast and then ride back in.

Many years ago, Hualien, I saw a student wearing slippers, walking out of the dormitory on the street, riding a motorcycle, his feet were still not on the front pedal, and than he stopped at breakfast restaurant at 50 meters away.

At that time I went for breakfast on bicycle, and soon I watched the student with food and straddle his ride again. Such distance with the accelerator in seconds, he went back to the dormitory and went upstairs.

Taiwan has a well sidewalk or pedestrian arcade along the narrow streets, but where are full of motorcycles of students and residents. However, Taiwanese are used to two wheels, forgetting that they still have feet to walk.

Recently, I went to the breakfast shop a few days ago and saw an young man, riding a motorcycle dozens of meters out of the community to get food and then back to his house in few seconds.
