2024.8.26

Kuo-Kuang Bus. When I was a rookie soldier at Longgang in Zhongli, it was called Taiwan Motor Transport. Every time my leave ended, I would go to the old and crowded Taiwan Motor Transport North Station to take a bus back to the barracks. Compared with the train, a bus let you rest and sleep a little, so it was always more comfortable!

It's a pity that the Taiwan Motor Transport North Station was later not preserved as a heritage site (see the article in the comments), but the original footbridge is still there, and whenever I walk across it I think of my complicated feelings back then.

From today the roles have been swapped, and Kuo-Kuang Bus has become the way our university-student child gets back to Hsinchu. Buying tickets, waiting in line, getting on and off are all similar scenes. What does not change are the figures of travellers coming and going, leaving for far places and returning home.

The newly built Chang Gung transfer station is very well equipped, thanks to careful planning by the corporate group. It is a composite building: the ground transport level has a waiting area, dozens of food stalls and platforms for several national-highway coach companies, linking every part of the country via the national highways, while the floors above and below are car parks with nearly a thousand spaces for the crowds coming for medical care at Chang Gung.
