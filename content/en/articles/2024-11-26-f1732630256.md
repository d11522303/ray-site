A little urban railway trip for the teachers and students of Travel Management. Following in the footsteps of Governor Liu Ming-chuan, we strolled along the site of the 1888 Dadaocheng railway, passing the 1909 Taipei Workshop, the 1919 Railway Department Office Building, the Octagonal Building and the Engineering Building, and walked into the National Taiwan Museum Railway Department Park, which was completed and opened in 2020.

This was the second stage of a guiding course. After the students stepped out of campus to ride the railway from a different perspective and link their learning with local guiding, I took them into the museum to experience how history and the modern day interact. I had assumed that young students only love the glamour on their phone screens and forget that a corner of the city holds finer textures. In fact, seeing the students' perspectives and their guiding, and how these connected with the rail concepts we had covered in the classroom, I was delighted to have opened a window of curiosity for this group of students.

Standing with the students in front of the North Gate, which has stood for 140 years, I had them pay the NT$50 Taiwan Museum admission fee themselves. Through this small requirement of a micro-credit, I hope this group of new Travel Management students can try to understand the relationships among the city, life, space, leisure and travel. I also hope this kind of practice gives them deeper foundations when they later have the chance to take foreign visitors around and promote Taiwan, so they can share the urban landscapes that sit right in the city and along the way, and introduce, as they walk, the Taiwanese tourism resources they identify with and are proud of.
