20240403 Hualien Earthquake

Friends often ask about our family's eight years of life in Hualien. I usually answer: it was a blessing from heaven to our family, giving us work in Hualien and a home in which to raise our children.

For all this I have to thank my dear classmate Alumi Lu, who recommended me for a teaching post in Hualien back then. Afterwards we met so many colleagues, teachers, good friends and students who looked after our family. Because of these connections, our lives were enriched and our days were full and good.

Because of these connections, the wounds on this land are tied to us. Especially since we came back to work in Taipei in recent years, I often think of our life in Hualien. On sleepless nights I still faintly imagine the calm, comfortable air of the rift valley, pretending that outside the window there is still a sky full of stars and the occasional chirp of insects. Those mountains, that water and those people are what I lean on to calm my body and mind.

I dare not watch the videos of falling rocks on the Suhua Highway. I dare not look squarely at each damaged house and the people in them. I cannot bear to look at the fire at National Dong Hwa University, alma mater of our dog's mum and dog. I feel deeply uneasy for not being there with them.

I pray deeply and hope that wounded Hualien will slowly recover.
