---
title: "A Teacher Beside Me Tried One-on-One Teaching, and a Thought on the 108 Curriculum"
excerpt: "A colleague's one-on-one teaching gets students moving, and I argue that better teaching reflects a maturing civil society, not the 108 curriculum."
published: true
---

A teacher who has just been sitting next to me chatted with me. He recently tried a one-on-one teaching approach in class and found that students began to move: hands writing assignments, minds thinking about the content, mouths joining the discussion. He feels that with the old way of talking at the front of the class, it is hard to tell whether most of the students in the department understand, and that more tools and methods need to be tried.

On the vocational education front line I also see many teachers working very hard to find ways to rekindle students' motivation to learn. Students cannot be defined by social incidents or exam rankings; every student is an individual case. Is this thanks to the 108 Curriculum?

I think it is not quite right for the author and CommonWealth Magazine to put it that way. I think this is the result of the progress of Taiwan's civil society and the effort invested in education, and has nothing to do with any government or curriculum. Those in high positions should not think too highly of themselves. Decades ago, under the Party-State curriculum, I also met teachers who worked hard to improve their teaching content and skills. Of course there were also very unsuitable teachers. The education management mechanisms of today are much better than before, and society is more diverse, so a teacher's unsuitability is easily amplified, and students' deviant behaviour is easily gossiped about by the media. Overall, the quality of both teachers and students in our schools is moving forward, and can even rank among the advanced nations of human society. This too has nothing to do with any government. Over the decades we have slowly come to respect every person and every role, not using seniority or position as dogma, and not using the ideas of a leader to define direction or right and wrong.

The content written by Ms. Mei-Chen Chiu is very good. It is a pity that such a good article was given the 108 Curriculum as its headline, which wastes it.
