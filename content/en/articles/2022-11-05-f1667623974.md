【Reusing School Campuses in an Age of Falling Birth Rates: A Feature in Korea's Dong-A Ilbo】

According to data from Korea's Ministry of Education, 3,896 public schools were closed between 1982 and 2020. The problem of school closures in Japan is much more serious. According to a 2017 report by the Korea Education Development Institute, 6,811 elementary, middle and high schools closed in Japan between 2002 and 2015, and 87% of them were used as educational facilities or public sports facilities rather than being sold.

How can schools be revitalised? Korean education authorities have outsourced 2,558 closed schools under their own control and 1,338 schools under provincial offices of education. Closed schools are used as facilities that raise residents' income, such as campsites and experience centres, or as educational, welfare and cultural facilities.

Unusual experiments in reusing school campuses also continue in Japan. Hatada Elementary School in Chiba Prefecture, closed in 2014, has been turned into a rest area because of its location near a railway station and a highway interchange. Its accommodation, gallery and restaurant have become a must-visit stop for travel in the surrounding area. In addition, depending on regional characteristics, closed schools are used for various purposes, such as public housing facilities or child development support centres.

Revitalising closed schools is hard. The report cites a survey of experts and scholars: if local residents are excluded when a closed school is put to use, it is hard to succeed. For example, Joo Yong-wook, head of the regional development department at the Gangwon Research Institute, said: "When meeting local residents in mountain areas, older people often choose a public bathhouse as the facility they most want, while young people often choose a hipster bookstore."
