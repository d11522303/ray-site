<figure class="ph">Photo</figure><p>Cruise decks are often painted with various patterns, but on Asian itineraries you rarely see passengers playing on them. Most cruise tour leaders and YouTubers don't know how to play either. Once on board, they just tell guests to go eat at the buffet or wander around, and after seeing this too often, cruise travel just becomes dull.</p><p>How do you play shuffleboard? Is it fun? Today I will use the teaching materials and equipment in the cruise classroom of the <a target="_blank" rel="noopener" href="https://www.facebook.com/hashtag/%E9%86%92%E5%90%BE%E6%97%85%E9%81%8B%E7%B3%BB">#HWUTravelManagement</a> department to explain how to play deck shuffleboard.</p><p>There are many forms of shuffleboard. Here I will use the common type on decks, with numbers at both ends, as the example.</p><p>First, a shuffleboard court on deck is about 4.75 metres long, a rectangle with a triangular scoring area at each end. Each player or team gets 4 flat discs and a cue. The discs come in two colours, usually yellow and black, with a diameter of 15 cm and a thickness of 1.4 to 2.5 cm. The cue is a pole no longer than 2 metres, with a U-shaped fork on the pushing end.</p><p>The two sides take turns sliding discs of their own colour until all the discs have been played. Play begins with the player of the [yellow discs]. On their turn, players place a disc inside the "10 OFF" area on their side and then shoot it toward the scoring triangle at the other end.</p><p>The yellow player's discs are shot from the left side of the court, and the black player's from the right. The cue may not be pushed past the scoring area when shooting. The disc must cross the "mid-line", which is 0.9 metres before the scoring area at the other end, but must not reach the edge of the court. Any disc that fails to cross the line, or touches the edge, is removed from the court.</p><p>Players try to land their discs in high-scoring areas and knock their opponents' discs into low-scoring areas or right off the court. The shuffleboard triangle is divided into 6 zones, and a disc must lie entirely within one of them to score. A disc in the apex zone of the triangle scores 10 points, a disc in either of the two zones below the apex scores 8, and a disc in either of the two zones below the 8-point zone scores 7. A disc that stops in the "10 OFF" area deducts 10 points from its player or team.</p><p>Note: some stricter rules</p><p>● Shooting from beyond the shooting area: deduct 5 points.<br/>● The body may not cross the boundary line when shooting: deduct 5 points.<br/>● A disc knocked off because of a foul is returned to the opponent to replay.</p><p>There are two ways to win:</p><p>● Slide the discs from one end of the court only, and the winner is decided directly by score.<br/>● Slide from both ends in turn, and the first to accumulate 75 points or more wins.</p><figure class="ph">Photo</figure><p>Alright! Next week in class we will practise in groups, so that when you take groups on a cruise in the future, you will remember to teach your guests how to play with confidence.</p>
