37 Tainan's Zuozhen Fossil Park is famous for displaying fossils. Which of the following fossils has NOT been found in the Cailiao River basin of this area? (1) rhinoceros (2) elephant (3) water buffalo (4) dinosaur

Explanation: The Tainan Zuozhen Fossil Park shares its site with Guangrong Elementary School in Zuozhen District, Tainan. The Fossil Museum exists because of the rich fossil deposits discovered in the nearby Cailiao River basin, and it systematically presents the ecology of this land of Taiwan tens of thousands of years ago, including fossils of many mammals such as the Taiwan Hayasaka rhinoceros, early elephants (such as the oriental elephant and Huai River elephant), deer and water buffalo, bearing witness to Taiwan's historical ties with the Eurasian continent. The fossil park also has in-depth introductions to the discovery and research history of Zuozhen Man, a human site from Taiwan's late Neolithic period.

The earliest human remains now recognised by Taiwan's archaeological community have been replaced by Penghu Man and Matsu's Liangdao Man. But a more interesting discovery in archaeology is the dog fossil unearthed at the Dabenkeng culture site at the Southern Taiwan Science Park in Tainan (about 4,800 years old). Called "Taiwan's First Dog", it is the earliest domestic dog fossil found in Taiwan with a close symbiotic relationship with humans. The bones are completely preserved, in a sleeping posture, which amazed the archaeological experts. After years of investigation, it is inferred that this skeleton is probably the earliest domesticated dog found in the country, clearly showing the deep bond that humans and dogs established.

Here are two archaeology questions related to national heritage sites:

#1 Which of the following pairings of "national heritage site, its representative culture and important finds" is the most correct?

(1) Shihsanhang site, Palaeolithic culture, Taiwan's earliest human fossils

(2) Peinan site, megalithic culture, large numbers of slate coffins and signs of a hierarchical society

(3) Yuanshan site, Iron Age culture, Taiwan's earliest rice agriculture

(4) Cailiao Zuozhen site, metal-tool culture, remains of Han Chinese settlers' lives

#2 If you want to explain to tourists how Taiwan's prehistoric society developed from early hunting and gathering to one with division of labour, iron smelting and the ability to trade abroad, which of the following site comparisons has the most representative and chronological significance?

(1) Yuanshan site (agricultural settlement and pottery) → Shihsanhang site (iron production and maritime trade)

(2) Changbin culture (Palaeolithic hunting and gathering) → Peinan site (megalithic architecture and burial system)

(3) Peinan site (social hierarchy) → Yuanshan site (food culture)

(4) Shihsanhang site (iron-smelting technology) → Changbin culture (chipped stone tools)
