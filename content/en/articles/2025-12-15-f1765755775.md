In fact, because of the changing makeup of their student bodies, many Taiwanese universities are jokingly called "XX International University" by their students, since the languages heard in campus classrooms are already Vietnamese and Indonesian, with only the teacher speaking Mandarin. Nearly all the staff at shops around campuses in recent years have also been replaced by Vietnamese employees, at the counter, in service and in the kitchen. Apart from the owner, almost everyone in the whole shop is a foreigner. So when the government writes that the accommodation industry will need 13,000 more workers by 2030, it seems far from the real situation. Come and feel it in Taoyuan and New Taipei. I think a 0 or even two 0s after that number is possible.

The Ministry of Labor, always a few beats slow, has finally begun planning to do something about the labour shortage. In fact, the Ministry of Education, the Overseas Community Affairs Council and other related agencies have long been bringing in students who can supply labour through other channels, filling the labour gap while they study in Taiwan.

Society is undergoing a huge transformation. Let's wait and see.

--------Executive Yuan press release-----------

The Ministry of Labor noted that Taiwan is facing an ageing population and a falling birth rate and has entered a super-aged society. Because of the shrinking labour force, industries have been reporting labour shortages. At the same time, Taiwanese workers also face low pay, with low-wage workers making up a higher share in the service sector, and all sectors are paying close attention to improving the shortage and low-wage issues.

The Ministry of Labor explained that, to encourage companies to raise the pay and employment conditions of Taiwanese workers and avoid the "low-wage anchoring" phenomenon, while actively recruiting and using transnational labour to help industries address worker shortages, it aims to balance industry staffing needs with a win-win mechanism for labour and management. Over the past several months, the Ministry of Labor has repeatedly held inter-ministry consultations with the National Development Council, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the Ministry of the Interior, the Ministry of Transportation and Communications, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Health and Welfare and others, and together they are promoting four major plans. On the premise of stabilising employment for Taiwanese people, these actively supplement the labour industries need while strengthening government efficiency to ensure a steady improvement in workforce quality. The four plans are expected to take effect in January 2026, as detailed below:

1. Raising pay for Taiwanese workers in manufacturing to increase migrant worker quotas: To enable manufacturing to fill its basic-level labour needs while also improving the pay conditions of Taiwanese workers, the Ministry of Labor plans to add a supplementary quota allocation mechanism for migrant workers in manufacturing. When manufacturing employers raise the pay of their full-time Taiwanese employees, for every Taiwanese worker given a raise, one additional migrant worker quota is allocated beyond the 3K5 tiered quota system, up to a maximum of 10% of the number of employees enrolled in labour insurance. For employers adopting this plan, the maximum cap on their migrant worker quota is raised from the original 40% to 45% of the number of employees enrolled in labour insurance.

2. Relaxing the cap on retaining foreign skilled workers: The Ministry of Labor plans to allow employers to apply to retain all senior migrant workers who meet the conditions for converting to skilled workers. The manufacturing cap on allocated skilled workers is relaxed from 25% to 100%, to help employers retain senior migrant workers by converting them to foreign skilled workers. However, migrant workers, foreign skilled workers and foreign professionals combined still may not exceed 50% of total employees. At the same time, rules on health checks, living care management and accommodation for foreign skilled workers will be loosened, giving them more autonomy in daily life, enhancing their labour rights and building a diverse and friendly society.

3. Bringing foreign skilled workers into the accommodation industry and commercial port terminals: "Accommodation services" and "commercial port cargo handling and distribution" will be opened to the employment of foreign skilled workers who may be recruited directly from overseas. When employers raise the pay of their full-time Taiwanese employees, for every Taiwanese worker given a raise, one additional foreign skilled worker quota is allowed, up to a maximum of 10% of the number of employees enrolled in labour insurance.

4. Strengthening government efficiency: In response to the international trend of fair recruitment of migrant workers, the Ministry of Labor will establish a Transnational Labor Recruitment Center and set up overseas outposts, specifically handling matters related to recruiting and testing transnational labour and concretely implementing direct government-to-government hiring. It will also add direct hiring service points, integrating bilingual human resources and strengthening services related to migrant workers changing employers, to improve the quality of public services.
