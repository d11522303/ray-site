Fewer High School GSAT Test-Takers Than University Places

The General Scholastic Ability Test (GSAT) for Year 113 of the Republic (2024) will be held from January 20 to 22, with an estimated 120,000 candidates registered. In Year 109 (2020), Taiwan's universities (excluding vocational universities of technology) offered 174,000 places for new students. The Ministry of Education's statistics (see the attached image) are released more slowly, but the figure should not be much different.

What follows is some grumbling from the parent of a candidate. Read on only if you're interested.

#The time and weight given to each subject are unreasonable

The GSAT subjects are Chinese (180 minutes), English (100 minutes), Mathematics A (100 minutes), Mathematics B (100 minutes), Social Studies (110 minutes) and Natural Sciences (110 minutes), six subjects in all. In terms of exam time, Chinese takes up two class periods. What's more, the GSAT Chinese exam is split into 2 papers, [Integrated Chinese Language Ability] and [Chinese Writing Ability], each 90 minutes.

The Integrated Chinese Language Ability test has an astonishing number of questions, up to 14 pages packed with them. There are 25 single-choice questions worth 2 points each, 7 multiple-choice questions worth 4 points each, mixed questions including 2 single-choice questions at 2 points, and 4 short passages at 4 points each. The questions are highly varied and have almost no overlap with the curriculum guidelines.

The Chinese Writing Ability test has two major questions, worth 25 points each. The first is divided into 2 sub-questions, mainly argumentative essays. The second tests candidates' ability to express feelings and sentiments, and asks them to write 3 pieces within 90 minutes.

#Sorry, Chinese teachers

I ask high school Chinese teachers to stop demanding that this or that important text be added to the curriculum, because it means nothing for the nation's future strength or for education. By contrast, I think the STEM-related Natural Sciences paper is vitally important: testing the fundamental abilities of four subjects, physics, chemistry, biology and earth science, in 110 minutes is highly unreasonable in terms of time proportion. For a Taiwan to which technological development matters so much, the fact that top universities' quality departments have abandoned the Chinese score shows that Chinese as a subject is completely out of balance between the supply side of high school teaching and the demand side of society's professional needs.

I love reading and writing very much, but I hardly remember what I read or what I was tested on in Chinese class in junior high and high school, and I have also forgotten what I stated in my essays in those few short dozen minutes.
