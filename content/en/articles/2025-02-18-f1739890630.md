Karma: Leave Everything to Fate?

These past few days I've been so tired that I've almost lost the ability to look forward to anything! Endless illness in myself and my family, preparing for the new semester's courses, project deadlines, unexpected trips out, and above all my parents at home, who leave me helpless, have pushed me almost to my emotional lowest, and I have even booked an appointment at a psychosomatic clinic in Zhubei for March.

But I still have so much attachment to and hope for this world. I hope to use a healthy body to see the world and experience cultures everywhere, and I am still full of curiosity about an unknown future. So I absolutely must not get stuck in fate, and I must not lose heart and give up everything because of it.

On a day when I had taught seven classes, I found an interesting film, The Extraordinary Journey of the Fakir (in Chinese, "Travelling with an IKEA Wardrobe"). Adapted from a French novel, it is very dramatic, just like the French romanticism about the world. With its lovely Indian music and an interweaving of refugees, ethnic groups and the scenery of various European countries, it weaves a wonderful adventure comedy.

Aja's mother says: don't let luck and fate toy with your life. Believe you can escape the poverty of your class through effort, and most importantly, be rich in your heart, because people who have love and trust are always rich.
