A. R. Penck

Germany, 1939-2017

Untitled IV

1962

Synthetic polymer paint on canvas

Ralf Winkler began using the name A. R. Penck when he held his first exhibition in West Germany in 1969. It marked a deliberate attempt to resist an aesthetic that could be easily identified through a name or a style.

Until 1980, Penck lived under the communist system in East Germany, unable to travel to West Germany to see exhibitions of his own work, nor to show art in his place of birth. This tendency to "seek change" and to "confuse" reflected the psychological impact of post-war German society. He used the visual potential of black and white and the primal power of his totemic figures to explore unequal power structures and to reveal the aggression that can exist between people.
