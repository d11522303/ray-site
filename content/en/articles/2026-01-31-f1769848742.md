22 Which of the following peoples never ruled over the territory of present-day England? (A) Celts (B) Normans (C) Basques (D) Ancient Romans

The answer is (C) Basques

The land of Britain has a history of being repeatedly conquered and ruled by different peoples. The key peoples who shaped English history are described below in chronological order:

1. Celts - from about 600 BC: Early inhabitants of the British Isles, living in tribal form.

2. Ancient Romans - AD 43 to 410: Emperor Claudius conquered the island and established provinces, leaving behind Hadrian's Wall and great roads.

3. Anglo-Saxons - from about the 5th century to 1066: After the Romans withdrew, Germanic tribes migrated in and founded several small kingdoms.

4. Vikings/Danes - from the late 8th century to the 11th century: Invaders from northern Europe who ruled eastern and northern England (the Danelaw).

5. Normans - from 1066: William the Conqueror began his rule after the Battle of Hastings, with a profound influence on law and architecture, and England gradually became a major state. Below is the order of dynasties after England was unified, for a rough understanding:

＃HouseOfNormandy (1066-1154)

Key figure: William the Conqueror.

Major events: In 1066 William, from Normandy in France, conquered the Nordic-descended peoples of England, introduced French and the continental feudal system, and carried out a survey of land and population. England only formally entered the dynastic era in history from here.

＃HouseOfPlantagenet (1154-1485)

This was the longest-ruling dynasty. It later split into two branches, "Lancaster" and "York", and the struggle between the two families for the throne caused the Wars of the Roses. In the end Henry Tudor (Henry VII) of the Lancaster family won and married Elizabeth of York, uniting the two families and opening the powerful Tudor dynasty.

Key figures: Richard the Lionheart, King John.

Major events: Signing of the Magna Carta, the Hundred Years' War between England and France, the Wars of the Roses.

＃HouseOfTudor (1485-1603)

Key figures: Henry VIII, Elizabeth I.

Major events: The Reformation, defeat of the Spanish Armada, the Renaissance, Shakespeare.

＃HouseOfStuart (1603-1714)

Because Elizabeth I had no heir, the throne passed to James, King of Scotland, who held both crowns.

Key figures: Charles I (executed by Parliament), James II.

Major events: The English Civil War, the Glorious Revolution, the Bill of Rights, and the formal union with Scotland to form "Great Britain".

＃HouseOfHanover (1714-1901)

Because the last Stuart queen had no heir, George I, of royal blood, was brought in from the Electorate of Hanover in Germany to succeed. When George I took the throne he could hardly speak English and communicated mainly in German and French. Because of the language barrier he instead delegated power to Parliament, which indirectly gave rise to the responsible cabinet system and Britain's first effective "Prime Minister", Robert Walpole. Under Hanoverian rule, Britain transformed from a European island nation into the global hegemon, the "empire on which the sun never sets", an important process.

Key figure: Queen Victoria.

Major events: Responsible cabinet government, the Industrial Revolution, the British Empire at its height (the empire on which the sun never sets).

＃HouseOfWindsor (1917-present)

After Queen Victoria died, the throne passed to her son Edward VII. His house was originally Saxe-Coburg and Gotha (also from Germany), but during the First World War, because of anti-German sentiment, George V changed the family name to the thoroughly English "Windsor", so it has been the Windsor dynasty ever since.

Key figures: Elizabeth II, Charles III (the current monarch).

In short, whether in the West or the East, rulers and inhabitants anywhere have been made up of many mixed ethnic bloodlines over the course of history, and constitutional-monarchy Britain still combines different peoples and geographic regions in the form of a United Kingdom, each with its own culture and history.

Organising this material gives a more concrete understanding for any future trip to Britain and visits around it, and broadens your own vision and perspective so you can understand more of what is happening in the world, which is very interesting.

＃1 Regarding the rule and influence of different peoples in English history, which of the following statements is incorrect? (A) During Roman rule, Hadrian's Wall was built (B) The Norman Conquest occurred in 1066, begun by William the Conqueror (C) The Anglo-Saxons were descendants of the Vikings from Scandinavia who founded the Tudor dynasty in the 11th century (D) The Celts were the early indigenous people of the British Isles, living mainly in what is now Scotland, Wales and other areas

Answer: (C): The Tudor dynasty was founded by Henry VII, not by the Anglo-Saxons. In addition, the Anglo-Saxons were a Germanic tribe, and although their period overlapped with the Vikings (Nordic pirates), they were not the same people.

＃2 In British history, which dynasty's reign saw the peak of the Industrial Revolution and expanded British territory into the "empire on which the sun never sets"? (A) Tudor (B) Stuart (C) Hanover (D) Windsor

Answer: (C): Queen Victoria belonged to the House of Hanover, and her reign (1837-1901) was the golden age when Britain's national power was strongest and its colonies were widest.
