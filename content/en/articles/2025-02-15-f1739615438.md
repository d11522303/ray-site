A week ago the British Broadcasting Corporation, the BBC, published a report (in the comments) on the shifting global situation. In many respects China's progress has made it a leader across the world's industries, leaving every previously profitable industry feeling threatened. The European car industry is one example. With the Chinese government's support and heavy investment, BYD electric cars entered the world market with unbelievably low prices and quality, and the result is that the century-old foundations of the German and French car industries have been almost completely wrecked.

The same situation will happen in every key industry of "Made in China 2025". Advanced countries will become very nervous and will want to use any legal or political means to slow down the timetable. China wants to be stronger, and the United States even more wants to keep its former glory. It seems President Trump has gone all-in at the table of competition, but has also cut off support from his former allies. In recent days India and Japan have followed, but it seems Britain and the European countries don't think so.

The BBC seems to have honestly presented its observations. And while looking recently for journals and papers on AI and tourism, I also found that more and more cross-disciplinary studies are being published by Chinese scholars in major world-class SSCI journals, especially in fields like the service industry. Since the foundation of services is consumers, the Chinese market of 1.3 billion people is a giant greenhouse for all service start-ups.

The above may just be anxiety from writing proposals. Although my phone has no Tmall, Douyin, DeepSeek or similar apps, I know that the young man who rode the elevator with me just now, from the 18th floor to the ground floor, kept his eyes glued to short videos produced in China until he got out. Throughout, he probably had no idea who was riding the elevator with him, or how many people there were. And the others in the elevator were chatting with each other.
