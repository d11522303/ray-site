---
title: "Taiwan Cares a Lot About Money, and Blames Money for Everything It Can't Do"
excerpt: "A reflection on chasing money for its own sake, and on what is truly worth pursuing: health, ability and happiness, plus giving back to society."
published: true
---

Taiwan cares a lot about whether people have money, and when many things cannot be done, the blame goes to not having enough money.

The result is that wherever there is money, people chase it, no matter what the job or purpose is.

I see many students and friends doing work that does nothing for society or the public. It is just about making money.

For money, they sell high-margin, high-profit products that are not necessities of life, and do nothing to raise quality of life or a sense of beauty.

For money, they use the pursuit of progress and retirement dreams as an excuse, but looked at closely, it is just words and ideas being turned over and over for sale, in order to find followers.

What we should truly pursue in life is physical and mental health, our own abilities, and a sense of happiness. These three have nothing to do with money and nothing to do with comparing ourselves with others. Building on ourselves, a little progress each day is enough to achieve them. While pursuing them, if we also give to society and others, improve our relationships with others, and care about the social environment, starting from family and extending to friends and neighbours, and even making it part of our enterprises and business, it is even better.

So how do we begin?

Let every trade and profession focus on its expertise: students do their studying well, teachers teach with dedication, restaurants value nutrition and safety, and scholars, farmers, workers and merchants all take part, towards the Great Harmony of the Book of Rites.
