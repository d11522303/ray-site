Since the day before yesterday I have kept thinking about my 2014 study trip to Zhengzhou, Luoyang and Dengfeng in Henan. The ancestral tablets in my home bear the hall name "Yingchuan" (穎川), common among Hakka families, which means my family name probably originated from the banks of the Ying River shown at the bottom of the attached picture. I felt it on that trip, and now, in this great disaster, it is even more painful.

There is not much news on Taiwan's television, and not much to read in the material from across the Strait. But it is very clear that at this very moment tens of thousands of families in these Henan cities cannot find their relatives, cannot find help, and cannot see the full picture.

Zhengzhou and Luoyang in Henan lie in the Central Plains and were the capitals of many past Chinese dynasties, but because of the Yellow River's flooding over thousands of years, the land of these two ancient capitals sits lower than the riverbed. Especially in the summer flood season, when this heavy rain is added, the water simply has nowhere to go.

Floods can be natural disasters or man-made. In 1938, during the Sino-Japanese War, the Nationalist army exploited the fact that the Yellow River bed is higher than the ground and chose, strategically, to use the force of nature to resist foreign invasion. It did win space and time as a buffer at that moment of the war, but there is much controversy over the lives of ordinary people and the impact on farming for the following years.

Today, the people and events of World War II have long passed, but the Henan floods have struck again and the people are suffering. I do not much care about Foxconn's Zhengzhou plant; I only care that the fellow villagers I met on the 2014 study trip are all safe and well.
