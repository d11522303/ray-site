---
title: "The 15K Dilemma"
excerpt: "Most students at vocational universities of technology work part-time, earning about NT$15,000 a month, at the cost of study, growth and relationships."
published: true
---

<p>Most of the students in my class have part-time jobs, for one simple reason: money.</p><figure class="ph">Photo</figure><p>This is something a vocational education teacher has to understand first when managing a class. It is more common at private schools than public ones, and at universities of technology than at general universities. I often see students attending class by day and working by night, going to school on weekdays and working on weekends. With that much time spent working, how much can they earn at NT$180 an hour? I asked several students, and they all said that, besides class, they make about NT$15,000 a month on average. That 15K means every working student reports for four hours a day on more than 20 days of the month.</p><p>So for students at vocational universities of technology who work to add 15K to their monthly income, it equals no time to discuss assignments = no time for deep learning = no time to develop themselves = no time for peer groups and clubs = no time to nurture family, friendship, or even love.</p><p>The biggest difference between a vocational university of technology and a general university is the 18-credit, year-long off-campus internship, in which students are placed in a related company for a full year before graduation, as most people in society know. But the University Act requires students to complete at least 128 credits to graduate. When a whole year cannot be spent taking courses on campus and yields only 20 credits, the other 108 credits must be completed during their time at school, averaging 36 credits a year. What society doesn't know, then, is that vocational students' course loads on campus are tighter than at general universities, which squeezes the time they have for part-time work and makes the room and time to earn that 15K even more compressed.</p><p>This is the students' dilemma, the teachers' puzzle, and above all society's reality show. When we see young faces serving us at shop counters after work, perhaps she or he is one of those students who gave up learning languages, taking certifications, joining clubs and making friends for a monthly income of 15K. Give her or him a smile and a thank-you!</p>
