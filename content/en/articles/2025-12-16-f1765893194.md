3. At the end of the 15th century, Columbus's voyages of exploration began a large-scale exchange of species, cultures and populations between Europe and the Americas, called the "Columbian Exchange." Which crop's cultivation most directly promoted the transatlantic slave trade within the Columbian Exchange? ① Corn ② Rice ③ Sugarcane ④ Tomato

Explanation: In the Columbian Exchange, the cultivation of sugarcane most directly promoted the transatlantic slave trade. European colonists established plantations in the Americas and grew large amounts of labour-intensive cash crops such as sugarcane, causing a surge in demand for African slaves and in turn giving rise to the large-scale slave trade to meet the need for labour.
