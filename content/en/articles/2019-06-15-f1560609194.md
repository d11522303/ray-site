Tavocan Learning Journey:

A walking study tour by the Hsing Wu University cross-disciplinary local revitalisation pilot class

A year ago, four students from the Department of Travel Management and the Department of Tourism decided to take a learning journey different from their classmates': the Hsing Wu University cross-disciplinary local revitalisation pilot class.

Along the way I casually asked whether they would recommend that younger students try a different way of learning. Nobody answered me; they only said they would think about it!!

The pilot class students first got to know Xinhua (Tavocan) in Tainan, a place with a beautiful old street. They felt the immediacy of standing on land in Taiwan they had never set foot on before, and walked through renovation examples such as Jinfa Rice Shop, Junjiu Street Village, Jide Chinese Medicine Shop, Xinshengxing Cloth Shop, the Liang Dao Former Residence and Changtai Western Pharmacy. Through the sincere sharing of the local Pickers Studio and the Shanhai Tun social enterprise, and group brainstorming with all five senses, the brain-burning discussions went on. The five working groups quickly compared what they heard with the Linkou Old Street event plan they had once worked on in person, and face to face put forward points that could be tried and evaluated.

Little ideas such as a small cultural village, social media, recording the stories of old shops, short-term youth return programmes and an AR walking experience all impressed the three teachers present, and the local community builders were even more hopeful about the possibility of direct internship placements.

These 36 hours of on-site visits and experiences, north to south, proved once again how important it is to "travel ten thousand miles". But the drive to learn and the ability to think across disciplines are even more important competitive strengths for future workers.

The students still did not answer me, because they have gradually realised that only by doing and thinking can you face the unknown changes and the future. As their teacher, I feel even more that the either/or question no longer applies to them, since they have already moved well beyond it.

And how lucky I am to be able to sit in on the class!
