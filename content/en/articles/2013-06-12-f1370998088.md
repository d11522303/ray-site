Many thanks to Ariel Chen for finding my thesis from over ten years ago. It also keeps reminding me of the statistics classes Professor Wen-Hsien Chou taught for the market research department after work at China Credit Information Service. His solid grounding in statistics and his witty lectures influence me at every moment. It also reminds me of the day I went to Li-Cheng Construction at Taimall Shopping Center to present a research report, and Professor Chou's presence there as a professional defender of the decision-making system.

Later, before I graduated from graduate school, I asked him to serve on my oral examination committee. Instead, Professor Chou had me go to the Department of Business Administration at National Chung Hsing University every week to discuss my thesis with his graduate students. I still remember that in those days I worked at Shinshin during the day, and after my classes at Chinese Culture University I still had to go to the office on Fuxing North Road to attend class and prepare discussion material, then join the seminars with the Chung Hsing business graduate students.

One evening, the professor even brought a table of statistics on names for his newborn little daughter for us all to choose from, hoping to give his precious girl a beautiful and bright name. Professor Chou was my first mentor at work and my co-advisor in graduate school; his effort and achievements are a model for my whole life. In industry he was a professional consultant; in academia he was a rigorous master. With a shelf of publications to his name, he left a lasting example for statistics and business administration.
