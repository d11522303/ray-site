Canada Study Tour Weekly Diary - HWU Travel Management Dreams Come True EP1-1

Today is a Friday typhoon day off in Taipei. While everyone is enjoying the calm before the wind and rain, it is also the last day for the technical-college joint recruitment and recommended-admission application. The application system will close at 17:00 on 7/10, and teachers are still checking whether vocational high school students are stuck in the exasperating admissions system. Messages in the LINE group keep updating.

In British Columbia, Canada, it is now the evening of 7/9. Stephanie Hsiao sent a weekly life diary video. The student's fun record of daily life follows, and you can read along while watching the video.

--------- Stephanie Hsiao's Weekly Diary----------

Following the local pace of life, with help from the boss's wife Tracy, I went to the small convenience store at the gas station to buy basic supplies and food, learning to plan three meals a day on a limited budget. I also tasted a local-flavour ice cream (TIGER flavour) while strolling the neighbourhood, and was lucky to join the community celebration of Canada Day on 1 July. At Reg Conn Park I experienced free hot dogs, a pie-baking contest and toss games, and fun fishing booths, and deeply felt the warm and lively atmosphere of Canada's national day.

Although my English still has room to improve, in daily life and work I can mostly communicate with simple English and body language. Local residents speak fast and often use colloquial expressions, so at first it took time to understand, but everyone was very patient, which gave me more confidence to speak up first, and I realised that actually talking with people is the fastest way to improve a language. Relationships in the town are very close and harmonious. In the afternoon the boss regularly meets and chats with townspeople at the café next door, and this open, easygoing community workplace culture impressed me.

On the first day of work I had to remember the sizes, types and folding methods of many linens. At first it was easy to mix them up, and I worried about making mistakes. So whenever I didn't understand something I asked right away, and practised repeatedly. Staying patient and careful, I gradually became familiar with the workflow, and my work speed improved a lot compared with the first day. This week was mainly about getting familiar with the accommodation and the job. The work was arranged step by step so that I could adapt gradually. The boss and local residents are all very friendly, willing to share life experience and help, which let me fit into the new environment faster, and I look forward to learning more kinds of work later.
