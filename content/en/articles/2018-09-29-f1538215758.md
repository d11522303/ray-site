Yesterday, 28 September 2018, a major earthquake struck Sulawesi, the large island in eastern Indonesia, and the huge tsunami that followed caused damage that will be hard to recover from. Because this is neither Japan nor Korea, our Taiwanese news may report it once and then pay no more attention.

Sulawesi covers 180,000 square kilometres, five times the size of Taiwan and only a little smaller than the island of Great Britain, and it is home to 17 million people, most of them Muslim. The earthquake and tsunami must have had an enormous impact on residents, who mostly live along the coast. The casualty figures now are only the official numbers; many places in this region are hard to contact from the outside, so news of the tragedy may not emerge for months. It is like how students all remember the great earthquake in northeastern Japan, but if I mention the South Asian earthquake or the Sichuan earthquake, which happened only a few years apart, far fewer students know about them.

There are many Indonesian students on the Hsing Wu campus. Indonesia has many ethnic groups, with different languages and economic circumstances, and most of the students on campus now come from cities, whereas Sulawesi is a relatively less developed region. For Taiwanese people, the strongest impression of Indonesia may be the tourist hotspot of Bali. Sulawesi lies just north of Bali, and it is much, much bigger. Perhaps you did not know that before, but now you can take an interest, because who knows when a big earthquake or tsunami like this might strike Taiwan again.
