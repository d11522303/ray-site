<p>The previous article mentioned only one person, Boss Lo. He was indeed the soul of this travel agency. Boss Lo was tall and thin, with deep facial contours and pronounced nasolabial lines, and he used hair oil to sweep his hair up and back into a distinctive style. This was a travel agency, but I never saw him lead a tour, nor did I hear him talk about leading one anywhere. What he did most was sit in his office smoking, receiving guests and making phone calls. People often came and went, and once they entered the door it was closed, and I never knew what they talked about.</p><p>The only time I heard him mention a tour was when a group of customers was going to Singapore and he asked me to see them off at Taoyuan Chiang Kai-shek Airport. Early that morning I rode my scooter to park near Songshan Airport, then took the Kuo-Kuang bus to Taoyuan airport, carrying a schoolbag full of the whole group's passport documents. I went to the airline ground staff at the counter ahead of time to get boarding passes issued for all the customers in a batch, then waited at the airport for the group to arrive and handed the documents to the tour leader. Then I took the Kuo-Kuang bus back to near Songshan Airport to fetch my scooter. I remember that day I also stopped by the former Lion Travel office on Dunhua North Road to collect something from a sales representative.</p><p>The Lion Travel office building was very modern, in the triangular corner lot at the intersection of Minsheng East Road and Dunhua North Road. When I walked into the building on a hot day, I was greeted by an indoor waterfall, cool and classy. I envied the office workers in that Lion Travel building. And I was just a delivery and airport-run boy who came to cool off.</p><p>Later, when I got back to the company on Minsheng East Road, Boss Lo walked up to my little desk, sat in the seat in front, turned around to face me and said: "College student, your English should be pretty good, right? Next time I'll have you lead the Singapore group." I was delighted, because I had never imagined I could go abroad. Working part-time after class every day and during winter and summer breaks, I started fantasising about the chance to go overseas. But in the end, after almost two years of part-time work at Daya Travel Agency, I was still just collecting payments and delivering documents. Indeed, I later learned I was probably the person with the best English in the company, but leading the Singapore group never had anything to do with me.</p><p>One day near noon, I got a call from the company accountant telling me to go to Ximending to collect something, and to remember to bring a rather large backpack. So that day I carried my high school schoolbag, parked my scooter, and followed the address to a mixed-use building. Looking at the floor directory, I learned that the place I was going to was a "red envelope venue". Outside the building were several old men with plastic bags beside them, eating boxed lunches. When the elevator door opened I was in a dance hall, where a shapely singer under flickering lights was singing nostalgic old songs with great feeling, several red envelopes in her hand, while below, on the sofas, several elderly but neatly dressed gentlemen were lost in the music.</p><p>After I explained my purpose to the dance hall attendant, she went into the bar and made a call on the internal line, then signalled me to follow her through a hidden door behind the bar. Past the hidden door was a small office, empty. After going around the office area, we pushed open another hidden door behind a counter, and it turned out to be a suite with a bed. I kept my eyes from wandering as I followed the attendant and opened yet another door. Suddenly it was bright, and I remembered it was high noon. Sunshine was streaming through the windows, and before my eyes was an office with an enormous rosewood chairman's desk. Sitting in the chair was the friend Boss Lo had told me to see. He wore a dark shirt and sat in a chair inlaid with either ivory or boar's tusk, looking exactly like the underworld boss in the movies.</p><p>The boss had me sit on the sofa beneath the window, and asked the attendant to pour me a glass of water, which I actually drank. The boss said: "Boss Lo asked you to come get money. Did you bring a bag?" I said yes, and pointed to the green schoolbag with the four characters for "Affiliated High School of National Taiwan Normal University" on it. The boss laughed, went to a cabinet, and took out stack after stack of green banknotes, piling them on the coffee table and telling me to put them into the schoolbag. I figured it was nearly NT$1 million. It filled the whole high school bag until it became square and boxy.</p><p>The dance hall boss then called Boss Lo to say I was about to head back to the Minsheng East Road office. I shouldered the heavy schoolbag and followed the attendant out of the maze-like rooms, taking one more look at the singer on the stage, who was still earnestly and rapturously singing with her red envelopes in hand. On the way back all I could think was: so much money. I had never seen so much money, and now it was in my schoolbag. With that feeling I returned to the old travel agency.</p><p>Thinking back to this point, was I what is called a "money mule" in today's reports? If I had been stopped by police at a checkpoint on the way back, would I have been taken to the police station for questioning, as in the news stories? How would I have explained that whole bag of cash? What would I have done?</p><p>Recently schools at every level have been running anti-scam campaigns, and I have no shortage of examples around me of people who lost money to scams. When you see money with your own eyes, it really can bewitch you, and the greed and desire hidden in everyone's heart stirs. The students I face every day are now the same age I was then, and they may run into similar part-time work experiences and have to do things they feel they must, just as I did. Without past experience and legal awareness as a foundation, naive college students may think this is society, this is experience, this is learning.</p><p>But it isn't. This is ignorance.</p>
