【International Students】EP1

Yesterday, while having dinner in Guishan, I was recognised by a smiling Vietnamese student who called out "Teacher!" And when settling the bill, he added, "Teacher, your wife is so beautiful!"

As a teacher, I don't pay much attention to the nationality of the students I teach, and I often chat with them. Each international student is far from home, struggling to make a living in a foreign country. Whether for a degree or for money, each one has a future they look forward to. Sometimes, looking at what they share on Facebook, you can see that every child who left home has a family who loves them very much. They often livestream to chat and share photos, and like Taiwanese young people they enjoy their phones, video games and music, love to look good and care about what they wear, just like you and me when we were young.

In fact, I have observed that most international students truly cherish the opportunity to study in Taiwan. In the first year students live in campus dormitories, and from the second year they move off campus to rented flats just like Taiwanese students. But unlike Taiwanese students, international students have strict leave rules, so even in an 8 a.m. class the whole class has 100% attendance. Students who study Chinese seriously can freely choose elective courses in their third year.

Near the end of the pandemic, Taiwan suddenly introduced, as policy, several new kinds of special programmes for international students. They are very different from the familiar approach of the Overseas Chinese University joint admission followed by exams and placement in universities. Many international students now come to study in Taiwan class by class through project applications, and must pass a Chinese proficiency test before graduating.

The Taiwanese government first opened applications for southbound industry-academia programmes at colleges of technology; today all universities may apply, and the organiser has expanded from the Ministry of Education to the Overseas Community Affairs Council. Below is a comparison of the common names and the official names. They look alike, but the devil is in the details, so here is a simple chart of comparisons.

1. Southbound industry-academia programme = (New Southbound Industry-Academia Cooperation International Programme)

2. 1+4 international programme = (International Specialised Division in a key industry field / Key-industry department in a key industry field)

3. New-type programme = (International Industry Talent Education Programme)

4. Overseas Chinese student industry-academia programme = (four-year industry-academia cooperation bachelor's programme / two-year associate degree programme)

5. 3+4 overseas Chinese student industry-academia programme = (Industry-Academia Collaboration Programme for Overseas Chinese Students)

Can you tell the differences among the above? Actually, I can't either, so I used the holiday to compile a table. I've also heard that some of the admission places can count toward a university's enrolment rate (still to be confirmed!). But the increased student numbers and tuition income alone are already a lifeline for private colleges and universities at every level, and an unspoken fact in keeping up the quality of grassroots services everywhere.

If you only say in one sentence that they are here to make money, not to learn, from my viewpoint that is unfair to this group of international students who work and study hard to live. When I was at university I also worked part-time everywhere, and my class attendance was even worse. Looking back, I didn't learn much at university either.

By Ruey-Feng Chen
