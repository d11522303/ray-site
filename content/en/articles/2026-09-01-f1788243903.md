Missing

2002.2.21: Star Princess made her maiden call at Keelung, the first time a 100,000-tonne cruise ship had entered Keelung Port since it opened. Professor Alumi Lu led us master's students on board to see the world, and, as a sideline, I did simultaneous interpreting for wealthy passengers. Over the two days I led two groups of rich guests, and on the second day at noon I even got to enjoy the food in the cruise ship's main dining room. The scene, full of grandeur and gathered dignitaries, is still vivid to me. I recall that Little Pan Pan and Huang Jen-chung were sitting at the next table.

2006.11.2: Helped arrange for the chairman, president and directors of Taiwan Hospitality & Tourism College (THT) to visit Princess Cruises.

2007.9.7-14: At THT's Office of Technical Cooperation, ran a cruise tourism teacher training camp on the Diamond Princess Alaska voyage.

2007.9.14-22: Followed with a THT student programme on Princess Cruises in the US and Canada.

2015.4.27-5.28: In industry-academia cooperation with Carnival (UK) travel agency and Jinjie Travel Agency, ran training for HWU Department of Travel Management students as passenger-service staff for Sapphire Princess's passenger changeovers at Keelung Port. After the internship, Princess Cruises even treated dozens of outstanding students to a meal in the main dining room of the Diamond Princess. Because I gave the teacher's place to students to try something new, I have no record of boarding that day. Did anyone from the Class of 2014 (ROC 103) advisee class keep photos from that year?

Princess Cruises has given me so many firsts! The first time I saw a big ship! The first time I went aboard a cruise ship as an interpreter, the first time I arranged an overseas trip for senior officials, contacted lecturers, trained students, handled the reimbursement and wrote the closing reports for US and Canada visit and cooperation projects. Memories of all these experiences come back to me scene by scene like a film. Over the past decade and more I have organised many courses and activities involving Princess Cruises, yet as the person in charge I have never sailed on a Princess Cruises voyage myself. I always watched wistfully as colleagues and students sailed with Princess, my first-choice brand in my heart, and I truly envied them, but helping many students have their firsts is its own kind of achievement.

Yesterday, 2026.8.31, Princess Cruises' Taiwan agent suddenly announced on its Taiwan website that it was ceasing all service operations in Taiwan! I feel very mixed. Still, perhaps it's fine: to enjoy an authentic cruise holiday you should board at an overseas port, where you can truly feel the diversity of cultures, the rich food and the professional performances of cruise travel. I'll keep working hard and saving, and when I get the chance, I'll take myself and my family on a Princess Cruises voyage.
