title: "Learning Can't Be Rushed: The Story of Hundun Opening Its Seven Apertures"

<p>In my original understanding, "Hundun opening its apertures" (渾沌開竅) meant to suddenly understand something and clear up what had been murky before. But in Zhuangzi's parable, "Hundun" refers to the Emperor of the Centre, who naturally has no eyes, ears, nose or mouth, and the term was later used to mean natural simplicity.</p><p>The Emperor of the South Sea was Shu, the Emperor of the North Sea was Hu, and the Emperor of the Centre was Hundun. Shu and Hu often met in the land of Hundun, and Hundun treated them very well. Shu and Hu planned to repay Hundun's kindness, saying: "All people have seven apertures with which to see, hear, eat and breathe, but he alone has none. Let us try drilling them for him." Each day they drilled one aperture, and on the seventh day Hundun died. [Zhuangzi, "Ying Di Wang" chapter]</p><p>In the parable of Hundun opening its apertures, Zhuangzi tells how Shu and Hu, thinking themselves clever, drilled apertures for Hundun, and as a result Hundun was killed. My son is about the same age as my students. I am not an anxious father, nor a teacher who forces students, and in learning I strongly agree with the way of Zhuang Zhou. Every student in a classroom needs different things. Students are like Hundun in the sea, meeting all kinds of ideas and rules in the ocean of knowledge and expertise, so Hundun need not have apertures, and can feel, think and shape himself through his natural, innate senses.</p><p>So when my students come to campus, I try to offer as many learning resources as I can, let students find me to chat, and give them a safe place to lean on when they are troubled. Naturally, year after year goes by, and perhaps the students will open their own seven apertures and awaken their wisdom.</p><p>Let me give an example of parent-child communication. When my son was in high school, after each evening's cram school we had to drive 20 kilometres home. On the way back I mostly listened to podcasts as I drove, making the daily drive between the basin and the plateau a time for self-learning, mostly interviews and thought-provoking content. I love and cherish this time, just like running for 30 minutes every day, which resets body and mind.</p><p>One night my son, who is usually quiet and resting in the back seat, broke the silence and brought up something he had just heard. He said his classmates were also discussing that the human soul might be antimatter, which current science cannot prove but which does not mean it doesn't exist. And he said he really liked Zhuangzi's view of the universe, a very scientific kind of philosophical thinking.</p><p>I was surprised that, besides the math, physics, chemistry and English he studied at cram school every night, he was discussing Zhuangzi with me. Right then I discussed Zhuangzi's philosophical thinking with him, from the standpoint of fish and humans to dreams and reality, and also discussed my own view of the universe and values. There wasn't much I could discuss with him: I don't know physics or chemistry, my English isn't as good as his, and I'm not a scientist, just a family member who tries to find time to accompany my child, reading and listening to books together.</p><p>If a teacher always thinks students are learning poorly, reacts a lot and meddles everywhere, that is as self-righteous as Shu and Hu drilling apertures. Drilling one aperture a day, perhaps like Shu and Hu who meant to repay a kindness, they may slowly kill off the possibilities of the students' development.</p><p>2026.2.24</p>
