[The Egg Affair, and What Can We Do]

This egg comes from the fridge of Jessie Huang, who has a super cute daughter, was formerly the beautiful girl at the Y17 climbing gym's front desk, and is now a gorgeous Spanish housewife. A few weeks ago, when everyone in Taiwan was looking everywhere for eggs, I was reading articles about eggs and discovered that the management of eggs in the European common market is very different from Taiwan's. So I asked her to take an egg out of her fridge and photograph it.

C. P. (should be the brand)

27-MAR (best-before date, 27 March)

2ES43006 (the 2 means this egg was laid by a barn hen; ES is the country code for Spain)

Since 2004 the EU has required all eggs sold on the market to be marked: DE for Germany, NL for the Netherlands, and so on.

I think the first digit of the mark is the most important, from 0 to 3.

It tells whether the hens that laid the eggs produced our food while living with some rights as chickens.

0: Organic eggs. Indoors, only 6 hens per square metre, with at least 4 square metres of outdoor space for them to roam; the feed the hens eat must also be organically produced and traceable to its source.

1: Free-range eggs. Indoors, only 9 hens per square metre, and there must also be an outdoor area available all day. The minimum outdoor space per hen is 4 square metres.

2: Barn eggs. The hens live in indoor houses with comfortable litter, again with only 9 hens per square metre indoors. They are not kept in cages, but sit on raised platforms in the barn covered with wood shavings.

3: Cage eggs. The minimum space per hen is 750 cm2, roughly 27 by 27 cm.

Before writing this post, the only thing I could read on the egg was the best-before date, 27-MAR. While gathering the material, I kept thinking of Taiwan's hens, spending their whole lives squeezed into tiny cages laying eggs for us, and when they lay less, being culled in droves and turned into salt-water chicken to be sold at Taiwan's night markets. I feel an eggy sadness for Taiwan's hens.

All right, reporting to General Manager Jessie Huang: I finished the article before the egg expired! I wonder if that egg is still in your fridge? Next time I'm back in Taiwan, I'll treat you to salt-water chicken!
