Teacher, Why Didn't You Fight Back?

Late last night this video suddenly appeared online. I found it shocking and had nightmares all night. Was it fear? Perhaps, because I woke from a bad dream and couldn't get back to sleep.

I dreamed that I too was dealing with a conflict between students. In the dream I could clearly see the vicious glares between them. I stepped in between two boys in conflict. One of them was clenching his fists and spewing unspeakable curses, demanding that the other come out and settle it. I saw their hostile eyes and felt the animal violence of young blood running hot, and I felt that perhaps I would have to take a few punches before the students stopped. Then I woke up in a fright.

As a fellow teacher at a private institution, I truly want to pay my respects to this teacher, because in her place I would have hit back long ago. Yet she used all her patience to explain the department's and the teaching's position to the furious students, defending what little dignity remained to her as a teacher. In a baking classroom, a teacher should be making good use of all kinds of equipment to demonstrate professional skills, teaching students hand in hand how to make nutritious and delicious cakes and breads, just like the professional beauty and social responsibility of vocational education described in many well-known lifestyle magazines. Yes, most campuses in Taiwan are like that. The students at the School of Tourism and Hospitality where I work are even more earnest, polite, dedicated and good at working with others, and anyone who has the chance to visit our campus will feel it deeply.

But in the video, this teacher had to stand bravely in front of her students, tidying chairs that had been knocked all over the floor, handling her own emotions and the difficulty of managing the classroom, enduring the students' out-of-control responses, while actively facing their provocation and abuse. Throughout, she tried to guide students not to do wrong or say wrong things, through both her example and her words. She was even asked to leave the classroom first to reduce the chances of anyone doing or saying something wrong, until she walked out of her classroom, her lecture hall, her kitchen, leaving behind a group of self-righteous students with no upbringing.

I think she must have been afraid too, especially when students' chairs were flying straight at her head. All the while she was defending her teaching space. A classroom has a sacredness about learning. We teachers are ordinary people who get tired, lazy and annoyed, but as soon as we step onto the podium our role is to pass on the way, impart knowledge, resolve doubts and teach according to each student's aptitude.

Choosing to be a teacher may mean having a bit of counselling skill, a bit of conscience and a bit more kindness.

But teachers don't come to campus to solve students' social affairs, family difficulties, emotional troubles or physical fights, let alone to risk their personal safety. On campus there are teachers actively refining their teaching skills for their students' learning, teachers who have long cared for students and built family-like bonds with them, and teachers who worry that the pressure of entrance exams overworks both students and teachers and who find every way to squeeze time out of busy administrative duties to counsel students again. This is the everyday life of teachers.

As a fellow vocational teacher, I once again offer my highest respect and concern to this university teacher in Keelung.

These days "fight the teacher" has replaced the classic three-word Taiwanese curse as a joke made behind people's backs in many settings. On campus, teachers sometimes face students who have long lost interest in learning, silent classrooms, and even, as the video showed, maliciously threatening words and out-of-control physical conflict. As a vocational teacher myself, I watched the video and could not sleep all night.

Turning this profession into a vocation is much harder in today's social climate than in the past, and even more so for private school teachers, who lack full job protection. I hope the school will provide legal assistance, so that teachers are not left as victims on campus. The one cursing was not only a student but also an adult over 18 who must take responsibility for his improper behaviour and words. And do not let the perpetrator be inexplicably protected on campus in the name of the right to an education.

Don't let teachers leave in helpless disappointment, like the uproar at the end of the video, as the classroom is taken over by the students...
