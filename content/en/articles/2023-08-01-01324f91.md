title: "Suddenly Discovering My Insurance Policies Have Loans!?"
excerpt: "How my elderly mother's policies were quietly borrowed against online, what policy loans really are, and how to repay them."
published: true
---

<p>If you have never taken out a policy loan and confidently feel this has nothing to do with you, I still recommend spending three minutes on this article, because one day you may be forced to deal with a policy loan, just as I was.</p><p>The day it happened, my younger brother sent a phone screenshot into our family group chat.<br/>"Big brother, Mom's policies have more than NT$2 million in policy loans! What do we do?"</p><p>I have already handled countless family problems, so although my heart tightened, I was not surprised. When the wind comes, block it; when the water comes, flood through it. What else can you do?<br/>"Send me Mom's app login and password first, and I'll think about how to handle it."</p><p>Only after logging into the online app did I find that, within the past six months, my family member had tapped a few buttons online to apply for loans and borrowed against many policies she had built up over several decades, almost all at the maximum amount. When I asked my elderly relative, whose cognition is already unclear and who forgets things, the answer was almost always: "Did I borrow? I don't know?"</p><p>This has happened most often in recent years to relatives who have just retired. The reason is mostly that they received financial information or investment proposals and, encouraged by others, used online policy loans. When I asked in detail where the money went after the loans, the answers were vague and muddled, and the large sum of retirement security and the protections it provided were on the verge of being lost.</p><p><b>If you have read this far and have a similar problem, please send me a private message and let's work it out together.</b></p><p><u><b>Is a policy loan that serious?</b></u></p><p>Actually, not so much. Many people assume that a policy loan is like a personal credit loan: if you borrow and don't repay, debt collectors will chase you and your car and house may be seized. In fact, <b>a policy loan will not result in debt collection, and you can even choose not to repay it</b>.</p><p><u><b>Why can you borrow money from a policy?</b></u></p><p>Because that is the total premiums the policyholder (the technical term is "applicant") has paid over many years, so <b>the money you can borrow through a policy loan is really your own money</b>. Because your policy has not yet matured, the clever insurance companies designed a money game that lets you borrow your own premiums from your own account, and they charge you 2% to 7% interest on it.</p><p><u><b>How do you repay a policy loan?</b></u></p><p>There are many ways to repay. The most important point is that, because interest is calculated, <b>the repayment amount differs every day</b>. If you are comfortable with online technology, you can go to the insurance company's website or download its app to get that day's repayment account directly. You can set the repayment amount anywhere from interest only up to the full balance. If you have spare cash, of course repay it all so that the policy can return to its proper role as savings and protection.</p><p>Finally, I still recommend not borrowing against your policies. If you have no choice, plan when you will repay at the same time you borrow, because this is money the present you is borrowing from the future you. If you cannot repay it, besides the money disappearing, the good intentions you had when you bought the insurance, such as medical care and a dignified end of life, will all become passing clouds.</p>
