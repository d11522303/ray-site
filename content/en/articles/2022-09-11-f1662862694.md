Weekend Reading: How Did Japan Win the Traffic War? (Article in the Comments)

In Japan, an average of 2.24 pedestrians per 100,000 people die each year in road crashes, versus 12.7 in the United States. Forty years ago, 41% of American children walked to school; now only 13% do.

The article discusses how Japan designed urban environments suited to pedestrian life. At a time when we can't travel to Japan as tourists, we can think about how to reshape Taiwan's environment into a good living environment that respects pedestrians and passers-by.

For reference, Taiwan had 2,962 traffic fatalities in 2021. In descending order: Kaohsiung (330), Tainan (293), Taichung (280), Taoyuan (264), New Taipei (264) and Taipei (130). There is a clear geographic pattern. Why?

I personally love walking. Anywhere I can walk, I walk, and if not, I ride a YouBike. My son walked to and from school by himself all through elementary and junior high school. We did worry, but he still had to learn how to survive in Taiwan's frightening traffic environment.

Across Taiwan, there is hardly any decent environment where pedestrians can walk safely and smoothly.

Taiwan's sidewalks suffer three plagues: scooters, stray dogs and electrical boxes.

But the worst of all: there is no sidewalk at all along the road.
