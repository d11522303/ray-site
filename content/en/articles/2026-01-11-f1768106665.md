Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep, Episode 37

#TourLeaderExam62DaysToGo

8 The student movement in Taiwan in 1990 that demanded the "dissolution of the National Assembly" and the "abolition of the Temporary Provisions" refers to which of the following? (A) The Defend the Diaoyutai Movement (B) The Wild Lily Movement (C) The Wild Strawberry Movement (D) The Sunflower Movement

In fact, from Taiwan's cultural movements to the recent Sunflower Movement, peaceful political reform has taken place without bloodshed. Besides the actions of the key figures in these important movements, the conscience of those in power and their comprehensive consideration of diverse viewpoints or of governance were also common factors in social movements succeeding as agents of social transformation. So putting only social movements and figures into the questions of a national exam for tourism licences is not appropriate. I think that if questions must be set, they could be set in the following way, which would actually help with tourism resources for Chinese-language tour guides.

#1 Which of the following important buildings in Taiwan was once the venue for the 1935 Taiwan Exposition and for the signing of the surrender agreement between Japan and the Nationalist government and the handing over of the surrender document? (A) Taipei Zhongshan Hall (B) The Presidential Office Building (C) The Taiwan Museum (D) Taipei Guest House

#2 The Legislative Yuan, which was occupied by students for nearly a month during the Sunflower Movement, is an important historic building. Which of the following statements is incorrect?

(A) The present Legislative Yuan administration building was originally a remaining building of Taipei Second Girls' High School

(B) The Legislative Yuan site has only been used by the Legislative Yuan since 1960

(C) The Legislative Yuan building has been used as our country's legislative body ever since it was built in 1927

(D) The Legislative Yuan administration building and chamber have been listed by the Taipei City Government as monuments or historic buildings
