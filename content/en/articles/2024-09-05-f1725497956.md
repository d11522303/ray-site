Is It Inappropriate for Vocational Teachers to Take University Entrance Exams Again?

With the falling birth rate coming, private school teachers have no pension, and after nearly thirty years of work, monthly retirement income is under NT$20,000. If I were thinking only of myself and had the ability to pass the exams, I would also like to try for a medical, dental, Chinese medicine or veterinary degree! It's just that today's GSAT questions aren't easy. Everyone should give them a try if they get the chance. What high school students are tested on now is much harder than before, with more depth and breadth and more emphasis on competency.

So we teachers go for certificates or learn skills. Take the dean of our college, who holds all kinds of certificates from plumbing and electrical work to professional driving, and even works hands-on in these fields. He has also earned a doctorate from a leading domestic university, a model of the vocational master. I fall a bit short; I have taken exams while working for various tour leader and tour guide licences and language certificates. I have also tried learning housekeeping work and would like to learn long-term care, figuring that when the tsunami comes, I should at least have a job to make a living.

But have you ever done housekeeping? Years ago, when I was in the hospitality department at Taiwan Hospitality & Tourism College (THT), I asked students to teach me how to make a bed. After just a few beds my back hurt. After cleaning a few bathrooms and rooms, my hands were sore, my feet numb and my shoulder joints so sore that I couldn't lift my arms. And in practice, housekeeping carries time pressure: you must finish a dozen or more rooms within 6-7 hours, from changing beds, carpets and windows to, of course, the bathrooms and the rubbish that need the most attention. You do the same tasks over and over, with occasional pressure from guests or colleagues in between. In 2024, HR at star-rated hotels say their housekeepers start at over NT$30,000 a month. But if you were unemployed, would you consider it?

As a vocational teacher in hospitality, I might. But at first my stamina could only handle half the workload, about one room an hour or a bit more. I might improve, but I would more likely wear myself out until I'm injured and report sick, or even suffer an occupational injury and be forced to quit.

Now I ask hotel operators in return: would you want to hire middle-aged and older employees who are starting over?

Business is business. To meet the KPI of 12-14 rooms per housekeeper, a new middle-aged hire would need twice the staff management, so converting the KPI to pay would actually mean NT$60,000-70,000. No wonder that even when we are willing, employers think it over carefully.

So I think vocational teachers earning useful degrees while keeping their student status is worth encouraging, and I would take that as a model and try hard myself. As for schools and media blowing the issue of degree quotas out of proportion, that seems rather inappropriate.
