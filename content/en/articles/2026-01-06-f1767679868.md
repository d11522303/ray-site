#TourLeaderExamCountdown67Days

46 Taiwan is currently not on UNESCO's World Heritage List, but it has many natural landscapes and cultural assets with World Heritage potential. Which of the following is one of Taiwan's World Heritage potential sites? ① Tainan Confucius Temple ② Lintianshan Forestry Village ③ Taoyuan Plateau Ponds ④ Sun Moon Lake Thao Culture

Explanation:

Taiwan currently has 18 World Heritage potential sites, covering natural and cultural landscapes, including natural landscapes such as Yushan, Taroko, the Alishan Forest Railway and the Tatun Volcano Group, and cultural and historical sites such as Fort San Domingo in Tamsui, the Shuijinjiu mining sites, the Kinmen/Matsu battlefield culture, the Lanyu settlements and the Peinan archaeological site. But some of them are pretty lame, and this question is about exactly the one I think doesn't deserve to be there 🤣

In class I often say that the site I consider most qualified for Taiwan's listing as a dual natural and cultural World Heritage site is the Alishan giant tree groves and mountain railway system in Chiayi. There is only one, no second.

Recently Guinness World Records listed the Dulishan Spiral of the Alishan Forest Railway as the world's longest railway spiral, well deserved.

The following is the Guinness World Records summary of the Longest railway spiral:

One of the most structurally complex sections of railway in the world is the Dulishan Spiral. It is part of the Alishan Forest Railway, a narrow-gauge tourist railway, first built in 1912, originally developed by the Japanese colonial government in Taiwan for the timber-logging industry.

The straight-line distance between the start and end of the Dulishan Spiral is only 570 metres, but the difference in height between them is 233 metres. So trains travelling between the two settlements of Zhangnaoliao and Shibijiao must run along a winding route 5.1 kilometres long to overcome this terrain of short distance and very large height difference.

Ordinary adhesion railways (which run relying only on friction between steel wheels and steel rails) face limits on steep slopes, with a maximum gradient of about 9% (about 1 in 11). So when a railway must cross rugged terrain, it is often designed with "loops" or "spirals". Such designs lengthen the route and combine tunnels and viaducts to ease sharp changes in elevation. Famous examples include the Horseshoe Curve in the Allegheny Mountains of Pennsylvania in the United States and the Raurimu Spiral on the North Island of New Zealand.

The Alishan Forest Railway itself has many special designs to cope with the terrain, including many switchbacks, in which trains zig-zag up and down the slope, and several loop routes. Yet even among the many special engineering works, the Dulishan Spiral remains the most representative section. This section has 11 tunnels, and the track crosses over or under the mountain itself as it goes up to 10 times, fully demonstrating its highly complex and ingenious engineering design.
