Yesterday I ate at Ohtoya at past 5 pm. Because of the heat at street level, quite a few people were dining in the Taipei Main Station underground mall. I saw that the restaurant had a privacy area screened off by curtains for the staff's break between shifts, so they could rest properly. I was very envious.

At work I normally sit in a big office without even partitions. It is called the faculty research office, but it is really a space with 16 computer desks lined up in rows, where teachers can only use their monitors and books to mark off a working space. Between classes, if I want to nap, I feel too embarrassed to lay my head down, so I put up with it, wash my face, and come back to grade assignments or prepare lessons, and a dozen or so minutes of free time slip away.

Often I have to wait until the students have finished class and gone home and my colleagues have all left, and only then can I doze at my desk without being startled by a student suddenly walking in.

In many of Taiwan's elementary and junior high schools, homeroom teachers' desks are at the back of the classroom. They have no office of their own. Teachers go to school, attend class and mark assignments together with the children, arriving before 8 am, watching over cleaning and keeping order and the children's interactions, even sitting in on the subject teachers' lessons. After the students leave at five, you often see the teacher alone in the classroom, working.

But perhaps I understand: that is when the teacher finally has time and space to themselves.
