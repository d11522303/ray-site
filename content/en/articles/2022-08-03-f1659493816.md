I enlisted as a Private Second Class at Dalin, Chiayi, on 20 September 1995, and was discharged as a Private First Class on 23 July 1997. During the Taiwan Strait missile crisis, new recruits did nothing but drill, and I was drilled until I wanted to sign up for three and a half years of voluntary service!

After basic training at Dalin came the assignment lottery, and those with no connections had to rely on luck. When the man who slept in the next bed in my intake drew the Gaodeng Command on Beigan (black-and-white image 2, Zone 6), his face fell on the spot. Not long after, I received a letter from him sent from the Beigan command, from the 73rd Group, every word written in blood and tears.

Compare the three images. Starting tomorrow may be a turning point in history, or it may pass quietly just like the Taiwan Strait crisis 27 years ago. In the barracks we didn't know what was happening outside. Were our families worried? Did we ever think we would go to war?

In fact at that moment we were just privates in olive-green uniforms, holding guns and drilling, counting steamed buns while looking at the freedom beyond the perimeter wall. Did we have the right to choose?

Last night I parked next to the Legislative Yuan. When I went to fetch the car after cram school, the area had been fenced off with no parking allowed. All the way home I discussed with my son the relations among China, the US and Taiwan, and how Taiwanese, born and raised here, should position themselves and develop on this island. When we got home my son posted a video story on Instagram, and he found that apart from his current high school classmates, nobody liked it.

I hope for peace. We can bend at the waist, but we will not bow our heads.
