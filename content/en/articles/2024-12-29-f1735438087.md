---
title: "In 2024, No National University Had a Registration Rate Below 80%"
excerpt: "Ray analyses 2024 university registration rates: science and technology schools fill up, hospitality schools struggle, and the figures are a weak guide to quality."
published: true
---

In the 2024 academic year (ROC year 113), no national university had a registration rate below 80%.

The ones with 100% enrolment are the schools strong in information, electrical and science engineering: Tsing Hua, Cheng Kung, Chiao Tung and Central.

The overall share of Taiwan's service industry has clearly dropped this year, as the industrial balance tilts toward the technology sector.

Needless to say, Taiwan Tech and Taipei Tech enrol 100%, but Pingtung University of Science and Technology and the National Kaohsiung University of Hospitality and Tourism are both around 80%, and one should be careful. Vocational teachers drawing national salaries need not worry, but it also means transferring into these two schools in 2025 is convenient, so everyone can take that as a reference.

National Kaohsiung University of Hospitality and Tourism was once proud to be the leader of vocational tourism education, but in 2024 its registration rate did not even reach 80%. This shows that parents and students are expressing, in the most direct way, that studying tourism, hospitality and services does not earn money. Moreover, today's students have a growing sense of regional preference and freedom, and asking students to wear uniforms to school every day in Xiaogang, Kaohsiung, means quite a few might transfer after a year.

The veterinary department at NPUST will certainly fill its quota, but other departments are probably worse off. As for rural areas, enrolment is poor regardless of public or private, and one can imagine that local students only want to study in big cities, while city students are even less likely to study in the countryside. Universities set up in the countryside will in future have to recruit international students to make up their intake.

I suggest private universities' enrolment rates be taken as a reference only, because the numbers can be adjusted and controlled. Just watch for extreme values: do not go to a school below 60%, or you will later have to transfer, which is very troublesome.

This year, several universities that adjusted their enrolment quotas and were warned by the Ministry of Education ended up with numbers that did not look bad either; you can find which ones with a quick Google, and all their presidents have been replaced. Wei Chia-hui, CEO of the higher education information platform "University Questions", analysed that the main reasons this year's overall registration rate for colleges and universities is somewhat better than last year are:

1. Schools increased their deferred-enrolment quotas

2. International students are counted in the registration rate

3. The Ministry of Education subsidises private-school students' tuition and fees

So this means the registration rate is no longer sufficient as a basis for judging a school's quality when students choose.
