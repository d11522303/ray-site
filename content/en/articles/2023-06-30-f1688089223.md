[2023 Higher Education Sprout Project, Phase 2: General Universities]

The Higher Education Sprout Project, the five-year competitive programme that Taiwan's higher education sector cares about most, has quietly been announced on the Ministry of Education's website, with some celebrating and others worrying.

The 2023 (ROC year 112) subsidies for the 72 general universities (see attached table) total NT$12.6 billion. Only 4 universities, including National Taiwan, National Cheng Kung, National Tsing Hua and National Yang Ming Chiao Tung, receive more than NT$1 billion, while 22 universities receive more than NT$100 million a year.

In truth, the Sprout Project is the small item. The big money is in the Ministry of Education's annual budget, with nearly NT$300 billion set aside in 2023 to invest in Taiwan's education environment. Higher education takes over NT$100 billion of that. Compared with the all-important 12-year national basic education, I can hardly judge whether the funding for Taiwan's higher education is reasonable (the report is in the comments)! But in 2023 about 200,000 children entered primary school in Taiwan, while the universities on the list are down to fewer than 100,000 new students. Even adding the new students at universities of science and technology, the total does not reach 200,000.

Everyone lately has been focused on the NT$35,000 tuition subsidy for private universities. As a private-school teacher, I am truly happy for my students, but as a citizen I cannot feel happy about it.
