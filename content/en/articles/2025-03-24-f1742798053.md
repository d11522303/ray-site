2025.3.24

Little Spring Weather

First thing this morning, my son at National Tsing Hua University took part in a general education course on the campus ecology. But because Monday's logic design course and exam content are huge, many students did not go home over the weekend and even studied all night, including him, and the 5 am campus excursion was merely a general education snack after their night of study.

I have watched children of the same age around me study at Johns Hopkins in the US. The coursework she shows is also heavy, with a great deal of reading and homework, and the tuition and living costs her parents pay are even more staggering than in Taiwan, but after graduating from a famous school and department, the pay at work and so on offers another vision.

Why is the coursework so heavy in the electrical engineering department of a top university?

For this department, is such a huge workload a necessary condition for maintaining the quality of its electrical engineering students?

Is learning in the electrical engineering departments at UCLA or MIT this intense too?

If you hunger for success and want to be above others, you cannot dodge hard work during the learning stage.

My post on Threads yesterday, a bit of fishing for reactions, worked very well, and I could see different responses and views, all very valuable. It seems MediaTek and Novatek are the same as Realtek in how they manage and develop their people: the engineers work very hard, and what they put in is worth the income. Those who see others doing well but cannot do it themselves are people envious of those who earn money, and my inference is that they may also be the sour-grapes type. Using quality of life and PUA as an excuse to avoid facing things is perfectly legitimate; but looking back, it is just not being able to compete or compare with others in economics and position, and ending up a keyboard warrior.

My overall takeaway is that when students choose any industry or department to study in, if they base their decision only on grade rankings, career promotion or income, they put their sense of achievement in the wrong place. The most important thing during university is not competing for department or class rank or chasing the book prize, so I very much disagree with how the Realtek manager described and introduced this job. It is a pity that, from his position and height, his introduction to first-year freshmen did not consider what this job means for being a human being.

My child chose electrical engineering at NTHU, and in terms of personality there is nothing wrong with that. The pity is that in how this department is run, apart from the professional content, teachers give students too little guidance, and the result is that market value is left to shape students' learning strategies. Ages 18 to 24 are the most beautiful time of life. Teachers should use their own experience to guide young and clever students to try and explore themselves and discover where they can gain lasting achievement, then refine themselves in the subjects, research or skills they love, pursue excellence in the fields of their strengths and passions, and set small goals for their subject or profession, continually gaining a sense of achievement in the process of learning and exploring, and so become Taiwan's most professional scientific talent.

A lovely spring morning

Youthful days without regret
