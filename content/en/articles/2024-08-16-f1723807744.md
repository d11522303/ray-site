2024.8.16

All stages of university admission (special talent selection, the Star Plan recommendation, GSAT individual application, and the subject tests) finally ended yesterday with the subject-test results. Having accompanied my child through three years of the 108 curriculum as a parent, I have a few thoughts. They are purely personal opinions, just a record.

1. The learning portfolio is generally a JOKE: in the first and second years of high school I attended many learning portfolio and talent selection briefings as a parent. If your high schooler has no special learning direction, the learning portfolio the Ministry of Education has been promoting is only a facade. Just fill it in.

2. Class ranking matters a lot for the Star Plan and GSAT individual application. But it has little effect on special talent selection and the subject tests, so high schoolers should find the route that suits them and use the system strategically to get into their preferred departments.

3. GSAT scores are important, especially Chinese, English and Math B, which are only tested once. If you don't have time to study everything, push to do your best in these three first, so you don't lose any chance of a comeback in the later subject tests.

4. High schoolers should list the subjects emphasised by the departments they are most interested in and go all in on those, so they stand in a favourable position at both the GSAT individual application and subject-test stages.

5. Finally, high school is an important stage of character formation. Good learning and living habits may matter even more than good scores, but I personally feel teachers play a small role here. The influence of family, classmates and school atmosphere is the key.

Accompanying my child through three years of study felt like living through high school again, especially since my own high school life was very bitter. I am grateful to my child for giving me a feeling of rebirth.
