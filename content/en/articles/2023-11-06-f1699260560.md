<p>Which bank does everyone go to for currency exchange before going abroad?</p><p>If I have time before going abroad, I will queue at Bank of Taiwan to save NT$100 in fees; but if I don't have time, I just exchange at Bank of Taiwan or Mega Bank at the airport, which is time-saving and safe.</p><p>But thirty years ago it seems it wasn't like this, because our travel agency always had people coming to exchange foreign currency. As soon as someone came in and asked if we wanted to change money, we would answer: look for Miss Chen in accounting! Miss Chen was the sister who felt the least old in that old travel agency. In fact, besides being the accountant of Boss Lo's company, she also served as an OP, and what she always had on her lips was: "Ah, work is just like this, busy, busy, and a day is gone."</p><p>What is an OP? In plain terms it is a travel agency product operations staffer: handling clients' documents and applications, collecting payments, booking flights with airlines and hotel rooms, and liaising with local travel agencies (LOCALs for short). This kind of work is mostly called OP, and for Japan routes there is another term: "tehai" (arrangements). The actual work is about the same. So this is the position in a travel agency that goes out the least, spending most of the time on the phone with clients in the office and sorting the documents collected by the runner (me).</p><p>But our company was strange. Miss Chen often sent me to change money, not at Bank of Taiwan or ICBC (International Commercial Bank of China), but on the second floor of a four-storey street-front building in an alley in Wanhua. As I remember it, I rode straight from Minsheng West Road to Yanping North Road, and after passing Beimen I continued along the Zhonghua Market, browsing as I rode past all eight rows named Loyalty, Filial, Benevolence, Love, Faith, Righteousness, Harmony and Peace. Suddenly, after Zhonghua Road Section 2, the cityscape turned quiet, and I turned right onto Guangzhou Street, going straight past Longshan Temple, and after crossing Xiyuan Road, a few alleys further I turned in and found a spot at the roadside on the ground floor to park my little white scooter. I walked to a corner shopfront beside which was a long staircase leading up to the second floor, the third floor and a vaguely existing fourth floor. On the second floor there was a wooden door on the right, behind which voices bustled, very lively, and as an old hand I turned the handle and went in.</p><p>Accountant Miss Chen often had me look for another Miss Chen, the second-floor Miss Chen. She was very young, at least younger than our accountant Miss Chen. When the second-floor door opened, you could see a whole rectangular room with five or six tables, each with a young woman sitting before it. The tables were divided by the foreign currencies exchanged, each table a different currency exchange area: US dollars, Japanese yen, Korean won, Thai baht, Malaysian ringgit and the francs, pounds and so on of the European countries. Miss Chen exchanged US dollars, and I went to her most often. When she saw me she always had a smile and asked all sorts of things: Which school? What major? Don't you have class? Do you have a girlfriend? Since I was just a very quiet university student, I answered a few casual words to get acquainted.</p><p>In those days I always carried, in my high school backpack, the New Taiwan dollars Boss Lo had collected to the second floor to exchange for US dollars, and the heavy bag became much lighter after the exchange. A few times I also went to collect documents at the nearby Dali Street, the gangland street corner. During the semester I also had to ride across Huajiang Bridge and Xinhai Bridge back to Fu Jen University in Xinzhuang for class, and besides the US dollars, my bag also held my textbooks and notes, so I was often in a rush because the teachers took attendance, and with group discussion courses it was even worse, impossible to skip. Often bundle after bundle of US dollars, passports and documents went along with me to class, after class and to student association activities, and perhaps only the next day did they return to the old travel agency on Minsheng East Road to be handed to our accountant Miss Chen.</p><p>Looking back now, was this money laundering?</p><p>That second-floor foreign exchange place not far from Longshan in Wanhua: however I think about it, I can no longer recall its exact location.</p>
