2025.7.24

In week three of the course we finally reached colour mixing and use in watercolour. This 12-colour set is a souvenir from our family trip to Germany in 2016. I was determined to one day use this box, which only needs water to colour, for my travel sketches. At first I could not even mix the blue of the sky. I kept feeling that the blue in the lower-left corner was the right one, but once I added water and tried it, it was just off.

The key to watercolour is controlling colour and the amount of water. If the colour is not accurate, you lose the flavour; if there is not enough water, there is no saturation or harmony, and in the end it is very hard to render moving clouds in the sky, let alone Mt. Fuji sitting at the centre of the picture.

Later, when I checked the original name, I learned it was Cyan Blue, not Blue. Cyan and sky blue are different. Cyan has a green undertone and can be used for lake water or scenery, but it is not suitable for the blue sky in this picture of Mt. Fuji. Only later did I notice that the dark blue in the upper right of the twelve colours turns into a sky blue once water is added. The experiment took at least 30 minutes, but flowing among the colours and thinking them through was very healing.
