The Dajia Mazu Pilgrimage: The Story of the Scholar's Pen Bearer

At the start of the semester I overheard a colleague in the next seat in the faculty office say that over the summer break they had walked the Camino de Santiago in Spain with their family, and I was so envious. That day I wrote in my diary: this year I must walk with Dajia Mazu again. But with life busy and moving so fast, how could I possibly find the time?

Where there's a will, Mazu will bless.

A few days ago I suddenly got word that I had to be at Providence University in Taichung early on the weekend morning for the general meeting of the Chinese Society of Tourism Management. I arranged my time right away. In fact I only got home at almost 11 p.m. on Friday night, and drove back to Taichung early the next morning. After the meeting ended at noon, I heard professors chatting: Dajia Mazu "today" was returning to her temple along the coastal route, and I could go and see it nearby.

I was delighted and checked Dajia Mazu's location right away. The palanquin was heading north from Longjing and would pass through Shalu to Qingshui. After lunch I immediately made arrangements to go and greet the procession. Because I had seen the astonishing crowds of believers five years ago, I first found the Shatian parking lot near Shalu Station to park the car, deciding to act ahead of time. In my delight, a professor from Providence told me once more that I absolutely had to find the Thirty-Six Officers, and even asked whether I had a pen with me, since my child happened to be taking the university entrance exams. The Thirty-Six Officers of Dajia Mazu's ceremonial guard consist of 12 colour banners and 24 weapons, and the Scholar's Pen Bearer is one of the 24 weapons. It is said that touching the Scholar's Pen Bearer blesses you with success in the exams and academic progress.

Of course I did. I came here to attend a meeting, so how could I not have a pen? I even had a pen case with me.

I drove into the small town of Shalu for the first time, deeply moved. Five years ago, after I walked the Changhua section, the pandemic, and family matters above all, made it hard to find the time. At the beginning of this year I saw that a travel agency partnering with our department for internships had arranged a one-day chartered shuttle for walkers, and I had thought of arranging it too, but I kept thinking about it without acting, and the days went by one after another without my noticing.

Along the coastal highway, with an endless dragon of people, I quietly walked south alone against the flow. Watching each believer queuing to crawl under the palanquin, I remembered my grandmother once saying she had also accompanied Dajia Mazu along this road. I wonder whether she crawled under the palanquin back then. If she did, she must also have been praying for her family's safety and health.

After the Scholar's Pen Bearer blessed it, my pen case travelled a long way with the Thirty-Six Officers. I basked in the not-too-hot Taichung sun and felt the cool breeze blowing from the mountains and the sea, thinking about my mission, my prayers and the blessings heaven has given me. So many coincidences brought me back onto the road of worship. At a different time but in the same place, my grandmother and I have both personally experienced the miracle of Mazu's procession, and felt the emotion brought by the pilgrimage road of her loving protection of all beings.
