Where You Land: 2024 University Application in the Recreation and Sports Cluster

A letter arrived at home recently, saying it contained extremely valuable information and should be opened with care. Opening it, I found it was a university application placement analysis from Taipei Ru-Lin. I have attached a screenshot of the GSAT (General Scholastic Ability Test) admission standards for the universities and departments in the Recreation and Sports cluster. I wonder whether everyone can make sense of it.

1. You need to understand what [Top], [Front], [Average], [Back] and [Bottom] mean.

2. How the screening multiplier is calculated.

3. The GSAT tests six subjects: Chinese, English, Math A, Math B, Natural Sciences (physics, chemistry, biology, earth science) and Social Studies (civics, history, geography). But that is misleading. In fact, it tests a full eleven subjects.

4. The English listening test is a chicken rib (hardly worth bothering with); ignore it.

---- Please follow along with the chart below ----

Pick a department you would like to study in. For example, take National Taiwan University's International Sports Bachelor's Programme. It has 5 places for high school applicants and takes the most subject tests of any department in this cluster: Chinese, English, Math B and Social Studies. So a student hoping to apply to NTU's sports programme needs 52.3 in Chinese, 43 in English, 62.3 in Social Studies, and an average-standard score in Math B. First screen by Chinese at a multiplier of 8 times the places, which is 40 students. From these 40, screen again with English at a multiplier of 5 to find the top 25 in English, and they will have a chance to enter the second stage of the GSAT application, with document review and interview.

But most departments in the cluster take only one subject. Only NTU cares more, so don't worry.

A quick introduction to how GSAT scores map to university application: [Top], [Front], [Average], [Back] and [Bottom]. These five levels are graded in steps of 12.5%. The Top standard is the top 12.5% of test takers, the Average standard is the score at 50%, and the Bottom standard is the lowest 12.5%.

Here are the scores that each standard represented in the 2023 (ROC year 112) GSAT:

Chinese, full marks 100 (general knowledge 50, essay 50)

[Top 62.7] [Front 57.5] [Average 52.3] [Back 41.8] [Bottom 36.6]

English, full marks 100 (multiple choice and fill-in 72, translation and essay 28)

[Top 73.5] [Front 61.5] [Average 43] [Back 25] [Bottom 18.5]

Social Studies, full marks 144 (civics, geography, history)

[Top 86.3] [Front 78.5] [Average 62.3] [Back 54.9] [Bottom 39.3]

All right, writing this has made me dizzy too. Anyway, the conclusions are:

1. Students who want to take the Recreation and Sports cluster are under little pressure; reach the Bottom standard in just one subject and there is a university for you.

2. The English Bottom standard is 18.5 points; guessing C or D on everything should be enough to reach it.

3. For all the subjects tested in the GSAT, in fact only top universities' departments care.

4. And where, then, are the multiple electives and learning portfolios that high schoolers worked themselves nearly to death on?

The above is just a high-school student's dad grumbling......
