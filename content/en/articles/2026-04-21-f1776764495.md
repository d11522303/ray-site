Game Learning EP1: Crossing Pushige

Today, as in mid-March before the tour guide and tour leader exam, I used the "#WalkThroughTaiwan" board game from the Mini Educational Game Design team of National Taiwan University of Science and Technology with students after class, for a relaxing and brainy review of Taiwan's tourism resources. Both teachers and students felt it was really effective. Several of the students who played then may bring good news of making the pass list at the end of this April, and I am looking forward to it~

Today we invited graduate student Mr. Hao-An Yu of the NTUST Mini Educational Game Design team (NTUST MEG), who brought his own unreleased board game [#CrossingPushige] so we could use our brains and explore the tourism resources of Yuli, Hualien together with the students. In the game the students learned more about the local travel resources, and used the game strategy to think further and plan trips. Everyone felt the two hours of afternoon time passed in a flash in game-based learning.

Although domestic travel has been hard to win over consumers in recent years, after playing the board game the students instead planned to make a special trip to Yuli in a future holiday. Whether for relaxing and switching off, local food, the daylily flowers of Chike, hot spring cuisine, old trails or small-town visits, they felt the game already let them imagine an interesting itinerary for themselves and for family travelling together. If you are interested, join the [#WaitAMomentRuey-Feng] game learning group in the comments section.

Class is not only play, but also learning and growth.

In Hsing Wu University's Department of Travel Management teaching practice, we are gradually seeing students learn content useful for their future through encouragement and stimulation. In vocational education, we start learning motivation from games, extend the learning curve with teaching strategies, and use scaffolded teaching so students climb higher step by step.
