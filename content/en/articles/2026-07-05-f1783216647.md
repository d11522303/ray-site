California Sunshine

Across the time difference, I often receive my child's summer learning feedback in the middle of the night. In the EE lab course, he deliberately split off from the Taiwanese students when groups were formed, and ended up with an undergraduate exchange student from Zhejiang University. Although he was a third-place scorer (tanhua) in Shandong's gaokao, his English listening and speaking were poor and his lab skills even weaker. This case suggests that the mainland exam system has trouble gaining an advantage in the American teaching model, but it can't be generalised to the whole picture. If I get the chance to go on exchange at Tsinghua in Beijing, I'll share more.

I also asked about comparing the teaching content and building facilities. Apart from the university canteen being astonishingly good, the undergraduate electrical engineering course content is only comparable to the maths and science level of Taiwan's top three high schools, and neither the teaching equipment nor the course difficulty goes deeper than in Taiwan. The distinctive feature is that the content of a 4-credit course expands to hands-on lab work, with learning outcomes assessed through projects. The most important gain is that classmates come from all over the world with diverse cultures. Study attitudes at the two schools are about the same; everyone works quite hard on their coursework.

Summer sunshine in California is free, and the Pacific breeze keeps temperatures at a pleasant 20 degrees or so. Students can also get the Summer Bruin U-Pass for just US$30 (details in the comments), and after class three classmates arranged to go all around to see Los Angeles's museums and scenic spots, which is very good value.
