Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP31

#68DaysToTheTourLeaderExam

13 The "Tomb of the Fifty-Four Ryukyuan Subjects of the Great Japanese Empire" in Tongpu Village, Checheng Township, Pingtung County, is related to which of the following incidents? ① The Rover Incident ② The Takow (Kaohsiung Harbour) Incident ③ The Mudan Incident ④ The Hamada Yahyoe Incident

Explanation:

The incidents in the options, in chronological order: first, in the early years of Dutch rule in Taiwan (1628), the Japanese merchant Hamada Yahyoe came to Taiwan holding a shogunate vermilion-seal licence to trade, and refused to pay taxes to the Dutch East India Company, causing a commercial conflict. In the end Hamada even seized the Dutch governor of Taiwan and forced him to give way, which extended into a long-running trade and diplomatic deadlock between the Dutch and the Japanese. Later, in the middle and late Qing period, in the 1865 Takow Harbour incident, Britain applied military pressure on the Qing court over trade, tariff and port management issues, reflecting the reality of the Qing government's limited sovereignty under the treaty-port system. Then, in the 1867 Rover Incident, an American merchant ship ran aground on the Hengchun Peninsula and its crew were killed by indigenous people. The Qing court shirked responsibility by calling the area "land beyond civilisation," so the United States sent warships to carry out a punitive action, highlighting the Qing court's lack of real control over southern Taiwan. Finally, in 1871, Ryukyuan boatmen were killed in southern Taiwan, which led to the Mudan Incident of 1874. Japan sent troops to Taiwan in the name of protecting the Ryukyuans, forcing the Qing court to pay compensation and to strengthen its management of Taiwan, which prompted the Qing government to formally establish Taiwan Province in 1885 and strengthen administration, military affairs and modernisation.

After Japan took control of Taiwan, to commemorate Hamada Yahyoe's heroic resistance against Dutch taxation, the Japanese erected a stone tablet at Fort Zeelandia in Anping inscribed "Site of the Valor of Hamada Yahyoe, Junior Fifth Rank." After the war, the government-appointed Tainan mayor Kao-Hsuan Cho had it re-carved as "Anping Fort," which is how it appears today in the photo.
