#TourLeaderExamCountdown84Days

11. Among the Hokkien people in Malaysia there is an idiom, "three generations become Baba," meaning that every third-generation Chinese person born in Malaysia becomes a Baba (Peranakan). Which of the following does this saying imply?

① It means the socioeconomic status of the Chinese community is relatively low

② It means Malaysian culture is superior

③ It means Chinese do not intermarry with local women

④ It means Chinese culture has blended with local colour

#2025TourismAdministrationTourLeaderExamQuestion

Explanation: Since the 16th century, Nanyang trade flourished, and male immigrants from China settled on the Malay Peninsula, especially in the port cities of Malacca and Penang, and married local peoples, gradually forming the group known as the Peranakan. Men are called "Baba" and women "Nyonya." Their way of life, clothing, language (Baba Malay, a mix of Malay and Hokkien), food and festival customs blend Chinese and Malay culture with elements from Portuguese, Dutch and British colonial influences, thus forming a unique cultural character.

#1 Malacca and George Town in Penang, Malaysia, are both World Heritage sites. In their urban spaces one often sees Nyonya architecture, Baba family houses, and cultural displays of women dressed in kebaya. Which of the following best explains the significance of the kebaya in the World Heritage cultural context?

① It reflects the result of Chinese immigrants maintaining pure Han traditions in Southeast Asia

② It shows the effect of colonial governments forcing a European clothing system

③ It symbolises the cultural fusion and localisation formed through long-term interaction between different ethnic groups

④ It represents Malaysian culture being superior to Chinese culture

#2 Walking in a Malacca square, you see the entire area of red "Dutch Red Houses" (Stadthuys) and Christ Church. If you analyse this landscape historically, which inference fits best?

① Since they are called the Dutch Red Houses, their colour and appearance have never changed from the 17th century to today.

② The red exterior walls symbolise the influence of local Baba-Nyonya culture, because red represents celebration in Chinese culture.

③ The existing red buildings are the "historical traces" left by different colonial powers (Dutch and British) at different times.

④ These red buildings were rebuilt as imitations by the Malaysian government only in the late 20th century to attract modern tourists.
