#UCLA

This week's courses had hands-on work and a paper exam, so I spent most of my time studying at school and doing lab work in class. The course also had teaching assistants for questions, and as a result the only ones who stayed behind after the whole class were the five Taiwanese exchange students. Classmates from other countries left as soon as class ended, and the Chinese students in his group were not diligent at all, skipping the lab assignments entirely and leaving everything to the Taiwanese engineering guy.

In our chat he mentioned the remote scholarship interview a few days ago for the Jason International Foundation, a charitable trust. In fact, even if he got this scholarship, it would be only US$1,000, but the interviewer was a senior executive at a well-known Taiwanese company who happened to be a senior alumnus of my child's high school. The two had a very pleasant conversation during the interview, and afterwards it was like an interview session that ran for nearly an hour, covering different perspectives on learning, career and knowledge, plus recommended reading and building good habits. All of it was guidance for a twenty-year-old, and being able to spend a whole hour through an interview talking with an alumnus of such vision and energy is hard to measure in terms of life value.
