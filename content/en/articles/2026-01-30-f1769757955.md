Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP56

#TourLeaderExamCountdown43Days

Among South Korea's World Cultural Heritage sites, which was the earliest to be inscribed by UNESCO? (A) Changdeokgung Palace Complex (B) Jongmyo Shrine (C) Royal Tombs of the Joseon Dynasty (D) Namhansanseong Fortress

Answer (B) Jongmyo Shrine

In 1995, South Korea had three cultural heritage sites inscribed for the first time at once: Jongmyo Shrine in Seoul, the Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple of the Silla period that we discussed last time, and the Tripitaka Koreana woodblock repository at Haeinsa Temple. As of 2026, South Korea has 17 World Heritage sites, including 15 cultural heritage sites and 2 natural heritage sites, spanning the long history from the Neolithic period and the Three Kingdoms period (Goguryeo, Baekje, Silla) to the Joseon Dynasty.

The 2026 World Heritage Committee session will be held in Busan, South Korea, and South Korea may add the "Hanyangdoseong" (Seoul City Wall) site. Also, many of you will travel to South Korea and will go to Gyeongbokgung to take hanbok photos, so here are some questions.

#1 Which of the following statements about South Korea's World Heritage sites is incorrect?

(A) Hwaseong Fortress in Suwon combined the fortress-building techniques of East and West of its time and has very high military defence value

(B) There are 40 Joseon Royal Tombs, fully showing Confucian culture and feng shui geomantic thought

(C) The Haeinsa Janggyeong Panjeon was built to house the oldest and most complete wooden Tripitaka in the world

(D) Changdeokgung's architecture blends harmoniously with the surrounding natural environment, and it was listed as a World Heritage site earlier than Gyeongbokgung

Answer: (D) Gyeongbokgung is not a World Heritage site!

#2 When a tour leader takes a group to South Korea and wants to introduce the country's UNESCO-certified World Natural Heritage, which of the following combinations is entirely correct? (A) Hallasan, Seongsan Ilchulbong, Manjanggul, Korean Tidal Flats (B) Bulguksa, Hallasan, Seoraksan (C) Jeju Volcanic Island, Namhansanseong, Seowon of Korea (D) Gyeongju Historic Areas, Korean Tidal Flats, Gaya Tumuli

Answer: (A) South Korea currently has only 2 natural heritage sites: Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes (including Hallasan, Seongsan Ilchulbong, Manjanggul and others), inscribed in 2007, and the Getbol, Korean Tidal Flats, inscribed in 2021. The rest are all cultural heritage.
