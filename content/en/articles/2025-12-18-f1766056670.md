Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP13

#2025TourismAdministrationTourLeaderExamQuestion

20. After Taiwan's ports were opened to trade in the 19th century, Presbyterian missionaries such as James Maxwell, Peter Anderson, George Mackay, David Landsborough and Campbell Moody came to Taiwan one after another to preach, also bringing Western medical treatment and ideas. From the perspective of the history of local social medical development, what effects did the missionaries' arrival have on Taiwan's medical system at the time?

① Under the strong incursion of Western power, Western medicine became the mainstream medical market

② Traditional folk therapies and Chinese (Han) medicine lost the mainstream market

③ Missionaries established Taiwan's earliest combined Chinese and Western medical system

④ Modern Western medical methods were introduced, giving people more medical choices

Solution: Many missionaries came to Taiwan in the early years. Their deeds are summarised below:

# James L. Maxwell: He was the first doctor posted to Taiwan by the English Presbyterian Church, arriving in Tainan in 1865. He founded a clinic on Kan-se Street in Tainan (the predecessor of today's Sin Lau Hospital), introducing Western medical techniques to Taiwan, and was a pioneer of medical missionary work in southern Taiwan. (Note: Kan-se Street was one of the main roads of Tainan's Five Channels Port area; as the name suggests, it was a street facing west, as the harbour area looked west.)

# Peter Anderson: Also from the English Presbyterian Church, he came to Tainan in 1879. He continued Maxwell's work, long led the medical work of Sin Lau Hospital, and devoted himself to expanding and modernising the hospital, contributing greatly to early public health in the south.

# George L. Mackay: A missionary of the Canadian Presbyterian Church, he came to Tamsui in northern Taiwan in 1872. Besides providing tooth-extraction services, he founded the Hobe Mackay Clinic and the Oxford College for training talent (the predecessor of today's Aletheia University), with a profound influence on the modernisation of medicine and education in northern Taiwan.

# David Landsborough: He came to serve in Changhua in 1895. He founded Changhua Christian Hospital. His best-known story is the sacrificial spirit of his wife Marjorie Landsborough, in the "love that gave skin" episode. He worked in central Taiwan's medical field for decades and was deeply loved by local people.

# Campbell N. Moody: He came to preach in Changhua in 1895 together with Dr. Landsborough. A passionate preacher, he was good at street preaching in fluent Taiwanese, and devoted himself to theological writing and opening up new churches. Together with Dr. Landsborough he is called the "twin towers" of Changhua missionary work.
