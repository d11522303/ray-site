A Student's Disappointed Smile Taught Me to Praise

Teaching work has gradually changed in nature in recent years, and my views coexist: on one side worry and concern, on the other challenge and opportunity. I hope what I feel and experience is positive. Watching the students we coached after work hours for the national exams earn their licences, they are happy and we are proud. Here I praise my students loudly and with all my strength: you are really great!

The reason goes back to something that happened after class a few years ago.......

A student walked up to me and said: "Teacher, the notes I wrote are great, aren't they? Praise me!"

I was a little stunned for a moment: "You're great :)" (My reaction felt perfunctory even to me....)

The student smiled at me, looking a little disappointed.

I blamed myself for not giving timely and appropriate emotional support for their learning.

After that I began learning in my career how to praise students and affirm their performance, and of course, at the right moments, to encourage myself too: I did well, and I didn't lose points in my role as a teacher. Over these years I have slowly found that education has truly changed. Every student who comes before us is an individual, and teaching needs designed interaction and even more practice, because words in a classroom easily get out of control. Last week, in front of nearly eighty students, I showed my emotions and said the wrong thing, and had to make an awkward turn at the podium. My state of mind was very complicated.

This is the result of the tour leader and tour guide exam announced the day before yesterday. Our free after-class coaching class has run from last year up to the exam. It is the result of Brother Kong of the HWU Department of Travel Management and me working after hours, with students staying behind to work together. I hope everyone will praise the students, because they truly worked hard and are truly great. Praise us too!
