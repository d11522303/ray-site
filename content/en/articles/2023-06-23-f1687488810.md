Teacher Su is referring to the teaching reality at national universities of science and technology and the difficulties students face after graduation.

As a university of science and technology teacher, what can I do? I believe the sandwich teaching method (2+1+1), combining professional courses, licensing exams and a full-year internship, must be put into practice.

Here is an example:

A few days ago, after the end-of-term class, I chatted with a student about how the full-year internship starts this summer.

Me: Are you nervous?

Student: Of course.

Me: Are you ready?

Student: Not yet.

Me: Actually, I'm nervous before starting a new job too, and I'm nervous facing students I don't know before class. It means we care.

Student: Oh, teachers get nervous too!

Me: Of course. We get nervous whenever something matters to us. But I prepare my lessons well and plan my work schedule well. And I think you're about as ready as you can be.

Student: Really? I don't know how to do anything.

Me: That's not so. You have part-time work experience, you've put in the effort to earn licences, and you have a faculty of teachers with decades of industry experience at school to be your work mentors. You're pretty much ready.

Student: (grins silently; I know they're thinking about and reviewing the past three years)

Me: Do your best, report back if anything comes up. The teachers will catch you.

Student: OK, thank you, teacher.
