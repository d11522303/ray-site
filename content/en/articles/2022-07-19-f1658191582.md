---
title: "Too Hot to Sleep, I Woke Before Dawn and Remembered My Childhood"
excerpt: "Woken before dawn by the summer heat, Ray jots down a childhood memory of sleeping between his grandparents in Taichung, with a hand fan, baby powder and the scent of magnolia."
published: true
---

Too hot to sleep, I woke before dawn and suddenly remembered something from my early childhood, so I jotted it down.

When I was little I lived in Taichung's Xingda Wucun, a single-storey staff dormitory house with a second floor and a magnolia tree as tall as the building. Before I went to kindergarten in Banqiao, Taipei, that house was my home. Every night I slept between my grandfather and grandmother. The bed was the kind with a plank mat of slats that pinched your skin and fine hairs. You had to lie straight and flat to sleep, on your buttocks and back, because any other position left a few spots on the body aching, and it was hard to fall asleep.

Summer nights in Taichung were not "cool as water." In my memory there may have been an electric fan on the cabinet beneath the plank bed, but it was never plugged in or switched on. As a small child I found the spinning blades frightening, since I had been hit by an electric fan and bled more than once. It never occurred to me to turn it on to cool off.

So every night, sleeping beside my grandparents, I would drift off listening to the sound of my grandfather waving his fan, to the breeze that sometimes reached me and sometimes didn't, to the fragrant, cooling scent of baby powder on my skin, and to the flower scent that came and went from outside the window.

Thinking back, perhaps summers weren't that hot after all.
