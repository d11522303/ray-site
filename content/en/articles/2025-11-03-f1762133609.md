【Holding Chopsticks vs. Holding a Pen】

The way of holding chopsticks in the top of the picture is what my family taught me as a child. The main idea is that the lower chopstick stays still, and only three fingers control the upper chopstick.

Because people learn to eat before they learn to write, when no one taught me how to write, I held the pen wrongly, resting the pen against my ring finger. The drawback of this grip is that the imbalance makes you use too much force when forming characters, and the range of motion is also too small, so you have to move your wrist or even your whole arm to write, and the speed never picks up.

But around the age of 25 I took the opportunity to correct my pen grip. During that half year I was preparing for graduate school exams at Nanyang Street and the National Central Library, and had to write a huge amount of notes every day, so I decided to use the chance to fix my grip. I kept writing from ugly handwriting until I could stand to look at it.

Because the students in my classes all need to take notes, and I have to read what they write, I know the age of AI and computers has arrived and handwriting is becoming an ancient skill. But when students in our department prepare résumés for airlines, handwriting is almost always required. If you ever get the chance to see a hand-written résumé next to a typed one, you will feel the difference deeply: seeing handwriting makes you imagine the person, even their personality, while text printed from a computer printer is cold and gives you no feeling at all.

If Kelly had only become my student today, yours would be the most beautiful résumé of all.
