【Hundun Gets His Seven Openings】

The emperor of the South Sea was Shu, the emperor of the North Sea was Hu, and the emperor of the Centre was Hundun (Chaos). Shu and Hu often met in the land of Hundun, and Hundun treated them very well. Shu and Hu discussed how to repay Hundun's kindness, saying: "All people have seven openings for seeing, hearing, eating and breathing, but this one alone has none. Let's try boring some for him." Each day they bored one opening, and on the seventh day Hundun died. [Zhuangzi, "Normal for Emperors and Kings"]

Every night after cram school, on the drive home, I mostly listen to podcasts while driving. The daily round trip between the basin and the plateau becomes a stretch of self-learning time, mostly interviews and thought-provoking content. I love and treasure this time, just like my 30-minute run every day: it resets body and mind.

One day my child, who is usually quiet and resting in the back seat, broke the silence and mentioned a passage we had just heard. He said his classmates had been discussing how the human soul might be antimatter, something current science simply cannot prove, which doesn't mean it doesn't exist. He also said he very much likes Zhuangzi's view of the universe, a very scientific kind of philosophical thinking.

I was surprised that, on top of the maths, physics, chemistry and English he studies at cram school every night, he was discussing Zhuangzi with me. Right then I talked with him about Zhuangzi's philosophy, from the perspective of fish versus humans to dreams versus reality, and about my own view of the universe and my values. There wasn't much I could discuss with him, because I don't have a PhD and I'm certainly not a scientist, just a family member who tries to find time to read and listen to books alongside my child.

In the fable of Hundun, Shu and Hu cleverly "open up" Hundun, and as a result Hundun is killed. My child is about the same age as my students. I'm not an anxious father, nor a teacher who pushes students. In learning, I truly agree with the way of Zhuang Zhou. Each student in class needs something different. Students are like Hundun in the sea, encountering all kinds of ideas and norms in the great ocean of knowledge and expertise. So Hundun doesn't need openings; he can feel, think and take shape through his natural, innate perceptions. When students come to campus, the teacher should give as many learning resources as possible, let students find the teacher to chat, and give them a safe place to lean on when they are troubled. Quite naturally, year after year goes by, and students may grow their own seven openings and unlock their wisdom.

If a teacher believes it is so necessary to dwell on a single missed essay such as Gu Yanwu's "On Integrity and Shame", that is like Shu and Hu boring openings in their self-righteousness. Boring one opening a day, they slowly smother the possibilities of the student's development.
