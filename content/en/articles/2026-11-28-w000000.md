---
title: "Election Day"
excerpt: "Election day 2010: a family call, a long drive from Hualien to Taipei along Highway 9 to cast a favour vote, and a promise that came to nothing."
published: true
---

On 27 November 2010, our family lived in the Zhixue Xincun 500 Households community in Hualien. Sixteen years ago today was also an election day. Although I worked at the Taiwan Hospitality and Tourism College in Shoufeng, Hualien, our Taipei home was still in Muzha. It was the special municipality mayoral race between Hau Lung-bin and Su Tseng-chang. The result isn't the point; what happened that day is, and I still remember it vividly.

Living a hundred miles away, our family didn't feel much involved in that year's election. A five-year-old child and busy school administration had long left me numb to the world; days just passed one after another. Then one day a family member who had never contacted us of their own accord suddenly called, asking us to support a certain candidate, and saying that if we were willing to go home to vote, they would give us some money. Money had always been tight in our family, and this offer made us think about going back to Taipei. But train tickets between Hualien and Taipei were hard to get, and a trip was never cheap, so in the end the three of us decided to drive home in our little green car. We didn't have anyone in mind to vote for; we only thought that after voting we'd get some help with living costs.

Our five-year-old was actually very sensible and a great talker. First thing in the morning he asked where we were going to play, and I said: Today we're going on a really long drive to Taitung!

Near noon we set off with a little food, all the way home along Highway 9. Apart from National Freeway 5, each of our homes happened to lie along Provincial Highway 9, from Guting and Roosevelt Road in Taipei, through Muzha and Jingmei, to Zhixue in Shoufeng, Hualien. For a very long time we travelled back and forth on that long road, for the sake of keeping in touch with family and for economic need.

We left at 11 and finally reached the polling station at Jingmei Girls' High School near our Muzha home at 3:50 pm. We left our child in the car and took turns going in to cast this favour vote. As soon as we had voted, we drove straight back to Hualien before dark, because our little one was asking: Taitung is so far and so boring. The fairy tales in the car and the scenery outside had made even our very patient child start to complain.

Afterwards we told the family member that we had gone back to vote. But did the promised support money arrive as agreed? My impression is that because the candidate they backed lost, the matter just fizzled out. By the time we drove back to Shoufeng our child was exhausted and asleep. That election day felt like sleepwalking: we had done something meaningless that came to nothing.
