A good listen to share: it takes 49 minutes

Over more than thirty years in the workforce, I have been a summer assembly worker in an electronics factory, a part-time attendant at a bowling alley, a visa-filing runner at a travel agency, a telephone surveyor, a book reviewer for Books.com.tw, and an author of exam prep books; and in full-time roles a market survey researcher, a clerk in the signal corps, an industry analyst for listed companies, an internet marketing project member, a leisure venue manager, a meeting-space rental salesperson, a marketing campaign manager, an information systems sales promoter, a travel agency manager, an international tour leader, a secretary-general of an NGO, and a teacher at a private vocational school. Looking back on it all in a jumble, the job in which I have felt the greatest sense of crisis is that of private-school teacher!

So at the end of each semester, when students graduate, besides helping them enter the workplace, I also think about my own future, turning that sense of crisis into motivation and help. I review my own efforts and direction, confirm the relationship between organisations and myself, and of course compare my expectations and dreams to see how far they are from my goals, letting them collide for real with my sense of crisis.

Today I would like to recommend that you join me in facing and thinking about the future from a different angle. This Dr. Hsieh, a graduate of the University of Maryland, a famous American university, devoted himself to the restaurant business after retirement and has observed the many possibilities for role changes among people in middle and later age. For Dr. Hsieh, his public-service pension was a major asset, but the situation of losing a work position too early gave me, who also feels a deep sense of crisis, a chance to think carefully about how to respond to changes that may come.

If you have thoughts after listening, I would be glad to hear them. If it helps your family or friends, you can also chat with them about what they think.
