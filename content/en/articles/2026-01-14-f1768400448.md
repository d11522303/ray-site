Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Countdown EP40

#TourLeaderExamCountdown59Days

14 After Japan began ruling Taiwan, it considered Taiwanese people to have three great bad habits of daily life. Which of the following is NOT one of them? ① Men's queues (braided hair) ② Women's foot binding ③ Opium smoking ④ Betel nut chewing

Explanation:

I still want to stand up for my ancestors a little. From a sociological point of view, a "habit" is something established by custom: whatever society and environment exist will shape behaviours and habits, and every behaviour that has ever existed has its reasons, with no good or bad. 

Confucius said: "If a gentleman lives there, what rudeness can there be?" "Bad habit" is a very subjective expression. The Japanese talk about Taiwanese, Taiwanese talk about Chinese, and liking to divide people into insiders and outsiders is the real bad habit. Reading questions like this makes me very uncomfortable. It is like when tour members love to ask: how much do people in this place earn each month? I often answer something unrelated and talk about other things, refusing to answer such prejudiced questions.

A similar question has appeared in a history reference paper from the College Entrance Examination Center, so everyone give these a try 🫠

＃1 In the early period of Japanese rule in Taiwan, the Government-General considered men keeping queues and women binding their feet to be bad habits and tried to ban them, but with little effect. After some time, however, Taiwanese people began cutting their queues and unbinding their feet, which became a trend. Colonial officials reviewed this and believed the trend was not the result of Government-General policy but was influenced by other factors. The factor this official most likely referred to was: (A) The Xinhai Revolution overthrowing the Qing regime (B) Wilson's promotion of national self-determination (C) Chiang Kai-shek's New Life Movement (D) The Marco Polo Bridge Incident triggering the Sino-Japanese War

＃2 In 1938, the Taiwan Government-General launched the "Temple Reorganisation Movement", demolishing Mazu temples and other temples and even banning Mazu pilgrimage processions, yet Taiwanese folk belief in Mazu was not shaken. After the end of the Second World War, people rebuilt the destroyed Mazu temples and resumed the processions, of which the Dajia Mazu pilgrimage is the most famous. What was the main motive of the Japanese Government-General in launching the "Temple Reorganisation Movement"? (A) To actively eradicate superstition (B) To promote the Kominka movement (Japanisation) (C) To widely establish national schools (D) To convert temples into military bases (2021 GSAT, ROC year 110)

Think about the questions above. Exams often have questions with such highly biased thinking, but each is still worth 2 points, so guess the answer the examiner wants and take the points first. The critical attitude can wait until you have your licence.
