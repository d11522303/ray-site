33 Which ancient city was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1997 together with Pingyao Ancient City?

① Nanjing Ancient City ② Xi'an Ancient City ③ Lijiang Ancient City ④ Kaifeng Ancient City

Explanation: This question isn't about the over-touristed Lijiang Ancient City. Let's look at Pingyao Ancient City in Shanxi, mentioned in the question, which has a history of 2,800 years. Pingyao Ancient City was first built in the Western Zhou period (about 827 BC to 782 BC) and was expanded in the third year of the Hongwu reign of the Ming dynasty (1370). It is the best-preserved ancient Han Chinese city in China. Pingyao was also a financial centre in the Qing dynasty. In 1824, "Rishengchang", the prototype of China's first modern bank, opened in Pingyao, launching the legend of the Jin merchants who remitted money across the land.

Pingyao's World Heritage area actually covers "one city and two temples": Pingyao Ancient City, Zhenguo Temple and Shuanglin Temple. Shuanglin Temple lies southwest outside the city and is famous for more than 2,000 exquisite Yuan and Ming painted sculptures, known as a "treasure house of painted sculpture art". The Hall of Ten Thousand Buddhas at Zhenguo Temple is one of the oldest surviving wooden structures in China.

① Pingyao Ancient City, the Yungang Grottoes and the Yingxian Wooden Pagoda are all inscribed as World Cultural Heritage

② The Yungang Grottoes and Mount Wutai are World Cultural Heritage, and the Hanging Temple is also listed

③ Pingyao Ancient City, the Yungang Grottoes and Mount Wutai are all World Cultural Heritage

④ Mount Wutai and the Yingxian Wooden Pagoda are World Cultural Heritage, while Pingyao Ancient City has not yet been inscribed

#2 The Yungang Grottoes, Longmen Grottoes and Mogao Caves are all important representatives of Buddhist cave art in China and reflect the development and change of Buddhist art in China across different periods. Which of the following statements best compares the historical background and cultural significance of the three?

① Yungang, Longmen and Mogao were all formed in the Tang dynasty, with highly consistent artistic styles

② The Yungang Grottoes show clear Western Regions influence, the Longmen Grottoes show the sinicisation of Buddhist art, and the Mogao Caves bear witness to cultural exchange between China and abroad along the Silk Road

③ Longmen and Mogao are both in the northwest and mainly served court religious activities

④ Yungang and Mogao mainly served as imperial tombs, while Longmen was a centre of popular belief
