<p>------------🥰Encouragement from a senior student🥰-------------</p><p>To the juniors still at school:</p><p>When I first chose my internship, I just thought that if there was a chance, I should try more things, and I also tried to find out about internship placements related to our department. I truly feel that when you work hard to seize opportunities, opportunities will come looking for you. If you are interested in something, ask and listen more, and you will always get a surprise you never expected. That is how I got the very rare news that THSR was opening up internships to university students as an outside recruitment, and in the end I successfully became a THSR intern. I still relish every moment of that process today!!</p><p>I hope all of you promising juniors will also, in your passionate university life, fight for and create more possibilities for yourselves. The future you will definitely always be proud of yourselves. Keep it up, keep it up～～</p><p>HWU Travel Management, Class of 103, Wu Chia-En ❤</p><figure class="ph">Photo</figure><p>-----------A reminder from the teacher---------------</p><p>The HR staff at Taiwan High Speed Rail called and urgently asked that HWU Travel Management students also strive for the 113 academic year THSR service internship opportunity this year. In this long process of internship preparation and CV writing, as long as you want this job, you will certainly find the time and the method to prepare.</p><p>Besides Chia-En, other students of the same cohort from the HWU Travel Management Department who interned at Taiwan High Speed Rail have also passed the exam to become station staff after graduating. Apart from going to pray at Guanyin Temple on Zhulin Mountain, there is actually a mindset for preparing, which I have organised below:</p><p>1. Fill in your CV carefully; it's not a hassle</p><p>Many students are put off because they have to prepare so much written material. In fact, once you finish reading this article, open your computer and start writing right away, and you will find it isn't that hard. The difficulty lies in constantly trying to find something to copy or material to learn from. In fact, THSR's materials use Excel to lay out everything you need to prepare. Start from the first row and write word by word, and as the words build up you will understand what the teacher is telling you.</p><p>2. Write the key points in your Chinese autobiography</p><p>Whether it is THSR or EVA Air, the autobiography can only be about ten lines, so I suggest writing two or three paragraphs. In the first paragraph write about your personal characteristics, describing the actual situation; if your vocabulary is limited, give examples. In the second paragraph write about your real expectations for the job you are applying for: what do you hope to learn, and what experience do you hope to gain? Why are you the most suitable candidate? In the third paragraph write about how you imagine life after starting work, or your expectations for your future career.</p><p>3. Put your important abilities and specialities first</p><p>Most CV forms have a list section. Put the hardest-to-get or best-fitting certificates first, and don't list them in the chronological order in which you got them, or everyone's first certificate will be the international etiquette certificate. English or other foreign language ability can all be listed, and you should look for chances to take those tests regularly; there is no last-minute cramming for this.</p><p>4. Reduce absences and value your grades</p><p>Companies attach great importance to personal character, but that can't be seen in the pretty photo from a Korean-style photo studio, and it is even harder to gauge from a standard teacher's recommendation letter. So HR asks interns for their past learning records. A transcript with no red marks proves you care about everything; a clean attendance record is a preview of professional dedication. Some organisations even require club experience or competition certificates. Striving for yourself while at school is the most practical thing.</p><p>5. Ask a teacher as soon as you find one</p><p>The teachers in the Department of Travel Management all have decades of industry experience. We have all looked for jobs, led people and learned things the hard way, and after years of hard work in the workplace we moved to vocational education, hoping to put armour on our students in advance. So as long as you prepare the materials above properly and humbly and proactively approach any teacher, everyone will cheer you on at that moment, help revise your content, and encourage you to walk toward a career path even more brilliant than ours.</p><p>Finally, writing has come to this point, and now it is your turn to open your computer and start typing your CV. All the imagining and deliberating in the world is a waste of time. If you don't seize the chance to apply, and just dither and choose, the result will be watching other people wear the THSR uniform.</p>
