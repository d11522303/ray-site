Travel Sketching

At last it was time to join a community college evening course, to win back a little time for myself.

At the start, apart from selling materials, the teacher spent the first and second sessions chatting and teaching without much method. I wasn't used to such a loose way of teaching; at least, that is not how I prepare for a three-hour class.

But I still studied seriously, because the teacher clearly has several tricks up their sleeve. A few strokes drawn on the spot, and a few new students or apprentices with some background to demonstrate, leave us beginners completely helpless. Later I mostly practised at home by copying the videos shared online and in the LINE group. For this kind of hard skill, doing with your hands certainly teaches more than talking.

Joining community college courses lets you meet many people whose worldviews differ from yours: those with no sense of time, those with no learning pace or stage, those who need emotional support, and all are very welcome to sign up. The fees are low, the schedule is flexible, and you can join groups more meaningful than an echo chamber of old-timers. Every day feels full and not wasted. I highly recommend it to retirees and to those who feel lonely!
