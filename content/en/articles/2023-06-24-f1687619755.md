【2023 Times Higher Education Asia University Rankings: Taiwan】

Does anyone know which university National Formosa University is? It's on the ranking list! I honestly learned for the first time that Taiwan has a "National Formosa University".

Summary of the ranking report:

1. Public universities did not do well; the four top universities (NTU, NCKU, NTHU and NYCU) did not make the top 4.

2. The ranking mainly reflects the quality and quantity of journal publications by a school's faculty and doctoral students.

3. Universities with medical schools have an advantage.

4. STEM fields (science, technology, engineering and mathematics) are the trend of the future.

5. Globally, teaching at universities is defined as research-oriented.

Only 46 Taiwanese universities made the Asia rankings, of which 22 are public, less than half of those listed. Even with ample funding and national subsidies, Taiwan's national universities are not particularly outstanding. Some will say rankings can be bought, but this is still public information. In an unequal competition, any university has the right to use reasonable and lawful means to win students for itself, because this is, after all, a competitive luxury good.

The 2023 Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings sampled 1,799 universities from 104 countries and regions. They measure institutional performance in four areas: teaching, research, knowledge transfer and international outlook. The ranking analyses more than 121 million citations across over 15.5 million research publications, plus survey responses from 40,000 scholars worldwide. So we can say with confidence that THE values academic work: research and publication account for 60%. The number of journal papers should be the focus. In the teaching portion, the figures come from the reputation survey (15%), staff-to-student ratio (4.5%), doctorate-to-bachelor's ratio (2.25%), doctorates awarded to academic staff ratio (6%) and institutional income (2.25%).

My personal thoughts and takeaways:

1. The Times ranking evaluates the strengths and weaknesses of the teachers on campus, and internationally a university teacher is defined as a researcher first and a teacher second. University teachers do not teach students step by step what they must know and what they needn't.

2. If a traditional Taiwanese university wants to compete internationally and be globally competitive, it must focus on STEM research, because other fields can hardly stand out in quantity and quality.

3. If a Taiwanese university student decides to attend a research university but lacks the foundational prerequisite knowledge, studying at a traditional research university is a waste of four years of tuition and time.

4. Taiwan's universities of science and technology should teach with employment in mind and rarely focus on publishing papers, so it is hard for them to score well in such evaluations. The teacher's role lies in mentoring, teaching and career guidance, which has little to do with this kind of ranking.

5. Ranking Taiwanese schools as public or private comes from guidance based on the past experience of teachers and parents. When parents send their children to a research university, they really should also consider whether the student's potential is suited to it.
