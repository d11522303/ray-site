<p>Daily Morning Reading-1 / 2026.2.20 (Day 4 of the Lunar New Year) </p><p>Who do you want to travel with?<br/>Today's reading is an academic paper published in 2025 by four scholars from China and Australia in the International Journal of Tourism Research (IJTR), titled "Who Would You Travel With? Identifying the Key Attributes of a Desirable Travel Partner." The findings lay out, in an orderly way, the considerations when looking for a travel partner.</p><p>Although the research is based on data from 1,086 user posts on the Douban platform, analysed using content analysis, exploring the selection criteria when young Chinese people looking for travel companions join popular group-travel matching, it also analyses the factors that influence particularly memorable travel experiences.</p><p>Who do you want to travel with? Is it the same as what you think? The research distils four traits of the best travel partner:</p><p>#1 Emotional intelligence ranks first: Emotional intelligence wins trust fast<br/>People who can stay calm and emotionally stable under travel stress, and who are sensitive to others' emotions, can create a positive atmosphere even when flights are delayed or the itinerary is blocked.</p><p>#2 Experience makes someone trustworthy: Experience builds confidence<br/>Travel partners with travel skills, familiarity with the destination and problem-solving ability make others more willing to rely on them and to share resources comfortably.</p><p>#3 Similar conditions reduce friction: Compatibility reduces friction<br/>For short-term travel partners, the fit in budget, personal energy, daily schedule and sequence of sightseeing matters most, while personality and values, and even whether you click, matter less.</p><p>#4 Responsibility keeps the trip running smoothly: Responsibility keeps things running<br/>Partners who each do their part, follow the plan and offer practical suggestions make the trip go more smoothly. But for the option of #CreatingUnforgettableTravelExperiences, the trait of responsibility is slightly less important than the three above.</p><p>Travel patterns are constantly changing. In 2026 I visited the penguin parade at Phillip Island near Melbourne for the second time. From a rough observation of the visitor centre car park, large coach groups were mainly Chinese tour groups, while over eighty percent of the other vehicles were mostly mid-sized buses for shared day tours. The 20-seat buses were full of independent travellers from all over the world, who gathered at the meeting point, went off to play together, and after returning dispersed on their own, carefree and unattached.<br/>The academic paper discusses the online travel-companion community behaviour that has become popular among young Chinese people in recent years. Although it can't be inferred to Taiwanese young people's travel behaviour, from the standpoint of tourism research, Taiwan lacks the volume of traveller behaviour and the diversity of destinations. Most travellers are concentrated in a few countries, and their travel behaviour leans toward shopping, food and pleasure rather than travel experiences such as natural wonders, cultural visits and activities in unfamiliar places.</p><p>Source: Fang, X., Yu, J., Zhang, K., &amp; Huang, S. (2025). Who Would You Travel With? Identifying the Key Attributes of a Desirable Travel Partner.
