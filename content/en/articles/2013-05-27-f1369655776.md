My child's schooling in Hualien is good in many ways, but this forced Confucian drilling is really hard to accept!!

Before finishing primary school, children have to memorise, mindlessly, the Standards for Being a Good Student and Child (Di Zi Gui), the Three Character Classic, the Analects, Tang poems, Zhu Xi's Family Maxims, the "Great Harmony" chapter of the Book of Rites, and Zhuge Liang's Letter to His Son. Item seven of the Hualien County Government's inspection recommendations even says teachers need not explain it line by line, and can simply offer "teachable moments" when these things come up in daily life!

I had never read Zhu Xi's Family Maxims myself. After looking up the full text online, I felt even more strongly: does this really need to be memorised by children?

*****************************************************************

Rise at dawn and sweep the courtyard, keeping the inside and outside of the house clean and tidy. Retire at dusk, lock the doors and gates, and always check them yourself. Of every bowl of porridge and every meal, remember how hard it was to come by; of every half thread and half strand, always think how difficult the materials were to produce. Prepare before the rain comes; do not dig the well when you are already thirsty. Be frugal in your own living, and do not linger over banquets for guests. Utensils that are simple and clean are better than gold and jade; meals that are plain and refined are better than rare delicacies. Do not build grand houses, and do not scheme for fine fields.

Gossipy matchmakers and female busybodies are the go-betweens of lewdness and theft; a beautiful maid or a charming concubine is no blessing for the household. Do not employ handsome servants, and wives and concubines should avoid showy make-up. Though your ancestors are far away, the sacrifices to them must be sincere; though your descendants may be dull, they must still read the classics. Aim for simplicity in how you conduct yourself, and raise your sons with a sense of righteousness. Do not covet windfall wealth, and do not drink wine to excess.

Do not take advantage of peddlers who carry their goods on a shoulder pole; when you see poor and struggling relatives and neighbours, be generous in sympathy and help. A family built on meanness will not enjoy its fortune for long; where the proper human relationships are violated, ruin soon follows. Brothers, uncles and nephews should share their means generously with the poor; elders and juniors, those within the household and those without, should be governed by strict and orderly speech. To heed a wife's words and estrange one's own flesh and blood — how is that the conduct of a husband? To prize wealth and slight one's parents — that does not make a proper son. When marrying off a daughter, choose a good son-in-law and do not demand a heavy betrothal gift; when taking a daughter-in-law, seek a virtuous woman and do not count the size of her dowry.

To look at the rich and powerful with a flattering face is the most shameful thing; to put on airs of arrogance before the poor is the lowest thing of all. In your household, avoid quarrels and lawsuits, for lawsuits end only in misfortune. In dealing with the world, avoid talking too much, for the more you say, the more you will get wrong. Do not rely on power to bully orphans and widows, and do not indulge your appetite by needlessly slaughtering birds and animals. Perversity and self-righteousness bring much regret and error; idleness and self-indulgence will never build a family. If you are too familiar with wicked youths, you will eventually suffer for it; if you humbly keep company with the experienced and mature, you can rely on them in a crisis. When you hear something said carelessly, how do you know it is not a slander by someone else? Be patient and think it over three times. When you quarrel over some matter, how do you know it is not you who are in the wrong? Calm your mind and reflect quietly.

Do not dwell on the kindness you have given, and never forget the kindness you have received. In all things leave some room to spare, and do not go back again to a place where you have already succeeded. When others have cause for celebration, do not feel envy; when others suffer misfortune, do not feel glad. A good deed that you want others to see is not true goodness; an evil deed that you fear others will learn of is great evil indeed. If you give rise to lust at the sight of beauty, the retribution will fall on your wife and daughters; if you hide resentment and shoot hidden arrows, the disaster will extend to your descendants.

When the household is harmonious and peaceful, even if meals are not assured, there is still joy to spare; when the taxes of the state are paid early, even if your purse is empty, you will naturally find the greatest happiness. In study, set your aim on the sages; in office, keep the ruler and the country in your heart. Be content with your lot and accept your fate; follow the times and leave the outcome to Heaven. If you live this way, you will come close to the ideal.
