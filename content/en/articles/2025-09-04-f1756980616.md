Are ESG and Sustainability Certificates and Tests Worth Taking?

Not long ago, out of curiosity, I signed up for the Sustainable Development Fundamentals Competency Test run by the Securities and Futures Institute. On exam day, at National Taipei University of Technology, there were a great many candidates. The whole building was full of examinees, each with a book in hand, cramming at the last minute. It confirmed the saying "sustainability is good business" on the spot, and I wondered who exactly was taking this exam.

Do you need to attend a course for this certificate? Is the exam difficult? How much time does it take to prepare? How much does it cost? I will set those questions aside for now. I asked beingbanker, an over-age examinee with a thousand followers on IG, and his answer turned out to be the same as my own motive: earning extra points in the bank department's performance review.

Let me start with a course I took during the pandemic, ISO 14064-1, "Greenhouse gases – Part 1: Specification with guidance at the organization level for quantification and reporting of greenhouse gas emissions and removals". Everyone in the class was a teacher with a tourism and hospitality background, using the winter break for training: three days of classes, 24 hours of online courses, plus an exam for certification. ISO 14064-1 is, as its name suggests, a standard for "how a company conducts a carbon inventory". It provides rules for quantifying and reporting greenhouse gases, using the organization as the basic framework, and sets principles and requirements for designing, developing, managing and reporting a greenhouse gas inventory, so that every company's carbon inventory data and reports can be standardised and inventory staff can carry them out.

For our carbon inventory exam assignment, we used domestic hotels and travel agencies as case studies, applying each standard to the different aspects of a business, before we completed the training and exam. Each day of those three days made me think deeply about the relationship between learning a skill and applying it at work, and of course it later helped me when I applied for projects!

But under the burdens of private-school enrolment and teaching, earning performance points remains the main purpose. So at the beginning of this year I bought a question-bank book from the bookstore in front of Taipei Main Station: the 2024 Sustainable Development Fundamentals Competency Test Study Guide and Question Bank, published by the Securities and Futures Institute, NT$495. I decided to use the crude method of practising past papers, finishing all the questions within two weeks, and memorising the answers for anything I could not understand. I spent the days until the exam memorising answers, and the result, as the picture shows, was a pass by the skin of my teeth.

So this 2025 certificate earns me 1 point in this year's annual performance review!
