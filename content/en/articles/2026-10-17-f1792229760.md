2026.10.17 The 2026 Key Competencies Game Season begins!

On Saturday afternoon I invited some HWU Travel Management students to join me online in a historical game from the MEG game design group at National Taiwan University of Science and Technology (NTUST). Its content is a major decision-making dilemma in the 16th year of the Chongzhen era at the end of the Ming dynasty (1643 CE), when the nation faced crisis. The main reason is that our teachers and students will visit the National Palace Museum in Taipei in early November, and I hoped we could build up related historical concepts and background knowledge before going.

I was very happy that all three students logged on on time and, step by step, followed the instructions of NTUST's supervising teacher through the game. Playing the whole game actually means taking part, without noticing, in thinking about and learning key decision-making abilities, and if you take part in three or more sessions you can also receive an NTUST learning certificate. It was my first time playing this historical AI game too, and I gained a lot of learning, gaming and AI-integration experience and inspiration. I won't give spoilers about the content.

Because it is an online game with no limits of space or time, everyone can find a free moment over the weekend and learn in casual clothes, interacting with a very wise AI elder minister who doesn't go off-topic, discussing strategy and views in classical Chinese. It was a really interesting experience.

If you are interested in this kind of historical game or in key-competency training, and hope to use it in your teaching or personal learning, you are welcome to message me privately. I will help put you in touch with the game's designers, so we can learn through play and learn how to play together!
