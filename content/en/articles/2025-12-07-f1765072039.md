Pass at 50? Or fail?

There is an island in the Pacific called Midway. At certain ages and moments, we always define them as very important, because life is like a vast ocean: all around looks exactly the same, and without a direction to guide you, you really feel at a loss in the endless sea. With a Midway Island as a stepping stone, the voyage has a direction.

So perhaps you, like me, at some important point in the sea of life, would look forward to a midway goal:

At 20, I hoped to date,

At 30, I hoped to have a stable family,

At 40, to have reached a certain rank at work,

At 50......

Recently, my high school classmate sailed around the world on a cruise with his family, and then, with astonishing perseverance, walked all the way around Taiwan on foot. He wrote his reflections: "From the scorching heat to late autumn, with a typhoon keeping us company at the end, 56 days, 1,561 kilometres, 2.12 million steps. Along the way there was inevitably pain, fatigue and danger, but these were all part of the journey and nourishment for growth......" As classmates, we simply watched him move forward day by day on Facebook, supporting our hardworking classmate with likes, and using the same likes to encourage ourselves.

In 2016, a Japanese stamp advertisement used a "pass" stamp to encourage a company as it headed towards its 50th year. In this last month of 2025, we should also stamp ourselves with a "pass", and pressing a "like" matters. As a teacher, stamping my students with a "pass" matters too, because we have all worked hard for ourselves, and a self who can give everything for something is truly great.

Now I have set off from Midway Island, with my family, friends, students and many memories on board. Actually, I don't know where the next port is, and I still worry about the uncertainties and challenges on the voyage, but I look even more forward to the different scenery of each new age.
