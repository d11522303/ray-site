Vancouver

Before taking students to Vancouver, I already knew there was a Vancouver Island nearby that you can only reach by ferry. Victoria, on the island, is the capital of British Columbia, and many travel agencies include it in a Vancouver day tour because Victoria has a beautiful cityscape and gardens. But I did not go, because I had assumed it was a boat ride like the one from Gushan to Cijin, when in fact there simply was not enough time.

Many Taiwanese overlook real distances when planning trips to large countries, and I did too. Only after actually being there did I learn that Vancouver Island is the largest island on the west coast of North America, longer from end to end than Taiwan, and only slightly smaller in area. Those of us living in a small country easily imagine the distance between two cities as a drive from Taipei to Taoyuan, and think of miles as kilometres. A two-hour drive in Taiwan is called going south or north, while in Canada or the US it is a nearby shopping trip or an outing, and the mindset is worlds apart.

Also, on this visit I learned that Vancouver is a person's name, given to commemorate a Royal Navy officer who surveyed the entire northeastern Pacific coast between 1791 and 1794. There are several places in the Pacific region of North America called Vancouver, including Vancouver Island, the city of Vancouver in British Columbia, Canada, and Vancouver, the fourth-largest city in Washington State, USA.

And Vancouver is a sister city of Los Angeles. I foolishly thought the distance between them was like a trip from Taipei to Kaohsiung, and without thinking much I arranged it as a transfer point. In fact huge Los Angeles gave me, a Taiwanese country bumpkin, another impression altogether, which I will talk about when I have time.

By the way, the Vancouver tourism board's website is superbly made, bringing out to the full the tourism highlights of the world's most livable city. I've put it in the comments, hoping that when I have a chance to visit the Cloud City again, I can feel its beauty more deeply.
