The Giant Bicycle Parking Garage in the Netherlands

In yesterday's world travel geography class I had been wanting to find this video to explain why Northern European societies lead on the Human Development Index (HDI). If a society's transport planning is built around bicycles, look at the astonishing and admirable bicycle parking facilities it can design.

I hope students can take a different view. When we need to move, our bodies come with a pair of legs. We can walk, and we can pedal. We can have a choice in how we move, rather than riding a motorcycle, or even driving and double-parking without getting out, just to go down the lift to a corner convenience store or breakfast shop for a coffee or a sandwich.

Sadly, few students ever lift their eyes from Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Instagram and Facebook on their screens. For them, comparing Western European society such as the Netherlands with the virtual society they follow, my worry may be unnecessary.

The HDI is calculated from per capita income, education level and life expectancy. But what a society chooses to do and enjoy once it has a certain economic capacity, whether its goals are not making money, year-end bonuses or buying consumer goods that mark social class, is probably not something an index can measure.

The internet's influence on values is now even more striking. When the values most members of society share are not health, education and community, but pleasure, financial freedom and self-improvement, the direction of that society's development has already been set. Every day at the McDonald's and other chains around Taipei Main Station I overhear too many whispers about wealth, networking and investing in new technology. Set against the bloody examples around me, it really is a cycle.

Many years ago I too was invited more than once by good friends to hear presentations on multi-level marketing or investment. At the time, the confusion about the future and the intoxication of wealth were very strong. At a property presentation in Tianmu, I saw many people put in units and money, and the whole frenzied money game made it hard for anyone to hold back. The outcome was always the same: all the speculators lost everything, and the mastermind of the scheme, with nowhere to turn, killed his whole family in Canada and then himself.

A game, a dream. It is more practical to walk or ride a UBike to buy breakfast...
