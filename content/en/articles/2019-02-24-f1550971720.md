---
title: "Running with My Son in the Evening, and the Poem \"The Burden\" by Wu Sheng"
excerpt: "A run with my son led me to Wu Sheng's poem about a father and his children, and a quiet winter-rain night of reading."
published: true
---

While running together one evening, my dog-nickname son (阿狗) asked me about Wu Sheng, the modern poet in his seventh-grade Chinese class. He said the poem had really moved him, especially the line about the spinning top, which he felt described exactly how he sees me! I casually told him that Wu Sheng is a well-known Taiwanese native-soil writer from Changhua with many works, and suggested he look them up online.

When I got home I was curious and looked it up. The truth is I don't read poetry, because I always feel that pinning your worries onto verse and forcing out sorrow doesn't suit the role I should be playing at this stage of life. But tonight, reading a poem on a quiet night of winter rain turns out to be a rather good choice...

The Burden --- Wu Sheng

After work, it is dusk.

Now and then I glance at the brilliant sunset,

but I no longer linger.

Because your little faces, turned up toward Dad,

reveal so much more expectation.

After overtime, it is deep night.

Now and then I glance at the starry sky,

but I no longer lose myself in it.

Because your little faces, fast asleep,

are more enchanting than the stars.

Day after day, Dad goes to work and comes home,

like a spinning top you flung with all your strength,

circling around you, round and round;

turning Dad's stormy passion,

one by one,

into long, fine tenderness.

Just like Grandpa and Grandma,

who wove for Dad a lifetime

of long and delicate care.

Children! Dad has no complaints at all.

Only because this is life's

heaviest

and also sweetest burden.
