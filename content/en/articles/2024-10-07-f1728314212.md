title: "When We Swallow Our Anger, We Give Up What Should Be Ours..."
excerpt: "On workplace bullying and why new employees should speak up early when something feels unreasonable, prompted by handling an intern case and teacher counselling."
published: true
---

When we swallow our anger, we hand over what should have been ours. It may look sensible and gracious, but it sacrifices all of our own rights and interests.

At work, if it is openly said that you needn't attend events in other counties on holidays, but privately there is constant hidden pressure and whispering that the newcomer doesn't know how things work, that is not management but workplace bullying.

At work, if a supervisor denies ever having made sexually harassing remarks, denies emotional outbursts that came from nowhere, and even openly lies, that is not education but leading newcomers astray, making new employees think such behaviour is acceptable out in society.

So as soon as a new employee feels uncomfortable, that something is unreasonable or unpleasant, they should speak up right away. If possible, respond with wisdom and words, and above all remind colleagues or supervisors face to face that they have gone beyond what the job requires.

The world is collapsing. Wronging yourself only shortens your happy time. These are my thoughts after handling a student internship incident and teacher counselling today.
