Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep, EP36

#TourLeaderExam63DaysToGo

49 Taiwan has rich geothermal resources and many types of hot springs. Which of the following areas was one of the four most famous hot spring areas in the Japanese colonial period?

① Hongye Hot Spring ② Guanziling Hot Spring ③ Wulai Hot Spring ④ Jiaoxi Hot Spring

This question is easy, practically a free point!

In the Japanese era, Taiwan had many "Top-N" scenic spots that can be counted off, such as the Eight Views of Taiwan, the three national parks and the Ten Views of Tamsui. Are the "four famous hot springs" really recorded in documents and literature? That is the point I am more curious about.

And people in Taiwan often write things like "the world's three great mountain railways" or "the three great undersea hot springs", which is really outrageous. For example, Iceland, which many Taiwanese love to visit, has the Hvammsvik hot spring right beside a fjord, and the Sicilian undersea hot spring named among the "three greatest" is in fact a mud spring, with a different geological pattern from the source of Green Island's Zhaori hot spring. In many texts and media we can see a lot of Taiwan-centred views with the feel of "a summer insect talking about ice" or "a frog at the bottom of a well".

Anyway, let me first list what the history and literature of the Japanese era definitely record, which is more likely to appear in exam questions. Take it as a reference.

#EightViewsOfTaiwan

1. 1696 (Kangxi 35, Qing), the "Taiwan Prefecture Gazetteer":

Anping Evening Ferry, Shakun Fishing Fires, Luer Spring Tide, Jilong Snow Piles, Eastern Sea Dawn Sun, Xiyu Sunset Glow, Feiting Listening to the Waves, Chengtai Viewing the Sea

2. 1752 (Qianlong 17), the "Revised Gazetteer of Taiwan County", the Eight Views of Taiwan County: Xiangyang Spring Hoeing, Chikan Evening Glow, Yanmen Mist and Rain, Luer Sails in a Row, Kunshen Gathering Nets, Golden Rooster Dawn Glow, Jitan Clearing Moon, Qiwei Autumn Hunt

3. 1913, the Taiwan Daily News special issue, photographs of the Eight Views of Taiwan: Hehuan Rising Sun, Nanjia Bright Moon, Niitaka Reflections, Pearl Lake Floating Islands, Guandu Returning Sails, East Sea Stone Screen, Qihou Sunset Glow, Northern Island Tide Watching

4. 1927, the Taiwan Daily News decided the New Eight Views of Taiwan by vote: Keelung Xu Hill, Tamsui Baxian Mountain, Sun Moon Lake, Alishan, Shoushan, Eluanbi, Taroko Gorge. Besides the eight views, two sacred sites were chosen, called "Bekkaku" (special class): the Taiwan Shrine and Niitaka Mountain (Yushan).

5. 1953, the Taiwan Provincial Government of the Republic of China redefined the Eight Views of Taiwan as follows: Twin Pools Autumn Moon, Yushan Snow, Anping Sunset, Alishan Sea of Clouds, Datun Spring Colours, Lugu Secluded Gorge, Qingshui Cliffs, Penghu Fishing Fires.

6. 2005, the Tourism Bureau, MOTC held a new vote for the Eight Views of Taiwan as follows: Taipei 101, the National Palace Museum, Sun Moon Lake, Alishan, Yushan, Kaohsiung Love River, Kenting, Taroko Gorge.

All right, tired? Let's try some practice questions! A tour guide's job is to package attractions, so the ordinary becomes distinctive and nature becomes unique, so that tourists who pay to travel feel the trip was worthwhile.

#1 The selection of the Eight Views of Taiwan can be traced back to the Qing dynasty's "Taiwan Prefecture Gazetteer" of 1696 (Kangxi 35). Limited by the areas that had been developed, the views were mostly concentrated near the prefectural city. Which of the following was "not" among the eight views chosen at that time? ① Anping Evening Ferry ② Jilong Snow Piles ③ Feiting Listening to the Waves ④ Shakun Fishing Fires

#2 For the Eight Views of Taiwan voted by the Taiwan Daily News in 1927, besides the basic "eight views", "two Bekkaku" were also chosen. Which two places are the "two Bekkaku"? ① Yushan, Alishan ② Taiwan Shrine, Yushan ③ Taiwan Shrine, National Palace Museum ④ Taroko, Kaohsiung Love River
