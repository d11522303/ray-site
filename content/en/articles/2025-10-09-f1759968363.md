Hsing Wu Travel Management Explores the Coal Fields: A Starting Point for Sustainability and Carbon Reduction Education

This field trip took "A guided tour of the history and mining culture of the Jingtong area: sustainable travel" as its theme. It aimed to guide students to understand the course of local industrial development, and, through on-site visits, to experience how railways and mining shaped the cultural landscape of the Pingxi area. During the activity, led by the guides and teachers, the students used green transport: everyone travelled by rail, from Taoyuan, Taipei, Ruifang and other places, and set out together from Ruifang Station, riding Taiwan's most romantic Pingxi Line all the way to the hidden gem of Jingtong Station. They visited the old street area, the former mine sites and the Mining Life Museum in turn. Through observing, listening and discussing, they gained a deeper understanding of the history of how Jingtong flourished because of coal mining in the Japanese colonial period, and of why the Pingxi Line was built and how it drove the local economy.

The Hsing Wu Travel Management students generally said the sights that left the deepest impression were Jingtong Station and the mine ruins. The century-old wooden station is well preserved and full of nostalgic atmosphere, letting students feel the character of the early coal-hauling railway and the daily life of the residents of the time. The mine area along the way let students appreciate the hardship of miners working in cramped spaces, and learn the importance of the coal industry to Taiwan's early industrial development. In addition, students were very interested in the cultural preservation of Jingtong Old Street. The wish-making bamboo tubes, local specialities and coal-mining cultural and creative products on the street fully show the efforts of community residents to combine historical heritage with sustainable tourism.

Overall, this activity not only deepened students' understanding of local culture and industrial history, but also developed their ability to observe environmental sustainability and reflect on social change. Through on-site walking and first-hand experience, they extended textbook knowledge into a real setting and formed deeper connections with history and environmental education. The activity also promoted cooperation and exchange among peers and improved the effectiveness of discussion and learning among them. We believe this kind of cross-disciplinary course can more fully achieve the goals of experiential learning and sustainability education.

More importantly, a whole day of outdoor teaching let a new generation of students put down the light of their phone screens and use all five senses to experience the beautiful travel scenery and in-depth environmental sites around Taiwan. Through group activities and team coordination they improved real verbal communication. Walking in Jingtong and sitting in the train carriage were real experiences different from the classroom, and they let us get to know one another better. Many thanks to the leaders of the Department of Travel Management for their support, to Teacher Yuan-Cheng Pu for his strong support on the trip, and to the key officers of the Travel Management Student Association and the Aviation and Railway Club for standing by us. It is because of them that we could have this little railway trip: a happy outing, rich and interesting learning, and a safe journey home.
