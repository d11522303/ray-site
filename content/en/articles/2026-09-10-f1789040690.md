10/690 Immanuel Kant (1724-1804)

Play is a free life activity that arises from people's inner purposes and reaches freedom in the sense of being for itself.

A bit hard to understand, isn't it? I think so too. Let me tell a little story first.

The first time I heard of [Kant] was at a packed lecture in the Zhongxing Auditorium of my high school, given by Legislator Chu Kao-cheng, who had famously flipped tables in the National Assembly in those days. The Zhongxing Auditorium was full of high school students listening to him talk about his view of Kantian philosophy from his studies in Germany.

It was a monologue of over an hour. The famous figure on the stage talked on and on without pause about Kant's three great critiques. I admit that at the time I had no idea what Chu Kao-cheng was saying, but I saw that none of the thousand-odd classmates around me left, and I assumed my classmates were so smart and gifted that they all understood something, so I pretended to listen too. I sat in my seat until the end, when thunderous applause closed what, thinking back, was a rather baffling event.

Today in class the professor posted this passage, and I felt that across time and space I was sitting in a seat again, but this time I roughly understand what Kant was trying to express in 1790.

This is why I am so happy and free to be sitting in this seat right now.
