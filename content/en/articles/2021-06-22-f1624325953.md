title: "Running on the Pavement in Linkou, and Why Taiwan's Sidewalks Fail Us"

---

The best thing about living in Linkou is that I can keep running on the pavement. In Japan or Korea, or even in any city in Thailand or China, this is perfectly easy.

Only in Taiwan are almost all roads designed for vehicles, and the pavement is the garage, garden or storage area of the shops and homes along the street. At a traditional roadside market, you can buy vegetables from an auntie or uncle who creeps along on a scooter and shouts out prices from the saddle. Most local residents hardly walk at all; even a breakfast shop 10 metres from home is reached by scooter. Famous scenic areas such as Guohua Street, Kenting Street and Sun Moon Lake are the best examples: stalls and shops sell snacks in layers under the arcades, while tourists and locals mingle on the road. Photos of this chaos end up in Japanese magazines and we still think it is romantic.

The hard part is that once we see the problem, how do we improve it?

Start with ourselves. Stop buying scooters, make good use of public transport or YouBike, and walk as much as possible for short distances. Support shops with proper storefronts, and give a little more support to businesses that pay taxes. When travelling elsewhere, buy less from street vendors and shop at stores instead, to encourage those who are willing to upgrade.
