Bangkok Travel Day 3: Klook Maeklong Railway and Floating Market Group Tour Experience

As an independent traveller, I joined a one-day tour run by a local travel agency. The price was reasonable and the standard good enough. The coach had people from many countries, and the guide, A-Mu, explained first in English and then in Chinese. The itinerary was simple yet rich, and the timing was a matter of experience and skill; it would be hard to achieve on my own. The only thing I found hard to get used to was getting up early to make the meeting time, which is one of the most troublesome things about travelling.

The tour began at the Maeklong Railway Market, departing from the meeting point at Central One in Bangkok. Because I worry about motion sickness, I still prefer large coaches when choosing tours, which feel less cramped, and the guide did not talk at length or push sales on the bus, so it was very comfortable and pleasant. I had visited the Maeklong station and the sight of the train passing through the market once before, on a graduation trip with students. The market of a dozen or so years ago was much plainer than now, mainly local fresh fish, vegetables and fruit; but today many stalls have turned to selling identical souvenirs and relaxing cafes. The itinerary lets you watch the train arrive and depart once each, and as soon as the train leaves, we got back on the coach and left.

In the second part, after half an hour on the road, we reached the boat landing of the Damnoen Saduak Floating Market. A long-tail boat with a startlingly powerful motor seats six. The ride is thrilling, and a video lets you feel it more as if you were there. Personally, I feel we have lost the gentle sway of the old hand-paddled boats, and the motorised long-tail boat is a bit noisy, but it is still an exciting and fun voyage, and I recommend it just the same, though for elderly people or those with mobility problems I suggest not joining.
