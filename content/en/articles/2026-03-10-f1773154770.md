Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep EP95

#4DaysToTheTourLeaderExam

44 Lombok and Bali in Indonesia are two neighbouring islands. What is the main religion of the residents of Lombok? (A) Buddhism (B) Catholicism (C) Hinduism (D) Islam

The answer is (D)

Put another way, on almost every island of Indonesia the majority of people are Muslim, and Bali, where most people are Hindu, is the unusual exception.

What is even more interesting is that the dividing line between these two islands is also very famous in geography: the well-known Wallace Line, which separates the biomes of Asia and Australia.

#1 Regarding the special nature of the Wallace Line in Indonesian tourism geography, which of the following statements would best help a tour leader spark travellers' interest in "biodiversity" when guiding?

(A) The line happens to coincide with Indonesia's religious boundary, with Hinduism to the west and Islam to the east.

(B) The strait nearby is estimated to have never been bridged between the two islands even when sea levels dropped during the Ice Age, so species evolved in long isolation.

(C) The plant landscapes on both sides of the Wallace Line are exactly the same, and the main difference is only the distribution of mineral resources.

(D) The world-famous Komodo dragon can only be seen in Bali, and Lombok has none at all.

Answer: (B)

#2 A tour leader leads an Indonesian island-hopping eco-tour to see the world's largest lizard, the Komodo dragon. Which of the following statements about the Komodo dragon's ecology and geographical background is incorrect?

(A) Komodo Island lies east of the Wallace Line and has distinctive transitional biological features.

(B) The Komodo dragon's huge size is related to its long evolution on isolated islands without competition from large carnivorous mammals.

(C) To see wild Komodo dragons, travellers should go to Komodo National Park, which has been listed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site.

(D) The Komodo dragon is the most famous wild animal representative of Bali and can be seen roaming everywhere in Bali's urban areas, where it is regarded as the local guardian spirit.

Answer: (D)
