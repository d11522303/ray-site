Godot x HWU x Teaching x Performance

I love reading novels and stories. Every night, the novels of Keigo Higashino, Teru Miyamoto or Miyuki Miyabe accompany me to sleep, and I let the plots and characters on the page turn me into an onlooker or a participant alongside the author, and we fall asleep together into a wonderful play. One day a student from my department came to ask me to sponsor the school's peace-of-mind student assistance event, saying a donation would earn a ticket to a musical. I donated right away, and by happy chance I got to watch the final-project musical of the musical theatre programme that HWU trains together with Godot Theatre Company.

Last night, after a full day of teaching on Friday and feeling exhausted, I went wearily to the Guangyi Hall at Quanta Computer headquarters in the Huaya Park. I never expected to take part in an absolutely wonderful musical that travels through time, and my whole day's fatigue was swept away. Everyone in the cast was a student from HWU's Department of Performing Arts. First-rate dialogue and blocking, first-rate music and lighting, first-rate dance and sets: it was a complete feast of theatre. You can imagine how much time the teachers and students behind the scenes spent learning and rehearsing, and how they gave all their energy and strength to the arranging of music and choreography, and to the script and lyric writing.

In the smoothly flowing 100-minute musical, I was won over by several original songs and the lead-singer student. There were many brilliant dance performances, and the combination of drama and dance, in a professional theatre design, strung scenes that cross time into the most beautiful and least regretful musical ending in life. Afterwards I truly felt the students were so professional, the teachers so dedicated, and the time so well spent. My family kept asking whether there would be a public performance so we could buy tickets and see it again (wow! As a member of HWU I am really a bit proud).

In recent years, as the competition for vocational students has intensified, the integration of teaching and practice and the design of teachers' teaching have become critical. Courses cannot lack substance, nor be too thin or too far from practice. Bringing industry resources into teaching is already the basic skill of every department at HWU. While watching the play I was also reviewing the blind spots in my own training and teaching, and thinking about more possibilities in teaching travel service practice. That is probably my biggest gain.

Thank you again to the teachers and students of HWU's performing arts department for giving me the most beautiful 🎶 minutes of my life.
