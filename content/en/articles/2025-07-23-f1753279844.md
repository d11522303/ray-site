New Taipei Travel Service Center Summer OJT Course @ HWU Department of Travel Management

A short two-hour class on a topic I'm not expert in took me more than ten hours to prepare, but it also forced me to read quite a few interesting journal articles and try to find the fun in them. I then broke the interesting academic findings down into course cases. Sharing them in class, everyone seemed to respond, which made it an interesting teaching experience too.

Let me first share two slides from the course. This is a paper published in a European work and organisational psychology journal, a study by three German scholars on how exercise after work affects mood. It also matches my own long-time exercise habit, and offers busy workers a way and a rationale for exercising.

The study collected 126 participants, who exercised after work on half of five consecutive working days, an average of 37.28 minutes per day, and completed an exercise diary twice a day. The exercise was flexible, and could be endurance training, strength training, team sports or other leisure sport. No particular exercise was required, but it had to be long enough to have an effect.

The findings were:

1. Exercise after work was significantly and positively associated with positive affect in the evening. This means people feel more positive emotions on the evenings after exercising.

2. Exercise was not significantly associated with negative affect. This may be because participants' negative emotions after work were already very low, or because negative emotions vary independently of exercise.

3. Satisfaction with oneself increased, and people felt their bodies were more attractive and their athletic ability better.

Good service is mostly judged by those being served, but in fact the role of the one providing the service is what matters. By facing the pressure of teaching, practising how to face it and then turning it into meaningful content, I am also providing quality service to myself!
