[Running and Climate Change]

Because running makes you more aware of the environment, I feel the relationship between temperature and my body every day. Since summer began, whenever I run the streets of urban Taipei, I mostly feel I am running through thick, humid air. Running on a plateau is better: at 250 metres elevation it is about 1.5°C cooler. Linkou now has no fog, and because the sea breeze blows through the surrounding forest, it is much more comfortable than the basin.

The attached image is a global temperature map for 6/10 taken from NCEP Climate Forecast System (CFS) data. The places that are red to the point of turning grey are over 40°C. In Asia, only the Tibetan Plateau looks cool, while everything from Europe to Siberia is warm.

Judging from the rising trend of the black line, we may be heading into the hottest summer in history. Around the turn of July and August, the 2023 global temperature (black line) keeps exceeding the +2°C average predicted by climate change forecasts.

Since 6/10 I have moved my running time to early morning. After exercising, my body also regulates itself better to room temperature, so I depend less on air conditioning. A DC fan saves electricity and is environmentally friendly.
