49 Which of the following countries holds the Holi festival at the March spring equinox, where people smear coloured paint on each other's faces and throw coloured powder and water balloons to celebrate the arrival of spring? (A) India (B) Brazil (C) the Netherlands (D) Indonesia

The answer is A

A few days ago I saw a Hsing Wu University senior living in India post a story about joining India's colourful Holi festival. She had also joined Diwali before. I'm very envious of how she fully experiences life among diverse cultures. India has very many distinctive festivals, and the year also includes the following major ones.

January: Harvest festival (Makar Sankranti / Pongal): Celebrates the sun's move northward. In South India sweet milk rice porridge is cooked to thank the farm cattle, while in Gujarat in the north it is known for large-scale kite-flying contests.

February: Maha Shivaratri: Honours Shiva, the god of destruction and regeneration. Devotees stay awake all night, meditate and offer milk to the "Shiva Lingam", symbolising the overcoming of darkness and ignorance.

March: Holi: People take to the streets throwing coloured powder and water, symbolising the arrival of spring and equality. It is also India's most energetic revelry, the one that most crosses class boundaries.

April: Mahavir Jayanti

May: Buddha Purnima (Vesak): Celebrates the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and nirvana. In places such as Bodh Gaya in North India there are solemn circumambulation rituals, with devotees dressed in white, meditating and giving alms.

June: Ganga Dussehra: Commemorates the descent of the goddess Ganga to earth. In cities along the Ganges such as Varanasi, thousands of people bathe in the river, believing it washes away past sins.

July: Guru Purnima: A fine tradition shared by Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism. Students pay respect to their teachers (Gurus), emphasising the importance of passing on knowledge. For educators it is a day of great cultural meaning.

August: Raksha Bandhan: Sisters tie a coloured thread (Rakhi) on their brothers' wrists, symbolising blessings for their brothers, while the brothers promise to protect their sisters for life and give gifts in return, showing deep family affection.

September: Ganesh Chaturthi: Celebrates the birth of Ganesha, the god of wisdom and remover of obstacles. In Mumbai in particular, countless gorgeous shrines are built along the streets. At the end of the festival, huge Ganesha statues are carried into the sea or rivers, symbolising the deity's return to the divine realm.

October: Durga Puja / Dussehra: This is the most important event in Kolkata in East India, celebrating the goddess's victory over evil. The streets are filled with temporary temples like art galleries. The Dussehra festival at the same time has the custom of burning effigies of the ten-headed demon king, symbolising that evil cannot defeat good.

November: Diwali: India's "Festival of Lights" and the grandest festival of the year. Every household lights oil lamps, decorates with colourful designs (Rangoli) and sets off fireworks to welcome Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, praying for a peaceful and prosperous year ahead.
