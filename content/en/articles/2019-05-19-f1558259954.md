[Junior High Comprehensive Assessment]

With a junior high student at home, even weekends are spent looking at exam questions as a way to "rest".

I wonder how many parents try to work through their children's questions themselves, then compare them with the reading and writing ability of the college students I meet in my own classes. I feel it keenly.

Attached is the Chinese exam that just came out for the junior high comprehensive assessment. The single subject runs to 13 pages. Reading each question closely, and considering the length of the multi-part question sets, I really doubt how many students can finish 48 questions in 70 minutes. That is only about a minute and a half per question, including filling in the answer card. They must also fully read and think through such lengthy passages, and the answers are just as similar-looking and complicated, expecting students to pick out the intricate shades of meaning in Chinese.

Take the first question. It is actually a combination of 4 small questions, as follows:

"'Taotie' is a monster of ancient times. You can't find it in a field guide or at the zoo. Like the dragon and the phoenix, it is an imaginary animal. □ it exists in the culture, □□ it is vivid and lifelike, □□ it has a distinct visual image, □ it breeds rich symbolic meanings." Based on the context, which of the following fills in the blanks, in order, most appropriately?

(A) although / unexpectedly / apart from / yet (B) but / however / merely / also

(C) is / still / actually / and (D) only / but yet / not only / also

Can any mum or dad get this one right in the nervous minute and a half after receiving the paper?

In a world where reading trends are TikTok's 15 seconds and Twitter's 140 characters, I feel the Chinese exam doesn't show a clear ability to discriminate Chinese skills. It seems more like a stage for the question-setter to show off their command of language, and it even feels as if they enjoy stumping 15-year-olds in Taiwan.
