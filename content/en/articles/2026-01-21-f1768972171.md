Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Prep, Episode 48

#TourLeaderExamCountdown51Days

9 On 28 June 1914, which of the following important figures was assassinated, leading to the First World War?

(A) Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria (B) Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany (C) German Chancellor Bismarck (D) German Field Marshal Moltke

The answer to this question is (A).

Both world wars began in Europe, mainly because of differences in religion and ethnicity, together with changes in the form of warfare, which set off more chain reactions. Otherwise, wars have never been scarce in human history.

#WW1

The First World War began in Sarajevo (now Bosnia and Herzegovina) on the Balkan Peninsula, a sensitive area where Slavic peoples and those ruled by Austria-Hungary intermingled. The fighting was concentrated on the Western Front in northern France and the Belgian plains, as in the Battle of the Somme and the Battle of Verdun, where large numbers of French and German soldiers and local civilians were exposed to trench warfare for a long time. The Eastern Front affected multi-ethnic residents in Poland and Ukraine. The war ended in 1918 with an armistice signed in the Forest of Compiegne in France, and the final peace treaty, the Treaty of Versailles, was signed in 1919 at the Palace of Versailles near Paris, France.

#WW2

The European theatre of the Second World War centred on Germany, Poland, France and the Eastern European plains. Important actions included the German invasion of Poland and the Normandy landings, and large numbers of European civilians, especially Eastern Europeans and Jews, suffered deeply from the war and persecution. The Asia-Pacific theatre was concentrated in north and coastal China, the Philippines and the Pacific island chain, involving Chinese civilians, colonial peoples and the Japanese army. The war ended in 1945 with Japan signing the instrument of surrender aboard the US warship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay, and the 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty in San Francisco, USA, later completed the postwar peace framework.

#1 A tour leader says in the itinerary briefing: "This place was originally a British colonial port. After it was captured by the Japanese army in 1942 it was renamed, and after the war it regained its original name. Today the city centre still preserves wartime bunkers and POW memorial facilities, and it has become an important base for war tourism."

Judging from the description, which of the following is the city most likely to be?

A. Manila B. Singapore C. Hong Kong D. Ho Chi Minh City

Correct answer: B. During the Second World War, Singapore was occupied by Japan and renamed "Syonan-to" (Light of the South). Today it preserves the Old Ford Factory, war memorial parks and bunker ruins, making it a typical war tourism city.

#2 A tour leader plans a "Second World War Asian Theatre Heritage Tour" with a visit to a coastal underground military facility. The commentary says that the place was originally a European colonial port city, was occupied by the Japanese army during the Second World War and renamed, and that today its underground tunnels and air-raid shelters are open to the public and serve as a war tourism site. Judging from these historical and geographical clues, where is the place most likely located?

A. Jakarta, Indonesia

B. Penang, Malaysia

C. Manila, the Philippines

D. Da Nang, Vietnam

Correct answer: B. Penang (George Town) was a British colonial port, was occupied by Japan during the Second World War and renamed, and still preserves air-raid shelters, underground tunnels and a war museum, often included in war tourism and World Heritage itineraries.
