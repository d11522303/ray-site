-- When You See the World Through Tinted Glasses, the World Becomes the Colour of Your Lenses --

All of Asia is working hard to develop cruise tourism, and China has recently built giant cruise ships of its own, waiting to seize a huge share of the global cruise industry's growth. Yet when Taiwan's travel industry merely organises a cruise group tour, some people pick up a high-minded magnifying glass and scrutinise every shortcoming. This uses the pandemic as an excuse to bring the cruise industry to a standstill, and may even cost us the chance to develop during the global recovery.

Cruise tourism is a travel industry well suited to Taiwan's circumstances. There is training, there is expertise, and there are employees and companies who make a living from it. It gives the industry a chance to move towards high-quality, refined service through professionalism, and a chance for the outlying islands to add infrastructure and add value to all kinds of products and services. Yet at a time when every branch of tourism needs encouragement and support, a self-righteous and inappropriate label has been stuck on it.

It is like Taiwan's dot-com portal Yam (蕃薯藤) (anyone heard of it? It actually still exists!). At the end of the twentieth century it led the search engine market across Taiwan and even the Chinese-speaking world, with a great opportunity to commercialise and scale up, and even to influence the Chinese-language internet. But because its founder insisted on a public-interest mission, it missed its big chance to enter the online world, and in the end became a bubble that never quite burst.

Every trade and profession, from the mountains to the courts, works hard and shares in the rain and dew. This is the effort of the cruise travel industry under the pandemic, and its call for government policy support.

Please support the campaign promoted by Professor Chiang-Chuan Lu: "Fight for the Cruise Economy, Don't Stigmatise It".
