This is Jingtong in Pingxi, which I visited often last year. During a workshop discussion, a local young man mentioned that he had just become a father, and his child was the only newborn in Pingxi District last year. His words carried both helplessness about the environment and pride in his own choice.

What should we do about the falling birth rate? I don't know either! It is a question of national strategy. As a teacher teaching in a school, I only feel the campuses gradually growing quiet, and even the edges of the greater Taipei metropolis are gradually becoming remote areas. You don't need to go to Hualien and Taitung or indigenous areas: Pingxi District in New Taipei City, within half an hour's drive, is a near-remote area right beside us, and teaching resources and student sources are already insufficient.

No children means no children. Not marrying and not having children is a collective choice. Raising a child means losing much of what you already have. Although you later gain more, it also brings more loss at the same time. It is a multiple-choice question of life risk. Conservative people don't marry or have children and just live their own lives well; impulsive people have children first and figure it out later, since they say children bring wealth. Having children or not is a difficult question for those of marriageable age, and from my observation most modern young people choose the former.

Meanwhile, in recent years too many problems have appeared across all levels of the education environment: primary and secondary schools lack teachers, higher education lacks students, and in addition to the original role of teaching only, teachers have taken on administration, counselling, admissions and other duties, while their fixed salary hasn't changed. The workload has increased, and by working-conditions logic that should mean a raise, but the rigid education system has no such thing.

So teachers don't stay, and in the environment you rarely see pregnant mothers, and slowly the campuses grow quiet.
