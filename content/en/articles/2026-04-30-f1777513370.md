A Student's Disappointed Smile Taught Me to Praise

Teaching has been gradually changing in recent years, and I hold two views at once: on one side worry and concern, on the other challenge and opportunity. I hope what I feel and learn is positive. Watching students we coached after work and after class earn their certificates in the national exam, they are happy and we are proud, so I praise them loudly.

The reason goes back to an after-class moment a few years ago.......

A student came up to me and said: Teacher, my notes are great, aren't they? Give me some praise!

I was a bit stunned: You're great (even I felt it was perfunctory....)

The student smiled at me, looking a little disappointed.

I blamed myself for not giving the emotional support that was right for the moment.

After that I began learning, in my career, how to praise students and affirm what they do, and of course to encourage myself at the right time too: I did well, and I haven't lost points in my role as a teacher.

Over these years I've slowly found that education really has changed. Every student in front of me is an individual, and teaching needs designed interaction, and design above all. Words spoken in front of a class can easily get out of control. A few days ago, in front of nearly eighty students, I showed my emotions and said the wrong thing, and I still feel complicated about it.

These are the results of the tour leader and tour guide exams announced by the Tourism Administration the day before yesterday. Brother Kong and I studied with the students in an after-class coaching group for many months, and this is the students' excellent result. Please praise them, because they really worked hard and did brilliantly. And give us some praise too!
