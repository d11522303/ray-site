Bangkok Trip Day 5: Chulalongkorn University Campus and the Banthat Thong Road Night Market

It turns out Chulalongkorn University sits in the very heart of Bangkok. The whole campus is just south of BTS Siam Station, close to department stores and the shopping district. Thai university students also wear uniforms to school. Different schools' uniforms are naturally a little different, but we couldn't tell which school's students were wearing what.

In the middle of the trip, we had long planned to visit Chulalongkorn University. I was a bit worried we would be kept outside iron gates, as at Tsinghua University in Beijing or the Southwest Associated University site in Kunming, and the many sources I looked up said little about how to visit a campus in downtown Bangkok. As it turned out, Chulalongkorn, Thailand's top-ranked university, is just like National Taiwan University: every part of the campus has an entrance, and even tuk-tuks can drive in, with no controls at all. Come to think of it, this is surely what a university is for. If a university has strict access control, then however high it ranks in the world, will the talent it trains have an open mind, a reverence for civilisation and truth, and self-reflection before learning and knowledge? All of these would raise a few more question marks because of the gate.

The Chula campus is large, with a beautiful university history museum in royal-style architecture, and the faculties are spread across different areas. Along the way we strolled past the Faculty of Design and the Faculty of Engineering, and also went to the Faculty of Education and the Faculty of Law, where students were preparing summer camp activities and academic workshops. The campus is lush and green and well planned, students on the sports field were playing football, and the whole atmosphere was like visiting the University of Tokyo campus earlier this year: free, open and professional, which makes one long to be there. Later, when I looked up more information, I found that quite a few Taiwanese students study at this school, and the career prospects after graduating from Thailand's top institution are full of potential. I remember that in 2012, when I organised the Asia Tourism Forum, the professor from Chulalongkorn's Faculty of Architecture who visited Hualien presented research on tourism at Thailand's World Heritage sites. Thailand is a tourism powerhouse, and I truly envy its rich research resources in history and culture.

Walking out of the campus, you reach Banthat Thong Rd., the Chulalongkorn University food street that tourists love. This stretch of Banthat Thong Road, with food shops clustered along more than a kilometre, starts to feel lively from around four or five in the afternoon, and is a food centre for tourists and locals alike. Along the road there are quite a few Michelin-listed or internet-famous shops, so you can stroll and consider what to eat. Most of the shops are proper storefronts, and the dining environment is much better than Bangkok's other night markets or street stalls.

On the first day of August, we walked 18 kilometres and 25,376 steps in downtown Bangkok. Because Bangkok is hot, we ate as we went and enjoyed quite a few restaurants we stumbled upon, and the MyBeer we tried for the first time was delicious too, adding to the fun. For our dinner of grilled meats and hotpot we could pay only in Thai baht, but the prices were even more transparent and reasonable, and the toilets and dining environment were of a good standard. The night view and atmosphere of the food street were romantic and enjoyable. Highly recommended.
