QUOTE: Lin Hsien-Tang, with his eldest son Lin Pan-Lung and second son Lin Yu-Lung, set out on May 15, 1927, and arrived on the European continent on June 20 of the same year. They left on March 14 of the following year (1928) for North America, arriving in the United States on March 20. They then departed on May 9 and crossed the Pacific, reaching Yokohama, Japan, on May 25, and returned safely to Taiwan on November 8, 1928, a total journey of about a year and a half. Their sea travel was mainly aboard mail steamers (ocean liners). From Keelung they took the Hozan Maru, changed at Hong Kong to the Kashima Maru, and sailed via Singapore, Penang, Sri Lanka and the Suez Canal in Egypt to Marseille in France. They then toured eleven countries on the European continent, from Denmark to Spain, from Britain to Germany, from the Netherlands to Switzerland, that is, from island to mountain recess, from coast to inland. Here they travelled mainly by train and car, with trains in particular being the main means of transport between countries. Later they sailed from Cherbourg in northwestern France on the great steamship R.M.S. Aquitania to New York in the United States. They then bought a "combined ship-and-rail ticket" from Nippon Yusen Kaisha and took the train from Boston and Chicago to Los Angeles, then went on to San Francisco to board the Tenyo Maru bound for Japan, passing through Hawaii on the way. (Lin Hsien-Tang, Travel Notes on a Journey Around the World)
