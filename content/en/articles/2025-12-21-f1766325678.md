14 Kinkaku-ji in Kyoto is one of Japan's famous historical and cultural sites. This temple was built in the late fourteenth century, but its original purpose was not as a religious site but as a private villa. Who originally built Kinkaku-ji as a private villa? ① Tokugawa Ieyasu ② Ashikaga Yoshimitsu ③ Oda Nobunaga ④ Toyotomi Hideyoshi

Explanation:

The shifting power centres of the four important figures Tokugawa Ieyasu, Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi also reflect the shifting political centre of gravity in Japan. Ashikaga Yoshimitsu held power from the late fourteenth to the early fifteenth century, with the Muromachi shogunate based in Kyoto, consolidating his rule within the existing political core. In the sixteenth-century Sengoku period, Oda Nobunaga rose from Owari Province (now western Aichi Prefecture) and entered Kyoto (now Kyoto Prefecture) in 1568, effectively controlling the imperial court. After that, Toyotomi Hideyoshi (1537-1598) completed the unification in the late sixteenth century, taking Osaka (now Osaka City, Osaka Prefecture) as his power centre and building Osaka Castle. Finally, after Tokugawa Ieyasu's shogunate took power, it established its capital at Edo (now Tokyo) in 1603 and founded the Edo shogunate, shifting Japan's political centre from the Kansai to the Kanto region and ushering in a long era of stable shogunate rule.

Kinkaku-ji (Rokuon-ji) was originally the mountain villa "Kitayama-den", built by Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, the third shogun of the Muromachi shogunate, in the late fourteenth century to display his political power and cultural taste. After Ashikaga Yoshimitsu died, it was converted into a Zen temple according to his last wishes, becoming the religious and cultural heritage we see today. So the correct answer to this question is Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, who established his shogunate in Kyoto.
