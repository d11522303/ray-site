Serious accident involving Spain's newest Iryo high-speed train

This is the second tragedy on Spain's high-speed rail in recent years, and as someone who loves rail travel I am deeply shaken. According to preliminary reports, the rear of a northbound train derailed and collided head-on with a southbound train. The rear of the northbound train, operated by the private rail company Iryo, struck the front of the southbound train, operated by the state railway company Renfe. The impact overturned both trains, and the front carriage of the southbound train fell down an embankment. The derailed train belonged to the private company Iryo and was less than 4 years old, while the other train, which was most severely hit, belonged to Spain's national railway company Renfe.

The derailed Iryo Freccia 1000 train is owned by a private Spanish high-speed rail company and is Spain's newest high-speed train. Its technology comes from Hitachi and Alstom, and it was assembled in Italy. Iryo trains have three classes designed for business travellers, but all seats are equipped with USB ports and standard power sockets, individual armrests and free 5G Wi-Fi.

Spain's transport minister said the accident was extremely rare because it happened on a straight stretch of track that had recently been renovated as part of a 700 million euro investment project. Spain's national railway company said the trains were travelling below the speed limit for that section, and that the signalling system would prevent trains from exceeding that limit. He said the limit at the time was 250 km/h (150 mph). One train was travelling at 205 km/h and the other at 210 km/h. A source at the Spanish train drivers' union told CNN that the union had earlier raised concerns about vibration along the high-speed line and the possible wear that followed.

The source said that last August the union wrote to the national rail operator Adif asking it to reduce the maximum speed on all high-speed lines from 300 km/h to 250 km/h, because vibrations in some sections were putting stress on the infrastructure. The source could not confirm whether these warnings were specifically about the area outside Cordoba where the accident occurred, but said the section had undergone maintenance in May. Trigueros, president of the Spanish engineering association, told Reuters that it is too early to speculate about the cause of the accident. In theory, all factors indicate that this should not be possible, and that is the only reality we face, Trigueros said; if the train had no fault, then some component on the track was not in the perfect condition it should have been.
