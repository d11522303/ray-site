2026.7.30

I have finally set foot on North American soil. For many years I dreamed of studying for a PhD in the UK, the US or Canada, learning to think and work in English, and I even vowed not to step into the US without an admission letter. Later I gradually found that to study in the US or Canada you need either brainpower or financial power. The brilliant take exams for government funding or scholarships; ordinary people can only choose regions with lower tuition and living costs and then compete for some financial support to come to the US as poor students.

This year, because of my child, who won a place in National Tsing Hua University's electrical engineering exchange programme and can go on exchange to UCLA, working together with roommates to write proposals, apply for scholarships and handle enrolment and housing procedures with the school, the students had the chance to set foot in the US together. To me, a father whose English has never gotten good, some things really can't be done without good English. Studying is completely different from travelling. Preparing for the role and having the ability are both challenges, and this kind of courage slowly diminishes with age. In my own case this time, thoughts of backing out surfaced several times before leaving, until I even disliked myself.
