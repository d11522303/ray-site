7/24-25

Non-Stop at Narada Hotel Group

On the second and third days in Hangzhou, I visited students on internships at four companies, including Narada Hotel Group (NARADA HOTEL GROUP).

Although Narada is not a listed company, its hotels are spread across 11 provinces and municipalities, including Zhejiang, Hainan, Yunnan, Shanghai, Chongqing, Jiangsu, Hubei, Jiangxi, Anhui, Fujian and Shanxi. It invests in and manages more than 50 hotels with over 15,000 rooms in total. Today the HR Director at headquarters, Director Xu, even mentioned that Narada is growing at an astonishing pace of 25 newly signed managed hotels a year, which creates a huge demand for hotel human resources.

This July's interns come from the graduate schools of Chinese Culture University and Providence University, plus 15 students from National Chi Nan University, I-Shou University and Hsing Wu University. Their jobs include HR specialists, administrative product planners, and Chinese and Western restaurant service staff within the group. The students have adapted well and gained a great deal. One of them even took part in the HRD annual conference, attending with the director a grand gathering of HR managers from all over the country.

The weather in Hangzhou is still scorching hot and humid, and the students who commute every day between their accommodation and workplaces have had a hard time. The one-month internship will end next Tuesday. Having the chance to live and work in Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province, should be the most unforgettable summer plan of 2018.

Hangzhou looks like one big garden, green everywhere, with electric vehicles, buses and subways reaching every corner. You would never guess it is a big city with nearly ten million registered residents. Famous e-commerce companies such as Alibaba, Ant Financial and NetEase are all based in Hangzhou, and the working population is estimated at around twenty million, so in business, economy, work and tourism it is one of the most advanced cities south of the Yangtze. Beside the thousand-year-old West Lake, I think the students and I all felt that beneath the timeless beauty of Hangzhou lies leapfrog development, moving forward at a speed that is hard to comprehend.
