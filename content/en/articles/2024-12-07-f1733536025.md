title: "Memories of Going Door to Door Looking for Work as a Boy"
excerpt: "Ray recalls walking the shops outside Zhonghe's temple as a worried ten-year-old, asking for a job, and the summer he spent checking circuit boards in a small electronics factory."

---

<p>In the 1980s, the main road in front of the Zhonghe temple square, four lanes wide, was an important route for Taipei Bus Route 10 running between Muzha and Banqiao. The temple square was lively. As a child I had no idea which deity the temple housed, but because I often passed the stalls and shops on both sides on my way home from school, and there was a night market behind the temple, the shops along Zhonghe Road did good business for decades, brightly lit every night. Only when I grew up did I learn that this temple, dating back to the eighteenth century, is called Guangji Temple and enshrines Chen Yuanguang, the Sage King who Opened Zhangzhou, an ancestor of us Hoklo people. Behind it stands another temple, Fuhe Temple, dedicated to Shennong, the Divine Farmer. Thanks to its flourishing incense and its position on a main thoroughfare, it became the birthplace of most of Zhonghe's commercial activity.</p><p>I remember one evening after school, when I was about ten, worrying about money in my heart. I walked to the long row of shops in front of the temple. I first passed the toy and model shop on Nanshan Road and peered in at the die-cast alloy kits, but the lady in the shop recognised us brothers, so I looked briefly and left. Then I went along the long street by the temple's dragon side, asking shop after shop whether there was any chance of a part-time job. Thinking about it now, of course nobody would hire a primary or junior high school student. I was thin and frail from childhood, with no appetite for food, probably because I was always worrying about this and that. Naturally I didn't grow tall, my limbs were thin and weak, and my gaunt face looked listless by comparison.</p><p>I remember pushing open the door of a pharmacy and asking whether they had any work a teenager could do. Almost 40 years later, I remember a lady answering me: where are your mum and dad? We have no work to give you. Later I went to a shop that had no door. I can't recall what it sold. They were watching television, and waved me off without even a glance. I felt a bit sad inside. I had graduated with the County Magistrate Lin Feng-cheng Award and thought of myself as a teacher's favourite good student, and I assumed my self-assumed cleverness could find me a job to earn money, but then I found it seemed useless. I was probably just too young and didn't yet know enough.</p><p>Later I did find work. Not long afterwards, through my mother's introduction, I went to an electronics factory under the structure of the still-unopened Yongfu Bridge, checking circuit boards. The little factory was on the ground floor, right under the bridge pier that now leads toward Gongguan. Looking up you could see the head of the Yongfu Bridge that had never opened to traffic. It really was only the head of a bridge, pointing firmly at the Xindian River with no span beyond it.</p><p>I worked at the electronics factory until Yonghe Junior High School started again. Looking back now, it must have been the summer before the second year of junior high. The work was dull: I kept holding a board and looking for wires that were poorly connected and might short-circuit, and each day I had to inspect a great many circuit sheets. When I think about it, why I wasn't studying then can no longer be traced. At least my classmates who attended the affiliated high school were probably at cram schools studying ahead of the syllabus or learning talents, living a comfortable summer vacation.</p><p>Two months later I didn't get paid. My mother may have collected it. I still remember that.</p>
