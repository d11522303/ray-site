5. The ancient Indus Valley Civilisation spread across parts of today's Pakistan and India, and is known for its urban planning, drainage systems and building techniques. Which site can visitors today tour to understand the urban structure and culture of this ancient civilisation?

① Lhasa in Tibet ② Samarkand in Central Asia ③ Varanasi, an ancient city in India's Ganges Basin ④ Mohenjo-daro in Pakistan

Solution: This question is really not easy. Using geographical knowledge and the process of elimination from the clues in the question, you can only rule out ① and ②, and have to guess between ③ and ④. Guess what my answer was at the time? No asking AI or Googling first!

Throughout history, success in water engineering has made and unmade empires, and because Central Asia lacks water resources, the Mohenjo-daro site in the Indus River Valley of present-day Pakistan is a model of the water-engineering civilisation of the ancient Indus 4,500 years ago. Through its grid-planned streets, Great Bath, advanced red-brick building complexes, comprehensive drainage system and the orderly housing of the lower town, visitors can deeply understand its highly developed urban management, public works and residents' quality of life. It shows a rare modern city character, and it may well have been a city built by the joint efforts of its citizens, rather than a power-centre city arising from kings or religion.

The National Geographic video in the comments mentions that the Great Bath inside the citadel area of Mohenjo-daro was built more than two thousand years earlier than the famous Roman baths, not to mention the complete system separating waste water from water supply. It is like a city model planned by modern people, yet it appeared 4,500 years ago, which is amazing!
