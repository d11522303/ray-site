Tour Guide and Tour Leader Exam Countdown EP14

#2025 (ROC Year 114) Tourism Administration Tour Leader Exam Question

39 "Three Mountains and Five Sacred Peaks", with rivers and streams stretching ten thousand li. Which three mountains are the "three mountains" widely known among the people? ① Huangshan ② Yandangshan ③ Huashan ④ Songshan ⑤ Hengshan ⑥ Lushan

Explanation: Among the "Three Mountains and Five Sacred Peaks", the three mountains widely known among the people are Huangshan, Lushan and Yandangshan. The Five Sacred Peaks are Mount Tai in the east, Mount Hua in the west, Mount Heng (Hunan) in the south, Mount Heng (Shanxi) in the north, and Mount Song in the centre. If you want to travel there, I suggest starting with those listed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Besides the beautiful scenery, you can collect three World Heritage sites:

＃MountTai: Located in Shandong, China's first dual World Cultural and Natural Heritage site. It is famous for its majestic grandeur and the imperial Fengshan sacrifices, and is known as "the foremost of the Five Sacred Peaks". With deep cultural roots, it is regarded as a symbol of the Chinese nation.

＃Huangshan: Located in Anhui, a dual World Natural and Cultural Heritage site. It is known for its four wonders: strange pines, odd rocks, seas of clouds and hot springs. The artistic conception of landscape painting is condensed here, making it a representative of Chinese landscape aesthetics.

＃Lushan: Located in Jiujiang, Jiangxi, a World Cultural Heritage site. It is the birthplace of the Quaternary glaciation landform theory, with rich Buddhist and Taoist culture and abundant literary and artistic landscapes. Su Shi's poem "Inscribed on the Wall at Xilin Temple" from the Song Dynasty, with the line "I cannot tell the true face of Lushan, because I am within this very mountain", is a masterpiece.

＃1 If we take "whether national governance capacity can intervene on a large scale in the natural environment" as the criterion, which type of World Heritage site best reflects highly institutionalised spatial planning and governance?

① World Natural Heritage sites in the southwestern mountains (e.g. Three Parallel Rivers, Jiuzhaigou)

② Mountain-type dual heritage sites (e.g. Huangshan, Mount Tai)

③ World Cultural Heritage sites in plains regions (e.g. the Ming and Qing Imperial Palaces, the Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, the Archaeological Ruins of Liangzhu City)

④ World Cultural Heritage sites in the Hexi Corridor (e.g. the Mogao Caves, Maijishan Grottoes)

＃2 Which World Heritage site among China's mountains displays both natural scenery and cultural thought, and combines religious belief, imperial political symbolism and the tradition of mountain worship?

① Lushan ② Huangshan ③ Mount Tai ④ Yandangshan
