2024.10.6

The Dodgers are playing today. My English is terrible, and I only vaguely remember this word from when I took a TOEIC cram class. It comes from the verb "dodge", so a dodger is someone who evades or dodges, and dodgeball is the game of the same name. At the time I was really curious why a team would be named with a word for such an odd behaviour.

I looked it up in an encyclopaedia and found that the Dodgers did not originate in Los Angeles. They are a long-established team from Brooklyn, New York. In those days, locals coming to watch games had to dodge the trolleys (a kind of rail streetcar) running along the streets, so the team was nicknamed the Trolley Dodgers, and the team name "Dodgers" came from the character of the local fans.

A little trivia question: under the A, B and C right-of-way categories used in rail transport today, which category does this kind of shared right-of-way, where people, street trams and even vehicles share the road surface, belong to?
