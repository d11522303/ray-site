Wine Estate Tourism, Lesson One: Johnnie Walker Follows the Money

I'll be teaching about wineries next semester, so let's start by talking about a 200-year-old whisky brand, Johnnie Walker. Its founder, Mr. John Walker, was actually a small trader who never touched a drop of alcohol, like a tobacconist who doesn't smoke or a pig butcher who doesn't eat pork. The Walker family began making big money as the laws changed: when liquor taxes were greatly reduced they went into the liquor trade, and when blending was permitted they began making blended whisky, buying malt whisky from various distilleries and blending it in the proportions people loved most.

Their family also started out renting and selling export warehouses, so the fuller each case could be packed, the more money they made. They therefore designed a new square bottle that optimised the number of bottles and the packing of export cases, meaning more bottles could fit in the same space while breakages were reduced. This is the origin of the Johnnie Walker square bottle. What a clever businessman.

The Walker family also graded their labels. Because labels only need to be printed, it was economical and cut costs, and grading by colour let them segment target customers and prices. So on the market you can see Johnnie Walker in red, black, green, gold, blue and more, from low price to high.

So when you give this bottle to a friend, pay close attention to the label colour, because one glance tells how much you spent on your visit.

If you go to a banquet and everyone is really drinking Johnnie Walker with the meal, see whether the bottles run from Blue Label down to Red Label according to the table order and seating, and you will know your place in the host's heart.

That's the first lesson of winery tourism. I'm off to check the cupboard to see what colour the Johnnie Walker was that someone gave me last time.
