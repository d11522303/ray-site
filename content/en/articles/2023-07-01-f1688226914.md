[2023 Higher Education Sprout Project, Phase Two: Universities of Technology]

Continuing from the previous post, this one is about Taiwan's technological and vocational universities and colleges, which the media ignores, parents don't understand, and which are only occasionally called the "Pride of Taiwan" in passing.

First, a question: in which city or county is each of these schools located? Cheng Shiu (), Southern Taiwan (), Kun Shan (), Lunghwa (), Hungkuang (), Ming Chi (), Chia Nan (), Chaoyang (), Shu-Te (), Chihlee (), Hsing Wu (). These 11 universities are all among the top twenty private universities of technology in the latest round of the Higher Education Sprout Project. The public schools are easy to name, since almost all of them are national universities named after a place.

[More private than public in the top twenty]

Among the top twenty technical universities receiving subsidies, 11 are private and 9 are public. The media rarely reports on the importance and efforts of private technical universities. It was only because this round of the Sprout Project for vocational higher education evaluated teaching and industry cooperation that these schools, which work hard to train industry talent, came to the surface.

[Heavy on universities, light on vocational education]

All 79 technical universities in Taiwan together received only NT$5.6 billion in subsidies, less than the NT$6.4 billion received by the four top universities (NTU, NTHU, NCKU and NYCU) combined. In 2021, Taiwan had 1.18 million college students: 640,000 at general universities and 540,000 at technical universities. General universities received NT$12.6 billion, twice the funding of the technical ones.

[The strong take more, the weak take less: survival of the fittest among technical universities]

The Sprout Project is a competitive programme, so the strong receive more and the weak receive less. You can see it in the funding distribution chart: universities ranked in the lower part of the competition receive only token amounts. In this era of a falling birth rate, universities are already caught in a law of the jungle.

[Vocational teachers and students: hidden champions]

Perhaps in the competition over handwritten school subjects such as Chinese, English, maths, science, literature, history and civics, test scores once defined these vocational students as being at the "back of the line". But vocational schools and their teachers work hard to design courses, emphasise connection to the workplace, actively engage with what companies need, and help students break through their self-imposed limits. They strengthen hands-on ability through training, rebuild confidence through skills, and so redefine what "the back of the line" means.

I really want to give a big encouragement to every vocational teacher who trains students in skills, coaches language, and prepares them for professional certificates. In this day and age we still insist that students respect workplace ethics and proper conduct, and learn that in service you must bow, smile, and be quick on your feet. Some may say we are training an unsuitable kind of labour. But if you joined us in teaching and training in the vocational education environment and cared about students' learning and development, you would know why we are so proud to give ourselves, and the students we have trained, a big round of applause. I hope society sees this group of earnest, hardworking pillars of the community.
