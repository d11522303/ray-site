Hsing Wu Travel Management's Year-Long Internship at UNI Air Outlying-Island Stations Begins

After finals, most students moving from third to fourth year are preparing to welcome their last summer vacation of university, but the students of the HWU Department of Travel Management are preparing to enter the most important change and challenge of their university years: a full-time corporate internship. To all students who chose the internship: your teachers are proud of you. You are entering real practice and bravely facing workplace challenges.

In recent years, UNI Air's outlying-island internship positions have been a key goal for HWU Travel Management students. Each year 2 to 4 students, after passing layer upon layer of assessment, are assigned to the Kinmen, Penghu, Nangan and Beigan airports for one year of ground service training and internship. Each selected student receives from UNI Air free round-trip tickets to report for duty and several half-price tickets to return home. But when they have just turned 21, they must leave home for a whole year and face strict training and a changing workplace alone. Needless to say, how much their parents ache for them, and even the teachers feel reluctant to let go.

These past few days the department chair and teachers have almost all been feeling unwell, but being able to go to the airport to see off the students we have looked after for three years as they walk alone through the departure checkpoint, and to give a bit more reminder and encouragement face to face, felt necessary.

On the learning path of private technical university students, the fourth-year internship is, for most, the last mile into the workplace. The companies that the Department of Travel Management cooperates with also use students to test the results of our teaching and training. In recent years we have felt deeply that the new generation of students care more about their own future than we do, and teachers are the ones building bridges and leading the way to find opportunities for them, repeatedly reminding them and serving as their backing, because once students walk into the checkpoint alone, they have to take hold of their own direction and height.
