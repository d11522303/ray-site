2026.7.29

I only went online today to open the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) review comments. Not facing something I haven't passed has always been my bad habit. I thought I had no problems, but in fact there were plenty.

I am pasting the three reviewers' valuable comments into today's post. In the days ahead I will write journal papers properly, find joy in research, and semi-retire at work.

First-round Reviewer 1:

Comments and suggestions on the proposal:

The applicant proposes:

The subjects of this research project are vocational college students undergoing cross-disciplinary empowerment before an internship in the tourism and travel industry. The research process uses AR guided-tour games and a sustainable tourism ESG course across disciplines, applies a longitudinal study based on multi-dimensional scaffolding theory, and designs a research process that uses AR digital tools to guide students to understand sustainable ESG perspectives, with co-teaching by industry mentors and related course application teaching. In the teaching process, a quasi-experimental design will be used to classify the research subjects, the ARCS scale will be used for statistical research along with qualitative records of students' learning perceptions, to analyse and understand the effectiveness of students' learning process, and an experimental test design with interleaved practice and perspectives will be used to examine whether the teaching application converts teaching content into practical application ability.

I think the intent of this project is good, but the overall design leans toward teaching practice. The project aims to "explore how AR guided-tour game practice-oriented learning (PBL), supported by multi-dimensional scaffolding theory, affects the learning outcomes of vocational students in an ESG sustainable tourism course." It lacks a theoretical breakthrough, and the applicant might consider applying for the Ministry of Education's Teaching Practice Research Program (the author also notes on page 12: "This study is a teaching practice study centred on problems in the teaching field").

In addition, in terms of the strength of practice, I suggest the applicant consider how to extend it from the campus to off-campus, giving students a more authentic test. In reviewing different teaching application theories, besides PBI, I suggest the applicant also review more, to increase the completeness of the theoretical thinking.

Comments on representative research outputs:

The format of the works listed in the applicant's personal data sheet is not the usual NSTC style and is hard to read. For example: 2017, Hospitality Education, Study on Motivation and Continuation Intention of Vocational High School Catering Students: The Case of Generation 2K, vocational students quantitative related, regression, domestic academic journal.

Besides exceeding five years (2007-2023), the format is also not the usual academic presentation. Moreover, the overall presentation of the works is quite hard to understand.

First-round Reviewer 2:

Comments and suggestions on the proposal:

1. This project takes sustainable tourism teaching in a vocational education context as its research topic and combines concepts such as AR gamified learning, multi-dimensional scaffolding and practice-based teaching, which has a certain degree of teaching innovation.

2. However, the current research objectives are mainly concentrated on building a teaching model, developing lesson plans and materials, and evaluating student learning outcomes, so the overall research positioning leans toward teaching practice and curriculum development. By comparison, the project is still somewhat lacking in theoretical research questions, conceptual framework and hypothesis development, and does not clearly explain how its results will make new theoretical contributions to existing learning theories (such as Scaffolding Theory, Practice-Based Learning or the ARCS motivation model). I suggest the applicant further strengthen the theoretical questions and academic contribution of the research, to improve its positioning as an academic research project.

3. In terms of research design, the experimental group integrates multiple teaching elements, including the AR game, multi-dimensional scaffolding support and a live-person NPC guide played by the industry mentor, while the control group receives traditional lecture-based teaching. Because multiple teaching design elements are present at the same time, the proposal does not clearly explain the specific way each type of scaffolding (such as cognitive, procedural, strategic, affective and peer scaffolding) is operated in the course, or how different teaching activities correspond to these scaffolding types, so the definition and implementation of the experimental manipulation remain somewhat vague. In addition, the project does not explain whether a manipulation check is designed to confirm that students did feel the different types of scaffolding support during learning, which may affect the internal validity of the results.

4. The correspondence between some research questions and the research design also needs further explanation. For example, research question Q3 mentions the effect of affective scaffolding and the live-person NPC on immersive experience, but the current experimental design only compares overall AR scaffolded teaching with traditional teaching, making it hard to separately identify the source of the effect of the NPC or affective scaffolding. Likewise, if the research intends to explore the role of different scaffolding types in the learning process, it should explain more clearly its teaching intervention methods and analysis strategy.

5. On qualitative data collection, the project mentions qualitative analysis through content analysis of worksheets and student interviews, which helps in understanding students' learning process. However, the explanation of the qualitative data analysis methods (such as analysis procedures, coding methods or analytic framework) in the proposal is still rather brief. I suggest adding relevant research procedures to improve the rigour of the research methods.

6. Research question Q5 proposes to examine, from a longitudinal perspective, the continuing influence on students' ESG practical operation ability and professional guiding confidence after they enter industry internships (months 6 to 12). However, the proposal has not yet specified the data collection methods and evaluation indicators for the follow-up, such as how students' ESG practical ability in the internship setting will be measured, and whether it will be measured through questionnaires, interviews or industry assessment. The research design for this longitudinal transfer effect therefore still needs to be made clearer.

Comments on representative research outputs:

Among the representative outputs listed by the applicant, there is currently only one paper in an SSCI journal, and the applicant is neither the first author nor the corresponding author. The rest are mostly domestic journal or conference papers, with relatively limited academic influence and visibility in the academic community. The applicant's recent academic outputs are mostly concentrated on tourism or leisure-related topics, with no obvious direct link to the project's focus on "AR teaching" or "multi-dimensional scaffolding." Although the publication list shows that the applicant has published several studies related to hospitality or tourism education, they are concentrated in the earlier period (about 2011-2017), and continuity in education or teaching-innovation research in recent years is relatively limited. I suggest the applicant explain the relationship and continuity between this project and existing research outputs, to demonstrate the applicant's research ability in this field.

Second-round Reviewer:

(1) Content of the proposal

Against the background of vocational tourism education, the applicant combines AR guided-tour games, multi-dimensional scaffolding, ESG sustainable tourism and co-teaching by industry mentors, attempting to improve students' learning motivation, understanding of sustainable tourism and practical application ability. Overall, the intent of the project is good and it responds to the gap between theory and practice in vocational education. However, the project currently leans toward curriculum innovation and teaching practice, and as an academic research project its theoretical contribution, research design and analytical framework still have clear shortcomings. Specific suggestions follow:

1. The project uses concepts such as multi-dimensional scaffolding, PBI, experiential learning, ARCS, AR gamification and ESG education at the same time, but the hierarchical relationship and division of labour among these theories is unclear, which easily becomes too sprawling and loses focus. I suggest clarifying the core theory and avoiding listing multiple teaching concepts side by side without integrating them.

2. The experimental group includes the AR guided-tour game, multi-dimensional scaffolding, industry mentor NPCs, ESG materials and practical tasks at the same time, while the control group receives traditional teaching. If the post-test results differ, it will be hard to tell whether the difference comes from the AR tool, scaffolding support, industry mentor guidance or gamification design. I suggest redesigning the manipulation, or clearly defining the core intervention factor this study intends to test.

3. The project mentions content analysis, worksheets, reflections and interviews, but the explanation of the coding procedure, unit of analysis, reliability checks and researcher triangulation is insufficient. If the qualitative data will be an important basis for understanding the learning process, it needs more rigorous planning.

4. The study repeatedly mentions key variables such as "ESG sustainable tourism practical ability," "guiding professional ability" and "occupational competency maturity," but does not clearly define their dimensions or measurement, nor explain how the abstract ESG concept is converted into assessable learning outcomes.

5. AR and gamified teaching may themselves produce a "novelty effect," raising students' learning motivation in the short term, but this does not necessarily mean the teaching itself has long-term effects. In addition, operating AR technology may increase learners' "cognitive load" and affect the learning process. I suggest considering and proposing specific control or testing strategies.

(2) Representative research outputs

Among the applicant's journal publications over the past ten years, only 1 paper is in an SSCI journal (Sustainability); the rest are mostly domestic journals (Journal of Leisure Industry Management, Hsing Wu Journal) or conference papers, so overall academic output is relatively limited. The research topics, cruise travel and cross-disciplinary tourism and leisure issues, are rather scattered, with related studies mostly applied or empirical case studies, and mostly co-authored. Overall, I hope the applicant will continue to deepen research publication and strengthen the continued accumulation and improvement of academic capacity.
