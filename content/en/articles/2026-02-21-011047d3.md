<p>Daily Morning Reading - 2 / 2026.2.21 (5th day of the Lunar New Year) </p><p>Continuing with the academic report from the IJTR, another finding in the study is also very interesting. It discusses the four traits of a travel companion: "travel partner exchange", as a mediating factor, has a positive effect on memorable travel experiences, and the gender of the travel partner has a moderating effect.</p><p>First, let's review the four traits of the best travel partner mentioned in the paper (emotional intelligence, travel experience, congruence, and conscientiousness); these four terms are the academic wording used in the paper's abstract. The study found that the behaviour of looking for travel partners in China is mainly among young people. I wonder whether young people in Taiwan go online to look for companions when they travel? At least in my experience with vocational university students, they mostly travel with people they already know, and I rarely see young people with this tendency.</p><p>The second finding of the study is that all four traits of a travel partner can positively affect "memorable travel experiences" through the mediating factor of "travel partner exchange" (Note 1), while gender composition plays a moderating role (Note 2). So what is the difference between mediation and moderation? Let's use this travel-partner study as an example.<br/><b>Mediation</b>: explains "why" or "how" the effect arises. (For example: partner traits → partner exchange → memorable travel experience).<br/><b>Moderation</b>: explains that the relationship itself stays the same, but its "strength" or "direction" depends on the circumstances (For example: partner traits → memorable travel experience, with the moderating variable being the gender composition of the partners). </p><p>In short, the choice of travel partner is influenced by partners' individual traits, so when we look for travel companions, we can systematically think about the four personal traits (emotional intelligence, travel experience, equal footing, conscientiousness) when considering candidates for exchanging or choosing partners: a person with these traits should be a good travel partner when going out with you, and can bring unforgettable travel memories.</p><p>If we match the four traits of a travel partner with MBTI (the 16 personalities), which personality types naturally have these "dream teammate" genes (the analysis below was completed with the help of AI, so please treat it as reference only)?</p><p>Below is a ranking from best fit to relatively fitting, based on how well the traits match:<br/>1. Conscientiousness: ISTJ (Logistician) / INTJ (Architect)</p><p>If we want to find the ideal personality for the best travel partner, ENFJ is generally considered the top pick for travel, because they combine organisational ability (J), emotional intelligence (F) and approachability. Mediation analysis</b> is a statistical method that aims to explain the internal mechanism by which an independent variable (X) affects a dependent variable (Y) through a mediating variable (M). It reveals that besides the "direct effect" (X→Y), there is also an "indirect effect" (X→M→Y). The stepwise regression approach of Baron &amp; Kenny or the Bootstrap method can be used to test the significance of the indirect effect.</p><p><b>Note 2. Moderating effect (Moderation)</b> is used to investigate whether the influence of an independent variable (X) on a dependent variable (Y) is affected by the strength or direction of another "moderating variable (W)". A moderator increases or decreases the influence of X on Y. It is often tested by moderated regression analysis, in which the independent variable and the moderator must be centred.</p><p>Source: Fang, X., Yu, J., Zhang, K., &amp; Huang, S. (2025).
