【The July University Entrance Exam Has Never Really Gone Away】

The university entrance exam is back in 2023? Half of this year's graduating high school students are still preparing for the subject tests!?

It is the Dragon Boat Festival, with the scent of zongzi in the air, and the summer solstice of 2023 in Taiwan. The hard-working senior students of academic year 112 are the second cohort under the 108 curriculum guidelines, and half of the boys are the unlucky ones whose military service went from four months back to one year. While the media and top schools are already celebrating how many students got into the medical school of University X, most candidates are still at school, in the library or at cram school during this long holiday break, all because they are still preparing for the subject tests on July 12-13.

With a falling birth rate and no reduction in university places, getting into university is no longer what matters; getting into a top university is what students and parents care about. Of this year's 118,000 high school candidates, how many will sit this pseudo university entrance exam on July 12-13? My own estimate is around 50,000. In the sweltering summer heat, there are still diligent students forced by a system we adults designed to go sweating into the exam hall, to sit again the subjects they already prepared for half a year ago. It really is strange. Why not simply use the General Scholastic Ability Test (GSAT) results to apply and be placed? What is the point of testing again?

The multiple-channel admission system was meant to steadily reduce the share of exam-based placement (like the old joint entrance exam). As the table shows, the design intention was for only a quarter of students to use this route, while gradually raising the share of the Star Plan and application admission. But amid a market where the birth rate is falling and university places have not been cut, the number of students giving up the earlier Star Plan and application routes hit a new high in year 112 (2023). Judging from the likely final admission proportions this year, it may even exceed 50%.

After years of education reform, the university entrance exam still lingers like a corpse brought back to life, split into two, with a third inside the two. Add the learning portfolios and diverse learning designs of high school, and young Taiwanese students, already forced to accept the system's arrangements during secondary school despite the falling birth rate, must still sit both the GSAT and the subject tests. Let us look at how Taiwan's university entrance exam has evolved. It was called the Joint College Entrance Exam (years 43-60), then the University and Independent College Entrance Examination (the "joint entrance exam", years 61-90), then the Advanced Subjects Test (years 91-110), and then the GSAT (from year 111) plus the Advanced Subjects Test's successor, the Subject Tests. From academic year 111 the "Advanced Subjects Test" became the "Subject Tests", cutting from 10 subjects to 7: Chinese, English and Math B are no longer tested separately and instead draw on the GSAT scores. The subjects available in the Subject Tests are Math A, History, Geography, Civics and Society, Physics, Chemistry and Biology. Looking at the subjects, in fact nothing has really changed. The old form is still there, and to match social expectations it has been patched here and there until it is even more complicated.

Especially in 2023, many candidates and parents, having seen last year's many cases of "low score, high placement" in the subject tests, had students whose GSAT scores fell short of expectations skip individual application admission and go all in on the subject tests. Reports also note that at the leading schools in southern Taiwan, more than half of students are registered for the subject tests, and the students I know at leading schools in northern Taiwan are likewise still wrestling with the exam subjects. In this Dragon Boat Festival season, I wish them well: may they "zong" (a pun on the zongzi and "hit the mark") into their ideal departments in this steamer-like heat.
