2023 EVA Air City Sightseeing Marathon

I have no impressive finish time or PB to write about, because I was picked up by the sweeper bus at kilometre 38! But there were lots of happy and funny things along the way after the start, and I am writing them down so I don't forget them all by the same time next year.

#IDidntKnowIdNeedToPeeThisMuch

I'm not usually someone who dribbles, and I don't know whether it was my body's reaction to prolonged exercise or a sudden ailment, but in the nearly 40 km I went to the toilet at least five times, about once every 7-8 km. So next time I must plan my toilet timing, at least so I don't start 8 minutes late because of queuing, since the portable toilets were so far from the start line. Several times I wanted to just go in the bushes. If I'd known, I would have started first and gone at Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall instead.

#MarathonRunnersHaveGreatBodiesAndGreatShoes

Since this was a proper marathon, everyone came prepared with fitted professional gear and shoes. Whenever I overtook someone, I could see pair after pair of famous and ridiculously expensive running shoes, and fit, toned bodies. If you're there to take part, this is a nice perk.

I have been running 3K a day for almost 5 years and have only changed shoes 3-5 times, all of them discounted Mizuno. After this, I want to try the many cool Asics, Nike and HOKA shoes on the course. Would my times improve?

#ManyCouplesRunTogether

Basically, I strongly advise couples not to run a marathon together, unless you met through a running club or have trained together for years. Today I saw many who were abandoned halfway, watching their partners stride off handsomely and leave behind a line like: "I'll wait for you at the finish..."

Actually, I think you will not be meeting that person there!

#TheSweeperBusDoesntDriveBehindTheLastRunner

I always thought the sweeper bus drives at the very back and people who can't run any more climb aboard. In fact, in a professional marathon, referees at many checkpoints are counting time. After I exchanged encouragement with two young women who had just overtaken me, we watched a few runners ahead pass the checkpoint, and then the referee blew a whistle to show we had passed the cut-off time, and our chips were cut off by force. After that we lined up to board the sweeper bus parked at the side. Since the pick-up point was at kilometre 38, we actually still had time, so the two young women kept begging the referee to let us continue. I thought they were brave, because an old guy like me would feel embarrassed.

#YouFeelALittleFeverishAfterALongRun

This part I did not expect. My legs were so sore I could hardly walk, and on the way home, experiencing Taipei's pedestrian space, I realised how much people with limited mobility need flat, wide sidewalks. The 50 seconds at the Zhongshan North Road traffic light is simply too short to cross.

These are my thoughts from my first full marathon. Although it is a pity I was just short of finishing, I still found the process fun and happy because I experienced so many things that are hard to feel through imagination. As Professor Chung-Hsien Lee wrote in his book Hidden Rebels: everyone should find something they are not very good at and work hard at it with focus, and only then will you know that life is not what you imagined.

I would also suggest that EVA Air remove the words [City Sightseeing] next year, because the route kept repeating up and down the Neihu-Nangang elevated roadway and the Keelung River bike path. You can't see the beautiful city, let alone sightsee.
