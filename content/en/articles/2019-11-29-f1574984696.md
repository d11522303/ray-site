---
title: "What Future Species Might Write About the Anthropocene"
excerpt: "An imagined entry from a future history: the Anthropocene, a self-interested age of Earth."
published: true
---

When future species record the Anthropocene, they might write:

Human development started from the self-interest of a primate organism. Humans could not see any greater possibility beyond themselves and considered only the greatest benefit reflected back from their own selves. This is proven by the fact that most surviving photos and images from the Anthropocene are front-facing solo portraits or selfies. Human development was largely driven by wherever the eyeballs were focused.

In the late Anthropocene, the many organisations that humans had built through reason ran into conflicts over disease, race, religion and even commercial interests. Even when most people or groups in a rational society reasonably pointed out the direction things should take, human society still proceeded according to the greatest benefit of individuals or a powerful few. Events whose conflict could reasonably have been reduced turned into humans attacking one another. The human species then abandoned the rational behaviour it had developed over many years and turned to emotion and primal needs, which ultimately led to the end of the entire Anthropocene.

Anthropocene: the self-interested era of the Earth, lasting about 5,000 to 6,000 Earth years.
