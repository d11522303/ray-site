Bangkok Trip Day 6: Kanchanaburi's Death Railway, Zoo and the Bridge on the River Kwai, a Dream Come True…

During the pandemic I made a list of several travel destinations I looked forward to in life, and today's Bridge on the River Kwai and Death Railway is one of them. When the car reached the broad River Kwai, I truly couldn't imagine how they managed to build a bridge back then! The river that the Bridge on the River Kwai spans in the film isn't as grand as what we see today. I think the foreign friends present must have felt the same about the gap between the film and the real place!

In fact, when we first arrived in Bangkok, the driver who took us to the hotel asked where we wanted to go. When we mentioned the River Kwai, he said the expressway between Bangkok and Kanchanaburi has been built, so the drive can be kept within two hours and is more comfortable than before. Otherwise a day trip to the Bridge on the River Kwai and the Death Railway would be quite a gruelling journey.

This tour was booked in Taiwan, a Bangkok holiday group tour, where travel companions from different places share a nine-seater minivan. Today's companions were all quiet travellers, and the guide didn't chat constantly, and also took lots of lovely photos of our family. The two groups of young people in the same van were punctual and quiet, and listened to the commentary attentively and seriously. I really felt that having good travel companions is wonderful. It was I who often drifted off and then asked more questions, becoming the "nagging adult" in my children's words.

The Death Railway trip is full of local flavour. At the countryside station the number of mosquitoes was astonishing, so we walked around between the tracks as much as we could to avoid being attacked by unidentified insects, and the train was, as usual, late. Things only got better when the long, slow train rolled in.

On the full carriage we met a group of Chinese Department students from Suan Sunandha Rajabhat University in Bangkok, who chatted with us about their motivation for learning Chinese, a bit of citizen diplomacy. They also recommended the snacks sold by vendors on the train, and after getting off they shared the best viewing spot with our family, so that we could record, from the best angle, this destination in my life's travel list. I was extremely satisfied.
