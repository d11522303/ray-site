30 June 2025

June is a special month for teachers. The homeroom class I led from freshman year has collected its diplomas one by one, a teacher I had worked with on projects for a long time suddenly announced a successful move to another school, and the proposals I spent time writing at the start of the year were all announced today as unsuccessful. A whole academic year of lectures delivered until my voice gave out, exam-licence coaching and club activities; internship matching that felt like a battle; competition training I was pushed into without choice, and so on, all the endless work, came to a full stop in an instant.

Inside and outside the classrooms the students have dispersed, and only the cicadas keep chirping across the campus. It is like finishing a wonderful film: however beautiful the ending, the curtain must fall.

From July (that is, tomorrow), interns will begin reporting to their host organisations one after another, which is both exciting and nerve-racking. The homeroom class for the incoming freshmen is also gradually taking shape, another new academic year full of hope.

What doesn't change is that I am still on the track. The Apple Watch I have worn for four years has accompanied me through 4,040 kilometres of running and walking, and the Taipei summer night seen from my balcony at home is as bright and beautiful as ever.
