-----Updated 2023.11.26-----

Statistics is not easy to learn, and once learned, you don't know where to use it.

After finishing graduate school, I discovered that the statistical methods in academic papers come in all varieties, and are difficult and unfathomable. Statistics is a specialty, and for many people it is a hurdle. These past few days, everyone has been stuck between 3% and 6%, each with their own explanation, which is truly helpless.

At this moment, when everyone is so fond of statistics, I want to use this post to look for an original textbook:

Business Statistics: For Contemporary Decision Making, 8th Edition, published by Wiley,

Author: Ken Black

I have searched many libraries in Taiwan and none have this book. It has now reached its 11th edition. I would very much like to see the printed pages listed, and I don't know where to find a copy.

The attached pages are the cruise study of city image across port cities in the three regions on both sides of the Taiwan Strait, which Teacher Lu and I co-published in 2011. It was included by statistics master Dr. Ken Black as a business case in his enduring masterpiece, "Business Statistics."

Students and graduate students still interested in statistics can read carefully the proof pages that the Wiley editor sent us back then. Dr. Ken Black used our cruise survey case to illustrate the use of the chi-square test in business decisions, and urged students to read and think deeply about the reasons behind the statistics, and gave researchers suggestions on comparing the statistical methods he mentions with their purposes.

I remember then the many people on board the SuperStar Virgo of Star Cruises who worked hard to collect hundreds of tourist surveys. Our study used only the most basic chi-square test to verify hypotheses. Using the statistical methods I had not yet given back to my teachers, I thought about the fundamental questions and research goals for our target cruise passengers, and used a suitable research method to test whether it fit the pattern of facts we originally wanted to understand.

I can look myself in the eye, and my heart is at ease.

Below is the full text of the AI translation into Chinese:

#CriticalThinkingInTodaysBusinessStatistics

#CityImageOfTaiwanStraitCruiseDestinations

Researchers Chiang-Chuan Lu (Alumi Lu) and Ruey-Feng Chen conducted a study with the aim, in part, of finding out whether cruise passengers perceive differences in the city image of three port cities across the Taiwan Strait: Hong Kong, Shanghai and Taipei. The researchers administered questionnaires to cruise passengers over four years to collect data. Approximately half of the respondents were male and half female, and over 50% had a university education. Slightly more than half of the respondents were from one of the three ports in the study, and 72% were from Malaysia, Singapore, China or Taiwan.

One of the research hypotheses was whether travellers living in the three cities (Hong Kong, Shanghai, Taipei) differ from travellers living elsewhere in whether they perceive differences in the image of the three cities. To test this hypothesis, the researchers conducted a chi-square test of independence. Below is the 2×2 contingency table and raw data used for this test:

Difference perceived / No difference perceived

Residents of the three port cities 58 5

Residents of other places 37 25

The null hypothesis of this test is that views on whether the three port cities differ in image are independent of the respondent's place of residence. The chi-square value calculated from the data is 17.97. At a significance level of α = 0.05 and degrees of freedom df = 1, the critical value is 3.8415. Since 17.97 is greater than 3.8415, the decision is to reject the null hypothesis. That is, the perception of whether the three port cities differ is not independent of where the traveller lives.

In fact, over 90% of travellers living in the three port cities believed the three cities differ, whereas among travellers living elsewhere, only slightly under 60% believed there were differences.

#ThingsToPonder

Why do you think such a high proportion of residents of the three port cities believe the three cities are different, while the proportion among out-of-town travellers is clearly lower?

How could this question be analysed using the methods of Section 10.4 of this book? Compare the results of using that section's techniques with the chi-square test of independence in this example, noting similarities and differences.

Source: Chiang-Chuan Lu and Ruey-Feng Chen, "Differences in Tourism Image Among Cruise Passengers across the Taiwan Straits," Global Journal of Business Research, Vol. 5 (2011), 95–108.
