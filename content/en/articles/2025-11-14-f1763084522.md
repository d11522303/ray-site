[How to Choose a Corporate Internship Placement]

Recently many students have been privately asking me about choosing an internship position. I often ask back, how will you choose? By salary? By brand? By the job content?

This is not something I can decide for students, because to decide on a job you must examine your own abilities and find a suitable job from your own perspective and the company's future development. And that perspective and future are accumulated step by step from the day you enrol.

I often look at a student and think about their learning, clubs, activities and all kinds of conduct over these three years, and in the end my conclusion is still to ask them to decide for themselves. This is not passing the buck. At this very moment, the decision is in your own hands.

How to decide? Let me explain by stage across the four years of university: freshman, sophomore, junior and senior.

#Freshman

From the classroom rules in the first year of university, such as no eating in professional classrooms and taking notes, to the voluntary activities arranged by the department's student association, to teachers repeatedly reminding you of the importance of licence exams, and flooding LINE with opportunities for after-class licence and language coaching, all of this is in the hope that first-year students move from being passive participants in high school to evaluating for themselves what to take part in.

Think about why the school and the teachers arrange such activities.

#Sophomore

From the second year, the department's teaching and guiding work begins to change angle and run in parallel. The department's teaching and coaching routines repeat, but sophomores are also expected to mentor the new freshmen, so teachers encourage second-years to join many club officer roles and competitions, to build a sense of responsibility and competitiveness, while constantly reminding them of the abilities and licences internship companies require.

In fact, we teachers often see you standing in front of the licence development wall, looking and discussing, and even helping freshmen understand concrete career development. We see the after-class coaching classes, and even the evening extra sessions, packed with practising students. The mixed feelings that stirs in us are hard to describe.

Also, do you know the English word for a second-year student is "sophomore"? Do you know what it means? The word derives from ancient Greek, combining the roots for "wisdom" (sophos) and "foolish" (moros). Dear students, second year is the watershed of your life. A future of "wisdom" or "folly" is yours to decide.

#Junior

At this point, third-year students in the HWU Department of Travel Management are already getting anxious, because internship vacancies from tourism and travel companies, as in the attached pictures, have been arriving one after another in the school mailbox since the start of term. But most students have just finished a long, hot summer break, either having played until their minds have drifted or having worked until exhausted and just wanting to lie flat.

Yet corporate internship recruitment waits for no one. The postings clearly state language and professional licence requirements, attendance hours, conduct grades and other things that should have been completed in the first two years. Even scarier, the preparation time is mostly only one week to half a month to complete your résumé.

Think about it: at this moment, are you a junior-level newcomer to society?

That is why teachers now offer micro-credit courses on résumé preparation and etiquette, and hope students will write their résumés together while teachers revise them one by one. You often see teachers and students queuing in groups to discuss, résumés written and rewritten, typos redone.

In truth, this is deliberately wearing you down. It is a process of developing the patience and perseverance to adapt to the long-term work, training and service you will face in the workplace.

Honestly, a teacher about to send disciples into a contest is more nervous and more invested than the students. How many times, when a beloved student has received an offer for a dream internship, has the first person to shed tears been the teacher who got the offer letter!

#Senior

For fourth-year interns, let's talk about something more mature. We all know the future is hard to predict. If your current internship gives you a strong sense of achievement, congratulations first. If you feel stuck in the workplace, I also ask you to truly appreciate what society demands, learn in good time how to communicate with and consult supervisors and colleagues, and find the way of surviving that suits you best.

The internship placements of HWU's Department of Travel Management in recent years are almost identical to the list of listed companies in the attached picture. Taiwan's universities of science and technology arrange a full-year internship in the fourth year mainly to put into practice, and test, what students learned and grew through in their three years at school, and also to offer vocational students a rare corporate viewpoint and life perspective.

The listing requirements for tourism companies in Taiwan are high. Generally, a company must have been established for at least three years, have paid-in capital of at least NT$600 million, and have stable profitability. It must also have at least 1,000 registered shareholders, of whom no fewer than 500 are outside insiders, together holding 20% or at least 10 million shares. If you get the chance to join a large company, you can experience the importance of the ambition and revenue the company keeps talking about, and feel the corporate leader's sense of social responsibility and direction for development.

As an internship supervisor, through my long-term interaction with companies, I better understand that efforts to close the "gap between learning and practice" run both ways, and I have a deep appreciation of corporate training, vocational teaching and student development. This kind of reward is hard to describe and hard to quantify by salary or promotion.

If you have read this far, students, give yourself a round of applause. It means you have the patience to read a long piece and think carefully about your future. Although we are students of a private university of science and technology, as long as you follow the department's plan, your teachers will do everything they can to embroider on your uniform the epaulettes you can be proud of.
