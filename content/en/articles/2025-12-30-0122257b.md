title: "The Economy of One by Kenichi Ohmae"

published: true
---

<p>A few days ago a student who once took my course sent me a private message, saying that in choosing a future internship she faced a choice between two options: a Taiwanese travel start-up, or a service-industry internship in Singapore. If it were you, how would you choose?</p><p>Somewhere along the way, opportunities to choose a job have become rare around me. In reality I still hope for change and development, but time has already run ahead. The AI ads Facebook gives me are already about groups for grown-ups, retirement planning and so on. The student's question reminded me that it is now my turn to give advice, yet in truth I am the one who most needs direction.</p><p>【Go and strive where effort is surely rewarded】This is the advice Kenichi Ohmae gives to young people aged 20-35 in his book "The Economy of One". He writes that the environment all over the world is changing. For young Japanese, he does not recommend staying at home to look for work. He believes that going out to strive in a place where effort is surely rewarded is how to create 50% growth, that going south to ASEAN is better than going west to China, and that if you know English, everything is easier to learn. Looking back at my own past years from 20 to 35, it seems I did not manage a single one of these things. These ideas are easy to read, but really hard to do.</p><p>So how would a young Taiwanese person growing up in a comfort zone, with little environmental pressure, actively choose such options at work? If they truly have the courage to choose, then overseas, it is the need to survive that stirs a person's survival instinct. Competing with international talent abroad, and experiencing how people of different nationalities, whatever their origins, fight to get ahead in a capitalist society, they gain more opportunities and income through effort. Only then does choosing a place to 【strive where effort is surely rewarded】 have meaning.</p>
