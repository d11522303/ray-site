Have you seen the series Black Mirror? If so, then that is the strange feeling of our family's flash 18 hours in Ningbo.

A typhoon turned our 10-day trip into 12 days, and the transit city changed from Fuzhou in Fujian to Ningbo in Zhejiang. Ningbo is a city we can't quite put into words. Perhaps we were tired from the journey, or perhaps the city's persona really can't find a place for itself, but it felt like a city without a soul.

When we arrived, the taxi drivers queuing at the airport were reluctant to take fares, and not keen when they saw lots of luggage. If we hadn't been truly tired, with no other cars behind us and a crowd of customers queuing behind, I wouldn't have wanted to take that taxi. Sitting inside was even stranger (see the picture). The driver didn't answer questions and just drove to the hotel and dropped us off. In the end, the fare on the meter was 42 yuan, and the driver quickly pressed a button and called out 43.

2024.7.30 Correction (it turned out there was a 1-yuan fee for printing the receipt. I only found this out while sorting my records, so I misjudged him. I apologise for the misunderstanding. The 1 yuan was mine to pay, though it would have been better if the driver had said so.)

Checking into the hotel was also strange. Let me first say that the facilities and star rating were actually good, the handover when we left early in the morning was thorough, and I have no negative reviews. I even have to admit being satisfied.

But the overall experience was just like the hotel's name, "Ningbo Overseas Chinese Wyndham Grand Plaza Royal Hotel": everything excessive. At every stage of service, the staff seemed to be acting. The front desk, the male butler, the female butler, the cleaning auntie, the auntie delivering snacks and the girl delivering pillows all loved to interact. They were enthusiastic and chatty, totally ignoring our family's tired faces, chatting about this and that, and in the end asking us to go online, write their names and give them a personal good review on a digital platform.

It gave me a feeling of doom, of being caught in a spiral of digital ratings.

And I feel sad for them, as fellow hospitality people.
