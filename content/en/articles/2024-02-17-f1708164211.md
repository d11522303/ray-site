The Munich Security Conference

I've just watched DW's interview with German Chancellor Scholz, followed by a panel with Ukrainian President Zelensky. This is the live broadcast of the annual Munich Security Conference (MSC). After the Second World War, the MSC began as a think-tank style conference for exchanges on international strategy and security, run by a private foundation outside any state system. It is not part of the UN system but is very influential, and since 1963 it has reached its sixtieth meeting.

The conference presents the Munich Security Report along with panels and interviews with key figures. Today's focus was Europe; yesterday the Chinese and American representatives had already discussed Taiwan, but our domestic media only cared about two brainless influencers who embarrassed Taiwan abroad, and about the unscrupulous travel agency that stranded its customers, with no follow-up.

Here is a short excerpt from the Munich Security Report; the full download is in the comments:

------The following concerns Taiwan------

China's revisionist behaviour has unsettled the United States and other countries in the Indo-Pacific. Of the worries over escalating sovereignty claims in the South China Sea, Taiwan is the most pressing. I think that apart from Taiwanese ourselves, countries around the world are very worried about us: citizens of every country surveyed in the Munich Security Index are more worried about the risk a Chinese invasion of Taiwan would bring to the world (Figure 3.1).

Meanwhile, a regional arms race has begun. China is rapidly strengthening its conventional and nuclear capabilities, and of course other Indo-Pacific countries have raised their defence budgets too (Figure 3.2). South Korea's defence budget is almost as large as Japan's. I wonder whether Koreans also worry that war on the peninsula could break out at any moment?
