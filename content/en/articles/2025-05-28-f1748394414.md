---
title: "Saying No to Unreasonable Authority"
excerpt: "A father's reflection on his son walking out of a physics exam after a professor accused him of cheating, and on being proud of how he stood up to unreasonable authority."
published: true
---

2025.5.28

Early this morning a family member mentioned that our child had just phoned to tell us that something happened at yesterday's General Physics exam.

In an exam hall of over a hundred people, for some reason the physics professor singled out our child. Perhaps it was because last week he had asked the professor, face to face, for extra points over a marking error on his midterm paper. The professor's face showed impatience then, and he even blamed the teaching assistant and told him to apply to the assistant in writing for the extra points, which he later did, but that is another matter. Perhaps because of that, this week during the final exam the professor came and stood beside him to watch him take the test, and then said that opening his iPad was cheating and demanded that he take his bag to the front. But in fact, in last week's class the professor had said that today's in-class review quiz could be open book, yet the professor refused to acknowledge it on the spot.

Imagine a young student in a large lecture hall of a hundred, under the pressure of the professor and with the whole class watching. He must have been extremely embarrassed.

So how did he handle it?

Sitting at his seat, he thought for a moment and decided to walk up to the professor with his exam paper. He said: "Thank you, Professor. Since you say so, I have decided not to take this exam." Then he left the classroom.

I think he is very cool. Although I am still worried that the professor will fail him, which would make the scores from his previous three midterm exams all wasted, I admire him even more for knowing how to say no in front of unreasonable authority, under enormous pressure and grade expectations.

This is something people of my generation, who are deeply servile, could never do. I could only tell him that, whatever happens, Mum and Dad are on your side. What I didn't say is how truly grateful I am that he told us what happened. We feel very much for him, yet we can also sense that he is exploring how to interact in a complex society and organisations, and this time we feel he handled it more maturely than we would have.
