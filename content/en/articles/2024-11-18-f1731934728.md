#Autumn Rain and Strong Typhoons, Week After Week

This was a field trip that had been postponed for a long time. I had planned before the semester even began to take students on a train ride. The seniors who joined the last train activity with the same banner have already graduated or gone on internships, so I always want to give students more shared travel memories with me. The typhoons arriving one after another were extremely annoying. The last time I disliked typhoons lining up at my door this much was the summer of 2008. For anyone working in a travel-related industry, typhoons are never welcome.

#Thoughtful Guiding Plans at Every Turn

What you fear most when organising an outdoor visit is rain. Early in the morning, before we had even gathered, Village Chief Chih-Chiang reported the local weather: "It's raining." I hurriedly notified the students at 7 o'clock to bring rain gear, but in order to reach the meeting point on time, many had already left home and were on their way. At 9 o'clock, as we were about to board the train, he reported again that "the rain is getting heavier," and my heart sank by half. When we stepped into Ruifang Station, what greeted us was a soggy platform.

As soon as he arrived, Chief Chih-Chiang gathered the students and took out a box of lightweight ponchos, one clear, colourless poncho for each person, so that the nicely dressed students would not look unattractive in raincoats. Along the way, he spoke about Ruifang's local architecture, culture and history, the light railway and its former glory in witty language, which kept the students very attentive, and they took out their phones to record everything we passed. During the walk, Chief Chih-Chiang was constantly concerned about the group's safety, finding suitable stops and interpretation points, and using professional audio equipment so that everyone could hear clearly and learn in detail. In the drizzle, my own heart settled at the sight of the students' focused eyes. Over a route and tour of nearly 90 minutes, the students all wrote about their rich gains in their learning feedback.

#Mobile Learning, Guided at Every Moment

This time I asked Chief Chih-Chiang, my graduate school classmate, to serve as guide and teacher, precisely so that his rich local connections and guiding experience could be lent to my own students. From planning to execution, and throughout the guiding activity, he thought of everything for us, and you could feel how different it is when an expert steps in. This is a micro-credit course in guiding and interpretation. Only through practical action can students realise that guiding is not just exam questions in a textbook, but a clever, integrated profession combining the interpreter and local resources. Of course, a group of students with a desire to learn, curiosity and consideration was also an important factor in the success of this course activity.
