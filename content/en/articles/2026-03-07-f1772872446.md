#TourLeaderExamCountdown7Days

38 When travellers bring precious metals into Taiwan, which of the following does NOT need to be declared to Customs?

(A) platinum worth NT$510,000

(B) diamonds worth NT$510,000

(C) pearls worth NT$510,000

(D) gold worth NT$510,000

The answer is (D): gold worth NT$510,000 does not need to be declared. Why? Gold has its own declaration threshold, US$20,000 (about NT$640,000 to 660,000). The other three should be declared as items suspected of being used for money laundering under the "Regulations Governing Declaration and Reporting of Articles Carried In and Out of the Country for Anti-Money Laundering", namely diamonds, gemstones and platinum beyond personal use.

Q1. Regarding the rules on duty-free tobacco and alcohol that travellers bring in, which statement is correct?

(A) Travellers aged 18 or over may bring in 1 litre of alcohol and 200 cigarettes duty-free.

(B) Travellers aged 18 or over may bring in 1.5 litres of alcohol duty-free; only travellers aged 20 or over may bring in 200 cigarettes duty-free.

(C) Regardless of age, every traveller enjoys a duty-free allowance of 1.5 litres of alcohol and 1 carton of cigarettes.

(D) The duty-free allowance for alcohol is 1 litre, but anything over 1 litre and within 5 litres needs no declaration as long as the traveller is 18 or over.

Answer: B

Explanation: Under the latest rules in effect from January 2025, the duty-free allowance for alcohol has been raised from 1 litre to 1.5 litres, with a minimum age of 18; the minimum age for carrying tobacco products (cigarettes, cut tobacco, cigars) remains 20.
