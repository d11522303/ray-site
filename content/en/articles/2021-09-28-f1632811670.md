[Funny Moments from My Days as a Class Helper]

When I attended Houpu Elementary School in Banqiao, it was the most crowded primary school in the world. Across its six grades there were 195 classes and more than 11,500 pupils. The flag-raising ceremony rotated by grade, and the lower and middle grades could only attend half-day classes because there weren't enough classrooms. The small, sandy playground couldn't hold the whole school, so for flag raising each class stood in the corridor in front of its own classroom and sang the national anthem. At the end of the day, the walking groups heading home were like long human dragons, and it took a long time to get the whole school dispersed.

I still remember one year when the news anchor Chou Chuan from CTV came to interview students. The school picked a few pupils who wouldn't say anything out of line. I remember telling the camera and "Auntie Chou Chuan": "Lots of us need the toilet during break, and everybody has to queue to pee. If we don't make it in time, we have to go back to the classroom." That clip was later broadcast on CTV's seven o'clock evening news. It was my media debut.

In fact, in fifth and sixth grade I was the science class helper. Every morning during self-study I had to read out the textbook text or test questions for classmates to write down or be tested on. One morning I needed the toilet badly but had to read out the questions, and I didn't dare go. In the end I really couldn't hold it, and I peed right there on the platform. My trousers were soaked and there was a puddle on the floor.

Afterwards I even fetched a bucket of water to the classroom and pretended to sprinkle the floor. I can't remember why classrooms back then were often sprinkled with water. Was it the heat? Was it all the dust? I smugly pretended to dilute my own pee...

Forty years have passed, and as far as I'm concerned nothing has been diluted at all. If anything, I remember it even more clearly.
