Interns in the Travel Industry

The attached photos show the students who are now in their eighth month of internship at travel agencies, along with the coaching process for a travel-industry operational error.

During academic year 112, twelve HWU Travel Management students interned at three well-known Taiwanese full-service travel agencies: Cola Tour, Wufu Travel and Lion Travel. According to each student's personality and interests, they worked as sales, administrative and product OP assistants on the cruise, domestic travel, corporate travel, Japan and island lines.

Since last July, the students in the photos look steadier and more mature, and behind the photos, as the students and I look back on this process, our feelings are mixed. These eight months have not been a calm and uneventful journey. All the students have gone through new-hire training, sales promotion, performance appraisals, staff turnover, job coverage and project assignments. They have experienced fairness and unfairness in the workplace, and appropriate and inappropriate interactions between people, and of course they have not been spared mistakes of all kinds, customer demands, service interactions and risks large and small.

The LINE example attached is a small operational mistake a student made before the New Year, which really happened. For students new to the industry, the coaching and support from their travel agency supervisors, and the process of mutual trust and interaction between school and organisation, are the key to whether the interns can transform over this year.

It was also during the coaching that I realised my own understanding of travel liability insurance was not up to date. Did you spot the error in the message? And seeing today's news about the Japan tour in Nara reminded me of this. I am happy that the students already know the current regulations better than I do, and I think when the students saw the news they must have felt the same as I did: this tour insured its customers for too little. Although it did not break the rules, by industry standards it is hard to justify.

An internship system is built through the joint efforts of three parties. There are the department's teachers who patiently and carefully cooperate in coaching; the supervisors and managers at Cola Tour, Wufu Travel and Lion Travel who provide internship opportunities; and the lovable and admirable students who are willing to take the elective challenge and face growth.

Today, from the confident faces of the interns, and from the whispers I overheard of them discussing whether to stay on, I can hear your professionalism and personal growth. To all you veteran interns, I ask loudly: the next batch of rookies is about to arrive, so please look after them!
