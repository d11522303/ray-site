2026.1.6 A Good Article Worth Sharing

@[100001963761488:2048:吳學銘] The president's precise writing and incisive views. If students today had to read editorials and write reflections on them in their weekly journals, as I did when I was a student, I would ask students in tourism-related departments to read this article closely.

But I did not forward this article directly to students, because the generation used to short videos will only see so many words as garbage text, and the person who forwards it is immediately classified as one of those people who lecture endlessly and spam good-morning pictures 🤣

I reflected on my recent way of teaching, and my first thoughts on possible approaches are as follows:

#1 Turn the article into a worksheet, print it on paper, and guide students to mark the key points with highlighters (this needs a demonstration, using different colours to separate "#facts" from "#opinions").

#2 Turn this article into a presentation of about 10 slides, comparing Taiwan and Japan on every slide, with the teacher explaining the problem points in detail at the same time. It needs at least #4 class periods.

#3 In class, slowly lead in through other prior knowledge, running the course from perspectives such as #overtourism, #destinationgovernance, #tourismmanagementpolicy, and comparisons between #regionalrevitalization and mass tourism.

#4 In the worksheet, ask students to turn the "opinions" in the article and the teacher's slides into their own individual "reflection text (#about50words)", handwritten on the worksheet. Students need time for this: one slide, one stretch of writing time. The teacher walks among the desks to see how the writing is going (#itdoesntmatterwhetheritiswrittenwellornot, #producingtextmeansthinking), while reminding those on their phones to come back to class and waking those who are too tired and asleep.

#5 Then, using one class period, remove personal information from the reflection texts, turn them into images and slides, and publicly explain the students' reflections, giving positive feedback and suggestions, so students can see their own words and understand how the teacher interprets and supports them.

#6 Finally, watch for the students with "#lightintheireyes", quietly remember them, and find opportunities to give them words of encouragement and support. In any crowd some run fast and some walk slowly. Vocational students have three years at school, and for those who warm up slowly, we can wait.
