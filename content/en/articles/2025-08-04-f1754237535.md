Bangkok Trip Day 7: The Scare at the Last Minute That I Missed

The last day was all about taking the BTS and strolling nearby. Even at dinner everyone was happy as usual, looking back on the week's travels and getting ready to use up the Thai baht we had left before heading home.

Just as I had left my family and gone for a 300-baht Thai massage, my family and child, because of missing AirPods, created the climax of this trip. The earbuds were not only dropped but also taken over by a Shack Shake employee who changed the account. My family and child hurried, half walking and half running, to the counter just before the department store closed, and sternly asked where the earbuds were. I think because my family was there and the staff seemed to decide to return them, the AirPods were eventually recovered, but when we reconnected them we found they had been changed to Thai-language content, plus the name Sandy.

My child also came to realise that however careful you are along the way, accidents still happen. When travelling you always need the idea of tolerating mistakes and risks, keeping a little money aside, or splitting items among people, which are all important. This is also the main reason that in recent years, thinking too much, I've increasingly not wanted to travel. I feel I no longer have much capacity or energy to bear things.

We go home tomorrow. I've experienced that wherever you are, risk is always by your side. All we can do is do more good deeds and give more, so that peace stays with our family forever.

***************************************

I'll also record a scare I did take part in: in the huge city of Bangkok, my family and I unexpectedly ran into Brother Kong. The place was the very common Terminal 21 food court. Anyone who has been there knows that in that food court even family members travelling together may get lost and lose contact. And in fact we had already eaten our fill at the boat noodle restaurant and just wanted to order a dessert of shaved ice to cool off, when we met Brother Kong, who was also travelling in Thailand to visit relatives, and his cousin.

The fate between people is truly strange. Later, on the way back, my family also remarked that among people of every nationality it already feels surreal, yet among millions of tourists we ran into a colleague from an office of only five people. Perhaps in a previous life we had already met on a journey. We are all time travellers who love travelling and cherish freedom.
