published: true
---

After 50, we should slowly cultivate good habits that don't require finding someone to chat with or constantly bothering others to stay in touch, and that let us enjoy ourselves. I have already started!

The first habit: talking to myself.

Thanks to the training of online teaching during the pandemic, as long as I have organised my lesson plan and read a bit more material, I can talk to myself for two class periods, and happily tell stories and jokes to myself. In fact, classrooms after the pandemic are much the same, which is quite funny.

The second habit: reading free books from the library.

Many of the stories in books were ones I never had a chance to read when I was busy with work. I read until my eyes ache and my hands are sore from holding the book.

The third habit: running three kilometres every day.

A few days ago, a professor who loved marathons logged out suddenly during a race. I hope that one day I will go like that too, not lying at home, not letting my family see me in a sorry state. And because I run often, I will always look energetic and won't fall ill.

Until I log out.

The fourth habit: learning a new language.

I have started learning Indonesian, because I have noticed that many elderly people are being pushed along in wheelchairs by Indonesian caregivers, who chat online all the time while the old folks understand nothing and grow more and more confused. I must learn quickly so that I can eavesdrop on what they are talking about.

The fifth habit: a handwritten diary.

If every day is the last day, the diary is my will. This is the spirit in which I record each day.

Habits I hope to form in the future

Building a website, learning plumbing and electrical work, writing travel journals
